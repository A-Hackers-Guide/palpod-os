## 0. Purpose and Instantiation Rules

This document is the working template — not the finished deliverable — for every quarterly Hearth board meeting and for the three standing committee meetings (Audit, Compensation, Nominating and Governance). It is written to the standard that a Series A or Series B diligence team will read it, note that the CEO is running a real board, and infer institutional maturity from the packet quality alone. First-time-founder boards routinely ship 40-slide packets with no consent agenda, no risk register, and no executive-session agenda; those packets are rewritten inside 24 hours of the Series A lead's first packet review and the founder spends the next quarter cleaning up. Hearth ships the mature format from meeting M1.

**Instantiation flow.** For each quarterly meeting: (1) Corporate Secretary duplicates this template into a new Diligent Boards collection titled `Q<n>-<year>_Board_Packet`; (2) function heads populate their sections against the deadlines in §5; (3) CFO reconciles financial figures against `FINANCIAL-MODEL-SENSITIVITY.md`; (4) COO reconciles operational figures against `KPI-DASHBOARD-FRAMEWORK.md`; (5) CEO writes the executive summary and the board memo; (6) packet distributed **5 business days before meeting** via Diligent Boards; (7) CEO board memo distributed **24 hours before meeting**. Committee packets follow the same flow with the committee chair as accountable owner.

**Confidentiality boilerplate — appears verbatim on every packet cover page:**

> *This packet contains material non-public information of Hearth Inc. and is distributed under the confidentiality and information-rights provisions of the Investors' Rights Agreement and each director's confidentiality obligations under Delaware law and the Board Code of Conduct. Reproduction, printing, forwarding, or discussion outside the closed board session is prohibited without prior written consent of the Board Chair. Directors and observers are reminded of their obligations under the Company's Insider Trading Policy. Personal-email distribution is prohibited; access is via Diligent Boards on approved iOS, iPadOS, or Windows/macOS clients only.*

# §3.5. Governance Concentration Risk on the Single Series A Independent

**Applies to Templates 2, 3, and 4 — referenced on each committee packet cover page.** Also referenced on the full-board packet §G Risk Register as a standing entry under category "Legal/Governance."

## §3.5.1 The concentration itself

At Series A close the Hearth board seats five directors under a 2/2/1 composition (2 common-elected including the Founder-CEO; 2 preferred-elected — one Series A lead director and one shark-designee; 1 mutually-agreed independent) per `BOARD-GOVERNANCE.md` §2.1. That leaves exactly ONE independent director in the composition. Per `BOARD-GOVERNANCE.md` §§6.1, 6.2, 6.3, the same single independent director is:

- **Chair, Audit Committee** (SEC Rule 10A-3(b)(1) financial-expertise posture, per Template 2 header).
- **Chair, Compensation Committee** (independent-chair requirement to avoid the "investor sets founder pay" optics problem, per Template 3 header and `BOARD-GOVERNANCE.md` §6.2).
- **Chair, Nominating and Governance Committee** (per Template 4 header and `BOARD-GOVERNANCE.md` §6.3).
- **Voting member of all three committees.**

## §3.5.2 Why this is a KNOWN concentration risk (and disclosed as such)

A single director carrying three committee chairs plus voting seats is a structural single-point-of-failure. If she resigns, retires, or is incapacitated: three committees go headless the same day; the second independent required by industry norms (2 independents specifically to spread committee-chair load) does not exist to backfill; and Series B diligence will surface the SPOF in the first governance session. The industry norm for well-governed Series B-ready private companies is two independents so that each committee has an independent chair plus at least one independent member other than the chair. Hearth is deliberately entering Series A one-short of that norm because 2/2/1 is founder-friendlier than 3/2 with two independents; this document acknowledges the trade-off rather than papering over it.

Series A diligence teams that read this section will read it as institutional maturity (the company sees the SPOF and has a mitigation plan) rather than as a red flag; Series A diligence teams that discover the same SPOF without a disclosed mitigation plan tend to write it up as a term-sheet condition. Better to own it here.

## §3.5.3 Mitigation plan — three layers

**Mitigation 1 — Recruit a second independent director at Series B close.** Per `BOARD-GOVERNANCE.md` §3 skills-matrix update, the Series B board composition target expands from 2/2/1 to a 2/2/2 or 3/2/2 (three preferred, three common, two independent) shape at Series B. Search for the second independent kicks off ~6 months ahead of Series B close, sourced via Bolster / Athena Alliance / WSGR Board Placement per Template 4 Tab 3. The second independent picks up chair of one committee (likely Comp or Nom-Gov) at Series B, redistributing chair load.

**Mitigation 2 — Move Nom-Gov chair to a common-elected non-founder director.** Permitted at a private company: the IRS Rule 162(m) and SEC Rule 10A-3 independence standards that would forbid this at a public company do NOT bind a private company on its Nom-Gov chair. This mitigation is available immediately if the sole-independent chairs vacate risk becomes material, without waiting for Series B recruiting. Target: recruit a second common seat holder (non-founder — could be COO or Head of Ops with an equity kicker) by Q3 2027 as an interim step, per Template 4 Tab 6 director-term renewals workflow. This move reduces the single independent's load from three chairs to two.

**Mitigation 3 — Board self-eval process surfaces bench strength; interim contingency plan pre-drafted.** The annual board self-evaluation administered by the Nom-Gov chair in September (per `BOARD-GOVERNANCE.md` §10.1 and Template 4 Tab 5) explicitly includes a bench-strength question: "If the current independent director resigned tomorrow, name three candidates the board would fast-track." Answers are compiled by the Corporate Secretary into a standing 45-day emergency-recruiting plan with the following pre-arranged levers:

- **Cooley executive search referral** — the firm's board-placement practice on standby with a pre-negotiated engagement letter (retainer only on activation).
- **WSGR Board Placement** — parallel executive search referral (standing arrangement per Template 4 Tab 3).
- **Bolster / Athena Alliance active-pool short-list** — refreshed quarterly by Nom-Gov chair; a 5-candidate short-list is maintained continuously so the search doesn't start from zero.
- **Interim committee-chair rotation plan** — Corporate Secretary maintains a rotation memo pre-signed by the Board Chair specifying which existing director temporarily chairs each of Audit, Comp, and Nom-Gov during the 45-day gap (Board Chair chairs Nom-Gov by default; Series A lead director acts as interim Comp Chair with the Series A investor observer stepping out of committee attendance to avoid the "investor sets founder pay" optics problem; CFO-nominated financial-expertise director from the shark's designee slot chairs Audit if she qualifies for 10A-3(b)(1) posture, else the search itself becomes an interim-Audit-Chair-required emergency).
- **D&O disclosure & bridge coverage** — Woodruff Sawyer briefed on the SPOF at every renewal; Side A DIC endorsement kept current so a director gap does not create a coverage gap.

Ad-hoc packet uses a slimmed version of the quarterly template — only the sections relevant to the ad-hoc issue plus a mandatory Financial and Risk Register snapshot.

## 6.4 Written consents

Board actions may be taken by unanimous written consent per DGCL §141(f). Common uses: quarterly grant approvals ≤0.1% of company, immaterial contract approvals above CEO authority ≤$250k, routine SAFE issuances if any, D&O policy binding at renewal. Standing consent boilerplate:

> *The undersigned, being all of the members of the Board of Directors of Hearth Inc., a Delaware corporation, do hereby consent, pursuant to Section 141(f) of the General Corporation Law of the State of Delaware, to the taking of the following actions by written consent in lieu of a meeting: [ACTION]. This consent may be executed in counterparts and by electronic signature (DocuSign or Diligent Boards), and shall be filed with the minutes of proceedings of the Board of Directors.*
