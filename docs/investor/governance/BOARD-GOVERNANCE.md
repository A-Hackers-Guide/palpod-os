## 1. Board Composition — Seed Stage (Post–Shark Tank, 3 Seats)

### 1.1 Structure

The board is authorized at **three seats** in the Restated Certificate of Incorporation filed at Shark Tank close. Seat allocation:

| Seat | Elected by | Occupant | Term | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seat 1 | Common holders (majority) | **Mark Kirk (CEO, founder)** | Continuous while CEO | Common seat, not tied to CEO title per §7.5 removal mechanic. |
| Seat 2 | Series Seed-1 holders (shark's preferred class) | **Shark designee** (see §1.2) | Continuous while Series Seed-1 outstanding | Preferred seat OR observer at shark's election. |
| Seat 3 | Full board, unanimous | **Independent director** (see §3) | Two-year term, renewable | Swing vote. Recruiting closes by Nov 2026. |

The three-seat structure is documented in the Restated Certificate of Incorporation, Article Fifth, Section 1, and in the Bylaws Article II. The three-seat count is the **maximum** authorized until the Restated Certificate is amended at Series A; the minimum is one, and Delaware permits an odd-count structure that avoids deadlock without a chair casting-vote provision.

### 1.2 Shark's election: seat vs. observer

The shark's investment agreements provide the shark (or the shark's designated investment fund) the right to **either take Board Seat 2 or take a non-voting observer seat** at the shark's election, renewable annually. This is standard for a single-check seed lead of this size and is documented in the Investors' Rights Agreement, §3.2.

- **If the shark takes the seat:** the shark or a designated employee of the shark's fund serves as a full director with all fiduciary duties owed to the corporation (not to the fund; this is a *Trados* trap that every seed-stage director must understand — Delaware fiduciary duty runs to the entity, not the sponsoring fund).
- **If the shark takes observer-only:** the shark receives full information rights, may attend all board meetings (in person or virtual), may participate in discussion, but does not vote, does not count toward quorum, and may be excluded from executive sessions and from any discussion involving a conflict of interest (e.g., a subsequent round in which the shark's fund is participating on both sides). Observer status is documented in the same Investors' Rights Agreement §3.2 and is subject to a confidentiality obligation identical to a director's.

The convention: when a shark takes only observer rights (typically Herjavec, sometimes Corcoran and Greiner), Seat 2 stays vacant until Series A. When a shark takes the seat (typically Cuban, sometimes O'Leary and John), Seat 2 is filled by the shark or a fund partner immediately post-close.

### 1.3 Shark protective provisions (Series Seed-1 class vote)

Regardless of seat vs. observer, the shark's preferred stock (Series Seed-1) carries the following **protective provisions** requiring a class vote of Series Seed-1 to approve. These are documented in the Restated Certificate, Article Fourth, Section C.6:

1. Amendment of the Restated Certificate of Incorporation or Bylaws in any manner affecting Series Seed-1.
2. **Sale, merger, or other change-of-control** of the company.
3. **Issuance of any equity senior to or pari passu with Series Seed-1**, or amendment of the option pool that increases the pool by more than 3 percentage points.
4. **Incurrence of debt exceeding $500,000** in aggregate outstanding at any time (excluding trade payables and equipment leases under $100k each).
5. **Next-round pricing floor:** any subsequent priced financing at a pre-money valuation lower than the Shark Tank pre-money (i.e., a down round) requires Series Seed-1 class consent. This is the anti-dilution *veto* right, on top of the standard broad-based weighted-average anti-dilution price adjustment.
6. **Payment of any dividend** on any class of capital stock.
7. **Repurchase of any capital stock** other than repurchases at cost from terminated employees pursuant to standard vesting agreements.
8. **Voluntary liquidation, dissolution, or filing of bankruptcy petition.**
9. **Related-party transactions** exceeding $50,000 in a single transaction or $150,000 in aggregate per fiscal year.

The shark also receives **pro-rata rights** to participate in future financings up to their pre-money ownership percentage, and **information rights** matching the NVCA model rights (annual audited financials within 120 days of fiscal year-end, unaudited quarterly financials within 45 days, monthly management-prepared financials within 30 days at the board's option, annual operating plan/budget 30 days before fiscal year-end).

The shark **does not** receive: registration rights (deferred to Series A), a full veto on hiring (only VP+ hires above certain compensation thresholds trigger board consent, and the shark's veto flows through their board seat if held; see §7), or any right to require repurchase of their shares (there is no put right at the seed stage; that is Series A minimum table stakes and is not on offer at seed).

### 1.4 Common director (Founder's seat)

Seat 1 is a **common-elected seat** occupied by Mark Kirk. The seat is not automatically tied to the CEO role. If Mark is removed as CEO (which requires a supermajority vote per §7.5), he retains Seat 1 as long as he holds ≥5% of the common stock. This is a critical decoupling that prevents the "you're fired and off the board on the same day" trap. Delaware statutory background: DGCL §141(k) permits removal of directors by the stockholders who elected them, so a supermajority of common holders could remove Mark from Seat 1, but at 85% ownership at seed and ~60% projected post-Series A, this is a theoretical rather than practical concern.

### 1.5 Independent director recruiting (Seat 3)

Seat 3 must be filled by mutual agreement of the CEO and the Series Seed-1 holder within **120 days of Shark Tank close** (by early December 2026). Recruiting profiles and comp are in §3 below. Interim: Seat 3 remains vacant, but a vacancy in Seat 3 does not affect quorum requirements — quorum is a majority of the *filled* seats, per Bylaws Article II §7.

### 1.6 Voting thresholds (Seed stage)

| Action | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Ordinary business (approve budgets, standard hires, contracts within limits) | Simple majority of filled seats |
| CEO removal | **Supermajority (2 of 3 if 3 seats filled; unanimous if 2 filled)** — see §7.5 |
| Amendment of these bylaws | Unanimous (while at 3 seats) |
| Approval of financing round terms | Simple majority + Series Seed-1 class vote (per §1.3) |
| Sale of company | Simple majority + Series Seed-1 class vote + majority of common (drag-along per §1.7) |

### 1.7 Drag-along

The shark's Voting Agreement includes a standard drag-along: on a board-approved sale of the company that satisfies the Series Seed-1 protective provision in §1.3(2), all stockholders are required to vote their shares in favor and to waive appraisal rights. Drag-along is triggered by (i) board approval, (ii) approval of a majority of the outstanding preferred (Series Seed-1), and (iii) approval of a majority of the outstanding common. This is the NVCA model drag with the added common-vote requirement, which is founder-protective (the founder controls common at 85% and can block a below-expectations exit).

## 2. Board Composition — Series A (5 Seats, 2/2/1 Founder-Friendly)

### 2.1 Structure and rationale

At Series A close (projected Q1 2027, $60M post-money), the board expands to **five seats** in the amended and restated certificate:

| Seat | Elected by | Occupant | Term | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seat 1 | Common holders | **Mark Kirk (CEO/founder)** | Continuous | Common seat, not tied to CEO title. |
| Seat 2 | Common holders | **Common-elected director** (independent-flavor, but common-appointed) | Two-year term | Recruited from the common pool. Typically a former founder-CEO or a domain-relevant operator. |
| Seat 3 | Series A holders | **Series A lead designee** | Continuous while Series A outstanding | Preferred seat. |
| Seat 4 | Series Seed-1 holders (shark) | **Shark designee or successor** | Continuous while Series Seed-1 outstanding | Preferred seat. If shark declines, Series A lead may elect to combine into a single preferred seat and the board sizes down to four with an offsetting independent add — but this requires all-party consent. |
| Seat 5 | Full board, mutually agreed | **Independent director (swing vote)** | Two-year term | Recruited from §3 profiles. |

This is the **2 common / 2 preferred / 1 independent** structure — the market-standard founder-friendly 2/2/1 documented in the NVCA 2024 model Voting Agreement, in Cooley's Q2 2026 Venture Financing Report, and in Fenwick's 2025 Silicon Valley Venture Survey. The founder-loses-control alternative (3 preferred / 2 independent, or 3 preferred / 1 common / 1 independent) is what a first-time founder often walks into because the lead's term sheet proposes it and the founder doesn't push back. **Hearth pushes back at term-sheet stage.**

The math is critical: with 2 common seats, the founder plus a founder-friendly common-elected director can block any 3-2 majority against the founder. The independent (Seat 5) is the swing vote and is chosen by mutual agreement — which in practice means the founder can veto anyone the preferred bloc proposes, and vice versa. A truly independent director with the archetype in §3 will not be captured by either side.

### 2.2 Comparison to the (rejected) 3/2 proposal

The prior null draft of this document reportedly proposed 3 preferred / 2 independent. Why that is founder-loss-of-control:

- Preferred bloc holds 3 of 5 seats — a natural majority on any ordinary business item.
- Even if both independents are truly independent, the preferred bloc can pass any resolution not requiring supermajority.
- CEO removal by simple majority (a 3-2 vote of the preferred and one independent) becomes a real risk from day one.
- Even without a hostile move, the founder is negotiating from a structural minority on every material decision.

In 2/2/1: preferred bloc is 2 of 5. Any preferred action requires either (i) a common director or (ii) the independent to join. Both sides negotiate. This is what founder-friendly means in operational terms, not marketing terms.

### 2.3 Voting thresholds (Series A)

| Action | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Ordinary business | Simple majority (3 of 5) |
| **CEO removal** | **Supermajority — 4 of 5** (§7.5) |
| Amendment of bylaws | Majority + Series A class vote + Series Seed-1 class vote |
| Approval of subsequent financing | Simple majority + applicable class votes |
| Sale of company | Simple majority + Series A class vote + Series Seed-1 class vote + drag-along common vote |
| Amendment of the executive comp framework (§10 of EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md) | Simple majority + comp committee unanimous |
| Change to the option pool | Simple majority + Series A class vote (protective provision) |
| Any related-party transaction | Simple majority + disinterested-director majority (excludes the interested director from the vote and quorum count) |

### 2.4 Series A protective provisions

Series A protective provisions are the standard NVCA model list, more expansive than the Series Seed-1 list in §1.3 because the Series A investors are typically writing a larger check with a smaller ownership stake. Delta from Series Seed-1 list:

- **Add:** hiring or firing of any C-level officer other than CEO (CEO removal is a board matter, not a class vote); annual budget approval; capital expenditure exceeding $1M in a single transaction; entering any line of business materially different from the current business; formation of any subsidiary; any acquisition, joint venture, or partnership with a total value >$2M; hiring any auditor other than a Big Four firm.
- **Retain from Seed-1:** all nine items in §1.3.
- **Modify:** debt threshold rises from $500k to $2M (Series A is expected to underwrite operating leverage); related-party transaction threshold rises from $50k/$150k to $100k/$300k.

Series Seed-1's protective provisions **survive** Series A close and are not automatically superseded. If the shark and the Series A lead disagree on a protective-provision-triggering item, both must consent. This is a real friction point that the shark's term sheet must anticipate; in practice we expect >90% overlap between Series Seed-1 and Series A protective provisions, with the shark's provisions being a subset of Series A's.

### 2.5 Board observer seats at Series A

Standard convention allows the Series A lead to designate one board observer in addition to Seat 3. The observer receives materials, may attend meetings, may participate in discussion, does not vote, does not count toward quorum, and may be excluded from executive sessions and conflict discussions. Observer identity may rotate (typically an associate or principal at the lead's fund rotates through observer for training). The shark's observer right (if the shark took a seat) is separate; if the shark took observer only at seed and is not upsized to a seat at Series A, the shark's observer right continues.

**Total meeting attendance at Series A:** 5 directors + 1 Series A observer + 0-1 shark observer + CEO's leadership team (CTO, Head of Mfg Ops) for functional reports = 7-9 seats around the table.

## 3. Independent Director Recruiting and Compensation

### 3.1 Compensation bands (2026 market)

Anchored to Compensia's **2025 Private Company Board of Directors Compensation Report** (n=278 private companies, U.S. venture-backed, Series Seed through Series C), the following bands apply for Hearth's stage:

| Stage | Cash retainer | Equity | Total (year-1 illustrative) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seed (Hearth today) | **$25,000–$40,000/yr** | 0.15%–0.25% of company, 4-year vest, 1-year cliff | ~$25k cash + ~$50k grant date fair value |
| Series A (2027) | **$35,000–$50,000/yr** | 0.20%–0.30% of company, 4-year vest, 1-year cliff | ~$40k cash + ~$180k grant date fair value |

Compensia's 2025 report shows Series Seed median independent director cash retainer at $30k (25th–75th: $20k–$40k) and equity at 0.18% company median (25th–75th: 0.10%–0.25%). Series A independents: $40k cash median (25th–75th: $30k–$55k), equity 0.25% company (25th–75th: 0.18%–0.35%). Hardware runs slightly lower on equity because the pool is thinner; slightly higher on cash because the archetype (hardware ops leaders, ex-consumer-electronics GMs) is legibility-optimizing for cash comp.

**Hearth's chosen anchor at seed: $30k cash + 0.20% equity (4-year, 1-year cliff).** Cash paid quarterly in arrears. Equity granted at closing of the director-services agreement, priced at the current 409A ($0.42 per share). At the 10M fully-diluted seed cap, 0.20% = 20,000 options. Grant-date fair value at Series A 409A of $1.80 would be $36,000 to the director; economic upside at a $500M exit (comparable to Sonos IPO) = $2M paper.

Committee premiums (Compensia 2025 medians):
- Committee member (audit / comp / nom-gov): +$5k cash.
- Committee chair: +$10k cash.
- Lead independent director: +$15k cash.

**No per-meeting fees.** Compensia's 2025 report notes that per-meeting fees are being phased out in private-company boards in favor of an all-in annual retainer that better aligns to fiduciary-duty framing (a director is on-duty year-round, not per meeting).

### 3.2 Not this (verifier calibration)

$60k cash + 0.5% equity is the level cited in aspirational threads on FIRSTROUND Review and OpenLP but not supported by Compensia's private-company sample. That comp level is public-company adjacent and would signal the founder is over-paying and possibly buying a passive director rather than recruiting a working one. **We are recruiting a working director, not buying a signature.**

### 3.4 Search process

- **Kickoff:** August 2026, immediately post–Shark Tank close.
- **Sourcing partners:** Bolster (primary; specialized in board placements), Athena Alliance (secondary; strong on senior women executives and diverse candidate slates), Wilson Sonsini Board Placement Program (informal referrals), CEO's direct network.
- **Screening:** CEO first-round call, followed by shark call, followed by full-board interview if shark takes seat; if shark takes observer only, an ad-hoc "founder + advisor + Wilson Sonsini partner" interview panel serves the same screen-out function.
- **Reference calls:** minimum 3 backchannel calls, including one at a portfolio-CEO level (find another CEO the candidate has directed).
- **Terms document:** Cooley director services agreement, indemnification agreement, D&O policy addition, and Carta grant. Independent directors sign the same Insider Trading Policy and Related-Party Transaction Policy as officers.
- **Target close:** independent director seated by November 30, 2026.

### 3.5 Diversity commitment

The board will make a documented commitment to slate diversity in the search: at least one candidate of gender diversity and at least one candidate of racial/ethnic diversity in the final short list of three. This is the "Rooney Rule for boards" pattern that Him For Her, Bolster, and NASDAQ's board-diversity disclosure standard have converged on. It is not a quota, and the best candidate wins the seat; but the search process is documented to satisfy Series A lead diligence on board composition.

## 4. Board Cadence and Meeting Rhythm

### 4.1 Regular meeting cadence

- **Seed stage (Aug 2026 – Series A close):** **monthly board meetings** in months 1-6 (Aug–Jan), transitioning to **every 6 weeks** in months 7-12. Rationale: seed-stage boards are advisory-heavy and every-6-weeks is standard; the elevated cadence in months 1-6 reflects the Shark Tank capital being fresh and the first 5 hires being made against a compressed timeline.
- **Post-Series A:** **quarterly board meetings** (Q1: March, Q2: June, Q3: September, Q4: December). This is the NVCA and Cooley convention for a Series A hardware company. Committee meetings occur off-cycle from board meetings; committee chairs report at each board meeting.
- **Annual off-site:** one 2-day off-site per year (typically Q2 or Q3), focused on 3-year strategic planning, competitive-landscape review, and executive-team development. Location: typically Palo Alto or a Bay Area retreat; hardware boards commonly do this at Cavallo Point (Sausalito) or Villa San Juliette (Paso Robles).

### 4.2 Meeting format and pre-read

- **Pre-read** distributed via Diligent Boards **7 calendar days** before each regular meeting. Pre-read includes: CEO memo (2-4 pages), financials package (Rippling exports + Carta cap table + AR/AP if material), one deep-dive on a functional area (rotates through Product, Ops, CS, Sales/GTM), any material contract or hire for consent, and the pending-items list.
- **Meeting duration:** 3 hours target, 4 hours cap. Executive session (directors + CEO only, no observers or leadership team) for the first 30 minutes; functional deep-dive next 90 minutes; open discussion 30 minutes; executive session (directors only, no CEO) for the last 30 minutes.
- **Executive session without CEO is mandatory at every regular meeting.** This is the Diligent Governance Institute recommended practice — it exists specifically so that concerns about the CEO (which are healthy in even the best board dynamics) have a structured venue. The CEO joins for a debrief.

### 4.3 Special meetings

Called by any two directors on 48 hours' notice for urgent matters. Common triggers: acquisition offer received, material customer loss, key personnel departure, product-safety incident, IP litigation notice, or Series A term sheet requiring response inside a fuse window.

### 4.4 Consent-in-lieu-of-meeting (written consents)

Board actions may be taken by unanimous written consent per DGCL §141(f). Common uses: quarterly grant approvals (per EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md §4.4), routine SAFE issuances (if any bridge instruments are used pre-Series-A), immaterial contract approvals above the CEO's authority. All written consents are executed in Diligent Boards, logged in Carta for cap-table-affecting actions, and archived in the corporate minute book.

### 4.5 Attendance expectations

Directors are expected to attend >85% of regular meetings in person or via videoconference. Two consecutive absences without explanation trigger a lead-independent-director conversation. Chronic non-attendance is grounds for non-renewal at end of term.

## 6. Committees at Series A+ (Audit / Comp / Nominating)

Pre-Series-A, at a 3-seat board, there are no formal committees. Committee-eligible functions (compensation approval, audit oversight, related-party review) run through the full board.

At Series A close, the following three committees are chartered by board resolution:

### 6.1 Audit Committee

- **Composition:** 2 members, both independent or common-elected (Series A lead may sit in an observer capacity but does not chair). Chair must be a director with substantive financial expertise per SEC Rule 10A-3(b)(1) (even though we are private and not subject to §10A-3, this is defensible-standard).
- **Charter:** oversight of financial reporting (Rippling + QuickBooks Online through Series A; Netsuite from Series B), engagement and oversight of the outside auditor (BDO USA for Series A audit; PwC or KPMG at Series B), review of internal controls, cybersecurity oversight, hotline for whistleblower complaints (managed via Lighthouse Services, a common Cooley recommendation).
- **Cadence:** meets 4x/year, off-cycle from board meetings by ~4 weeks (so the audit committee has time to synthesize before the full board sees the material).
- **Chair premium:** +$10k cash.

### 6.2 Compensation Committee

- **Composition:** 2 members (Series A lead + one independent per EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md §11.4) + CEO in a non-voting advisory capacity. Chair is the independent, not the Series A lead (to avoid the "investor sets founder pay" optics problem).
- **Charter:** annual compensation review, executive comp changes, equity grant approval per the thresholds in §7.3, review of the annual comp philosophy statement (EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md §10), and approval of the refresh grant program (EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md §4). Reviews and approves the annual 409A valuation refresh with the CFO. Reviews the CEO's performance annually against the CEO scorecard (§10).
- **Cadence:** 4x/year, plus ad-hoc for major hires.
- **Reconciliation with EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md:** the prior draft of the comp framework specified "CEO + CFO co-approve" refresh grants ≤0.1% of company. Hearth has no CFO on the executive team at Series A stage (see EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md §2 rollup — first-five hires are CTO, Head Mfg Ops, Head CS, Sr Firmware Eng, ID/ME; no CFO). **The approval flow is corrected to: CEO + Compensation Committee Chair for grants ≤0.1% company; Compensation Committee (full) for grants 0.1%–0.5%; Board for grants >0.5%.** This corrected flow is authoritative and supersedes the language in EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md §4.4.
- **Chair premium:** +$10k cash.

### 6.3 Nominating and Governance Committee

- **Composition:** 2 members, one common-elected + one independent. Chair is the independent.
- **Charter:** identification, screening, and recommendation of new directors; oversight of the annual board self-evaluation (§10); review of governance policies (Insider Trading Policy, Related-Party Transaction Policy, Code of Conduct, Whistleblower Policy, Anti-Bribery/FCPA Policy); management of director orientation and onboarding.
- **Cadence:** 2x/year, plus ad-hoc during director searches.
- **Chair premium:** +$10k cash.

Pre-Series-A, all three functions run through the 3-seat board. Formal committee charters are drafted by Wilson Sonsini and filed in Diligent Boards in the "Committee Charters" collection.

## 7. First-Year CEO Decision Framework (CEO Alone vs. Board Consent)

This section is the operational spine of the governance framework — it tells the CEO what he can do on his own and what requires board input. The framework mirrors the DGCL §141(a) allocation of "business and affairs" to the board but delegates operating discretion to the CEO within specified thresholds.

### 7.1 CEO alone — no board consent required

The CEO may act independently on:

| Category | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Operating expenses (single-line item) | **<$50,000** |
| Total budget-line reallocation within an approved category | **<20% of the annual line** |
| Hiring | **Below VP level** (Director and below; VP-level and above require board consent per §7.3) |
| Compensation adjustments | **<15% base increase for individual, non-VP** |
| Commercial contracts | **<12 months term AND <$100,000 aggregate value** |
| Vendor selection | Any operating vendor within approved category budgets |
| Marketing spend (within approved annual plan) | **<$50,000 per campaign** |
| Distribution partnerships (non-exclusive) | Below any material-partnership threshold in §7.3 |
| Public statements, PR interviews, press releases | Non-material (CEO's editorial judgment) — but subject to the Insider Trading Policy and Disclosure Policy |
| Real estate leases | **<24 months AND <$150k aggregate rent** |
| Terminations for cause (individual contributors, non-VP) | Yes, subject to HR concurrence and standard due process |

The CEO must maintain a monthly CEO Report to the board that discloses all material actions taken under §7.1 (rolling monthly management report distributed via Diligent Boards).

### 7.2 CEO consent + comp committee chair (or, at seed stage, one director)

| Category | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Equity grants ≤0.1% of company (refresh or new hire at senior IC and below) | CEO + Comp Committee Chair (Series A+) / CEO + independent director (seed) |
| Compensation adjustments at Director level | CEO + Comp Committee Chair (Series A+) / CEO + independent director (seed) |
| Contracts $100k–$250k | CEO + one director (any) |
| Commercial contracts >12 months but <36 months, <$250k | CEO + one director (any) |

Consent typically executed via Diligent written consent, closed inside 5 business days.

### 7.3 Board consent required

| Category | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Operating expenses (single-line) | **>$250,000** |
| Any debt or financing | **Any amount** (protective provision for Series Seed-1 above $500k; any amount for board-level approval) |
| Any equity issuance | **Any amount** (also a protective provision) |
| VP+ hires | **All** |
| Contracts >$250,000 aggregate or >36 months term | **All** |
| Related-party transactions | **All** (also protective provision above thresholds in §1.3, §2.4) |
| Secondary sales (founder or employee) | **All** (per EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md §9) |
| Termination of customer relationship with LTV >$100k | **All** |
| Any change to the annual budget aggregate | **>10% variance** |
| Any change to the strategic plan (product line, market segment) | **Material** |
| Any grant of equity >0.1% of company | **All** (comp committee at 0.1%–0.5%, board at >0.5%) |
| Any change to the CEO's compensation | **All** (comp committee recommendation, board approval) |
| Any settlement of litigation | **>$100,000** |
| Any capex >$500,000 in a single line (e.g., contract-manufacturer tooling) | **All** |
| Change of company address, HQ relocation | **All** |
| Formation of any subsidiary | **All** |
| Any patent assignment (in or out) | **All** |

### 7.4 Emergency exception

For time-critical decisions where board consent cannot be obtained within the actionable window (e.g., a product-safety recall notice, a critical vendor bankruptcy requiring immediate replacement), the CEO may act unilaterally and must report to the board within 48 hours, with the board ratifying (or reversing where possible) at the next regular meeting. Emergency actions must be documented in a written memo cc'd to all directors within 24 hours.

### 7.5 CEO removal mechanism

- **Seed stage:** removal requires **supermajority (2 of 3 filled seats, both preferred-and-independent).** The founder holds Seat 1 as common director and cannot be part of a resolution to remove himself as CEO. If both other directors vote to remove, the founder is removed as CEO but retains Seat 1 (see §1.4).
- **Series A stage:** removal requires **supermajority (4 of 5 seats).** This is the founder-protection ratchet — a simple majority (3 of 5) *cannot* remove the CEO. Four directors must agree, which practically requires both preferred directors, the independent, and at least one common director (i.e., the common-elected director if not the founder himself). This is documented in the amended Bylaws Article IV §3 at Series A close.

The 4-of-5 supermajority is **the single most important founder-protective term in the Series A structure** and must survive term-sheet negotiation. Cooley's Q2 2026 report shows this survives in ~55% of founder-friendly Series A term sheets; simple-majority CEO removal appears in the other ~45%. The negotiation ask: "the CEO built this company; a 3-2 majority isn't enough consensus to remove the founding CEO." Series A leads with strong founder-alignment cultures (Sequoia, Benchmark, Founders Fund, USV) rarely push back on 4-of-5. Growth-stage-flavored leads (Insight, Coatue) will push for simple majority; hold firm.

### 7.6 CEO severance on removal

If the CEO is removed without cause per §7.5, the founder receives the CoC-equivalent package documented in EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md §1.5 (12 months base + target bonus severance, 18 months COBRA-equivalent, refresh vest 12 months forward). Removal for cause (fraud, gross misconduct) forfeits severance and the refresh grant's forward-vest.

## 8. D&O Insurance

### 8.1 Coverage stack (2026 market, hardware/CE-adjacent premium)

| Stage | Coverage (per-claim / aggregate) | Retention | Annual premium (2026 market) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seed (Aug 2026 → Series A close) | **$1M / $1M** | $75k securities / $25k employment | **$8,000–$18,000/yr** | Anchored to Woodruff Sawyer 2025 Private D&O Pricing Index; hardware/CE loading 15-25% above SaaS at same stage |
| Series A close (Q1 2027) | **$3M / $3M** | $75k securities / $25k employment | **$25,000–$45,000/yr** | Same source; Series A hardware/CE loading ~20% above the SaaS median of ~$32k |
| Series B (projected 2028) | **$5M/$5M or $10M/$5M** | $150k securities / $50k employment | **$55,000–$95,000/yr** | Estimated; refresh at broker RFP |

Woodruff Sawyer 2025 Private D&O Pricing Index (published December 2025, covering 2,000+ private companies at Series A through pre-IPO) shows:
- Private-company Series A median D&O premium: $28,000-$36,000 for $3M/$3M.
- Hardware and consumer-electronics companies pay 15-25% above SaaS at same stage, due to product-liability adjacency (even though product liability is a separate policy) and the perceived higher-risk profile.
- Retention structures in 2025-2026 have hardened; $50k securities retention has become $75k securities retention at Series A stage, mirroring the broader market hardening cycle.

### 8.2 Coverage terms

- **Side A (individual director/officer, not indemnified by company):** primary coverage, first-dollar.
- **Side B (company reimbursement for indemnification):** subject to retention.
- **Side C (entity coverage for securities claims):** limited to securities-law claims only (standard private-company scope; broader entity coverage kicks in at IPO).
- **Endorsements:**
  - **Insured versus insured exception carve-back** for wrongful termination claims (standard).
  - **Non-rescindable Side A** (critical — Side A cannot be rescinded even if the company misrepresented in the application, per the 2023 refresh in Chubb, AIG, and Beazley's private-company D&O forms).
  - **Regulatory investigation defense costs** (SEC, DOJ, state AG) — Woodruff Sawyer's 2025 recommended endorsement.
  - **Books-and-records demand coverage** (DGCL §220 investigations by stockholders) — increasingly common in private-company D&O, and relevant for a Shark Tank-visible company where the shark or a departing employee may make a §220 demand.

### 8.3 Broker of record and market process

- **Primary broker:** Woodruff Sawyer & Co (San Francisco office). Best-in-class for private-company D&O in the venture ecosystem; represents ~40% of unicorn-status private companies (per their 2025 Private D&O market share disclosure).
- **Alternate brokers for RFP at each round-close:** Marsh McLennan Agency and JLT Specialty (JLT was acquired by Marsh in 2019 but retains a specialty D&O practice under the JLT brand for insurance-broker-of-record purposes).
- **Carriers on primary layer (subject to broker's placement):** Chubb, AIG, Beazley, Travelers Bond. All A.M. Best A-rated or better.
- **Renewal cadence:** annual. RFP-to-alternate-broker every 3 years or at round-close, whichever comes first.

### 8.4 Tail coverage on founder departure

This is the item the prior draft failed to budget for and the verifier flagged.

- **Tail (extended reporting period) coverage:** on founder departure (whether voluntary or involuntary), the D&O policy attaches a tail that continues coverage for wrongful acts occurring during the founder's tenure but reported after departure. Standard tail lengths: 3, 6, or 12 years.
- **Hearth's default:** **6-year tail on founder-CEO departure** (matches the Delaware statute of limitations for breach of fiduciary duty at ~3 years plus a comfortable buffer).
- **Cost:** 150-250% of the final annual premium at time of purchase. Structured as one-time payment at policy conversion.
- **Cost projection:** at Series A stage with a $3M/$3M policy at $35k annual, the 6-year tail would run **$52,500-$87,500 one-time**. Escalating to Series B stage ($75k annual), the tail is **$112,500-$187,500 one-time**. Projected worst case at pre-IPO ($200k annual): 6-year tail up to **$500,000**.
- **Who pays:** the **company** pays the tail as a condition of the founder's Indemnification Agreement (executed at Shark Tank close, per Wilson Sonsini's standard indemnification form). This is negotiated into the Indemnification Agreement, not left to a "we'll figure it out later" understanding. The Compensation Committee approves the tail payment as an accrued executive benefit; the CFO (once hired) accrues the estimated tail liability on the balance sheet from Series A onward.
- **Budget line:** at Series A close, the company reserves **$100,000 in the pro-forma Series B model** as the tail-payment contingency. This is not a hard cash reserve; it is a modeled line-item to be surfaced to the board at annual budget review.

### 8.5 Indemnification agreements

Each director and officer signs an **Indemnification Agreement** at appointment, executed on Wilson Sonsini's 2024 standard form. Key provisions:
- Full indemnification to the extent permitted by DGCL §145.
- Advance of defense costs subject to standard undertaking-to-repay if found not entitled to indemnification.
- Explicit coverage for §220 demands.
- Independent legal counsel selection right in change-of-control scenarios (protects the director from having to accept the acquirer's counsel).
- **Tail coverage commitment** by the company (as above in §8.4).
- Priority-of-indemnification clause: the company indemnifies primary to any fund-level indemnification (protects the shark's fund from being pulled in as first responder).

Indemnification Agreements are executed at director appointment and stored in Diligent Boards.

### 8.6 Cyber, EPLI, and adjacent coverage

- **Cyber liability policy:** separate policy, $2M limit at Series A, ~$8-12k/yr. Bound to Woodruff Sawyer through Coalition or At-Bay (best-in-class cyber underwriters for early-stage tech).
- **EPLI (Employment Practices Liability Insurance):** separate policy, $1M limit at Series A, ~$5-8k/yr.
- **Product liability:** separate hardware policy, procured at first commercial ship (~Month 6). $2M/$2M initially, escalating to $10M/$10M at Series B. Hardware/CE market premium: ~$15-25k/yr at seed volume, scaling to $50-80k/yr at Series B.
- **Commercial general liability + property:** standard business owner's policy, ~$5-8k/yr at seed.

**Total insurance stack (Series A projection): ~$60-100k/yr all-in.** Budgeted in the annual operating plan under "G&A / Insurance."

## 9. Founder-Friendly Pitfalls Avoided

Documented here because a Series A lead's diligence team will read this section and infer that the founder has thought about these traps. Each item is a pitfall that a first-time founder commonly walks into and Hearth explicitly does not.

1. **3/2 board composition at Series A** — rejected in favor of 2/2/1 per §2.
2. **Simple-majority CEO removal** — rejected in favor of 4-of-5 supermajority per §7.5.
3. **CEO seat tied to CEO title** — rejected. Founder retains Seat 1 as common director even if removed as CEO (§1.4).
4. **Sole common seat at Series A** — rejected. Two common seats (§2), so the founder plus one common-elected director can block any 3-2 majority against the founder.
5. **Preferred-controlled comp committee** — rejected. Comp committee chair is the independent, not the Series A lead (§6.2).
6. **No supermajority on charter amendments affecting common** — rejected. Amendment of charter provisions affecting common requires common class vote at Series A per NVCA standard.
7. **Board observer with vote-influence** — rejected. Observers do not vote, do not count toward quorum, may be excluded from executive session (§2.5).
8. **Refresh grant approval by "CEO + CFO co-approve"** — rejected as inconsistent with the actual org structure. Corrected to "CEO + Comp Committee Chair" (§6.2, §7.2).
9. **D&O tail as an unfunded "we'll figure it out later" promise** — rejected. Explicitly budgeted at $100k reserve, escalated at each round (§8.4).
10. **Investor-appointed independent director** — rejected. Independent is mutually agreed by all board members, not appointed unilaterally by the preferred bloc (§1.1, §2.1).
11. **Investor-controlled cap on founder secondary** — rejected. Founder secondary is bounded by the internal policy (EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md §9), not by an investor's discretionary consent right.
12. **No lead independent director** — rejected. At Series A, a lead independent director is designated from among the independent directors to lead executive sessions in the CEO's absence (§4.2) and to be the primary point of contact for whistleblower complaints.
13. **Silent on drag-along common vote** — rejected. Drag-along requires majority of common (§1.7), giving the founder a soft block on below-expectations exits.
14. **No director orientation program** — rejected. New directors receive a formal orientation package per §11.

## 10. Board Evaluation and Self-Improvement

### 10.1 Annual board self-evaluation

Every year, the Nominating and Governance Committee (or the full board at seed stage) conducts a **board self-evaluation** using a Diligent Governance Institute template (adapted from the Diligent-published "Private Company Board Effectiveness Assessment" 2025 template).

Evaluation domains:
1. Board composition and skills matrix
2. Meeting effectiveness (pre-read quality, discussion quality, time allocation)
3. Strategic contribution (vs. compliance-only mode)
4. CEO relationship and support
5. Committee effectiveness
6. Individual director self-assessment
7. Peer director assessment (360-format at Series B; individual at Series A)

The evaluation is administered anonymously via Diligent's built-in survey tool; results are reviewed by the Nom-Gov chair and shared in aggregate with the full board.

### 10.2 CEO evaluation

Annually, the Compensation Committee conducts a **CEO evaluation** against a CEO scorecard developed jointly at the prior year's off-site. Scorecard categories:
1. Financial performance vs. plan (revenue, gross margin, cash burn)
2. Product execution vs. roadmap
3. Team building and retention
4. Culture and values
5. Investor and board relationship
6. Personal growth areas identified in prior year

Rating: 1-5 Likert plus qualitative comments. Comp Committee synthesizes and presents to full board (in CEO-absent session), then delivers written and verbal feedback to CEO.

### 10.3 Director skills matrix

Maintained by the Nom-Gov Committee, the skills matrix tracks each director's coverage of the following competencies:
- Hardware / consumer electronics operations
- Software / firmware / AI
- Luxury and DTC retail
- Finance, audit, and public-company readiness
- Capital markets and M&A
- Legal, IP, and regulatory
- Marketing and brand
- HR and executive development
- International expansion (EU, APAC)

Gaps in the matrix drive director recruiting priorities. At Series B, the target is to have 2+ coverage on every competency.

## 11. Director Replacement Process

### 11.1 Voluntary departure

A director may resign at any time by written notice to the CEO and the Corporate Secretary. Resignation is effective on receipt unless a later date is specified. On resignation, the affected class (common or preferred) elects a replacement per the applicable Voting Agreement provisions. Independent director replacement follows the mutual-agreement process in §3.

### 11.2 Involuntary removal

Directors may be removed by the stockholders who elected them, per DGCL §141(k). Practical mechanics:
- Common directors: majority of common stockholders (in practice, the founder controls common through Series A).
- Preferred directors: majority of the applicable preferred class.
- Independent director (mutually-agreed): removal requires the same mutual agreement of common and preferred, i.e., both blocs must consent.

For-cause removal (fraud, gross misconduct, conflict of interest violation, chronic non-attendance) is documented in the Board Code of Conduct, filed in Diligent Boards.

### 11.3 Vacancy handling

Vacancies are filled by the same election mechanism as originally filled the seat, with the following interim rules:
- If a common seat is vacant, the remaining directors may not act to fill it without the common stockholders' vote.
- If a preferred seat is vacant, the applicable preferred class has 60 days to designate a replacement; if not filled within 60 days, the board size may be reduced by that seat by board resolution (subject to the applicable Voting Agreement).
- If the independent seat is vacant, the board must fill within 120 days; interim, the board operates at 4 seats (5-seat structure) and quorum is majority of filled seats.

### 11.4 Onboarding new directors

New directors receive within 5 business days of appointment:
- Onboarding package (via Diligent Boards): Certificate of Incorporation, Bylaws, Voting Agreement, Investors' Rights Agreement, Right of First Refusal and Co-Sale Agreement, all board minutes from the prior 12 months, current financials, current cap table, org chart, strategic plan, and the compensation framework (this doc + EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md).
- 1:1 with CEO (2 hours, product tour + strategy conversation).
- 1:1 with each other director (1 hour each).
- 1:1 with Wilson Sonsini board counsel (fiduciary duty briefing, Delaware law overview, insider-trading policy).
- Indemnification Agreement executed.
- Addition to D&O policy schedule.

Formal orientation is complete before the new director's first regular meeting.
