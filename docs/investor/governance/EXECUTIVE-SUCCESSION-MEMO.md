## 1. Why This Document Exists

Executive succession planning is not optional at Hearth's stage. It is a Delaware fiduciary requirement of the board, an expectation of every institutional investor conducting Series A or Series B diligence, and — most importantly — the mechanism by which the company's mission survives the humans who run it. Hearth's mission (offline AI + media-server hardware sold as a lifetime-owned appliance, protected against the streaming-rental drift of the industry) is longer than any single career. This memo formalizes how continuity is engineered.

The document addresses two categorically distinct scenarios:

**Planned succession** covers voluntary transitions. A founder-CEO who has led the company for five or seven years and elects to step into an Executive Chairman role. A hired CTO who chooses to retire, take a sabbatical, or accept a role elsewhere with adequate notice. A CFO whose term as agreed at hire has concluded. In every planned case, the company controls the timeline — typically twelve to eighteen months of private board-level planning followed by a six-month public transition. Planned succession is a hiring exercise conducted at leisure with an incumbent still in seat.

**Unplanned succession** covers crisis. A founder incapacitated by illness, accident, or death. An executive terminated for cause. An executive who resigns abruptly to accept a competing offer. An executive who becomes the subject of investigation, litigation, or public controversy. In every unplanned case, the company loses time as an input variable. The board must act within hours, not weeks. Communication templates must exist before they are needed. Interim authority must have been pre-designated. The distinction between planned and unplanned succession is not academic — it determines whether the sequence in Section 12 activates in forty-eight hours or eighteen months.

The board's fiduciary duty of care under Delaware law (see BOARD-GOVERNANCE.md §6 and §11) requires that succession be planned in advance, documented, reviewed annually, and updated as the executive team changes. Directors who fail to plan for CEO succession have been found personally liable in shareholder derivative suits. VC diligence at Series B, and certainly at Series C, will request this document by name. Its absence is not merely embarrassing — it is disqualifying for institutional lead investors.

This memo therefore serves four audiences: the board itself (as a governance instrument), institutional investors (as a diligence artifact), the executive team (as a continuity commitment to them and their families), and future successors (as a documented onboarding record).

## 2. Founder-CEO Succession

The most sensitive and most scrutinized category. Mark Kirk is currently the sole executive, sole director beyond the pending board build-out, sole product owner, sole media presence (Shark Tank), and sole face of the Hearth brand. This concentration is normal at pre-Series-A stage and is not itself a fiduciary failure. The failure would be leaving it undocumented as the company scales. The Pre-Series-A Interim Floor described in Section 2.2 is the operational answer to this concentration risk during the window between now and Series A close.

### 2.1 Planned CEO Succession (Y5+ Voluntary Transition)

**Timeline.** Twelve to eighteen months of private lead time before any public announcement, followed by a six-month public transition period during which the incoming CEO shadows the outgoing CEO, receives introductions to key customers and investors, and gradually assumes P&L responsibility. Total transition arc: eighteen to twenty-four months from decision to full handoff.

**Search process — venture-native tier (Series A / early Series B).** Retained executive search engagement with one of the venture-native firms that specialize in placing CEOs at consumer-hardware and consumer-technology companies in the Series A through early Series B stage. Preferred firms: True Search, Daversa Partners, Riviera Partners, or Bolton Partners (consumer practice). Retained fees at this tier run approximately $150,000 to $300,000 for a CEO search, one-third at engagement, one-third at shortlist delivery, one-third at placement acceptance. These firms understand founder-led consumer-hardware companies at Hearth's scale and comp band; they are the market-correct search partners for the company's stage.

**Search process — Series C+ optional escalation.** Only if the CEO search scope and target compensation grow into the Fortune-500 / late-stage band ($1.5M+ total cash + institutional-scale equity, or a public-company-track CEO profile) does the Nominating Committee escalate to the traditional Big Three: Egon Zehnder, Spencer Stuart, or Heidrick & Struggles. Retained fees at that tier run $250,000 to $500,000 or more, priced against first-year target cash comp. The board should not default to these firms below Series C without a specific reason (e.g., a category-defining public-market hire). Below Series C, the venture-native tier is presumptively correct and the Big Three are presumptively over-fit.

**Selection made by the Nominating Committee** based on the specific fit of the search partner to Hearth's stage and category. The Committee documents its firm selection rationale in its minutes so future boards can audit whether the tier match was intentional. Search process runs six to nine months from retention to signed offer at the venture-native tier; nine to twelve at the escalation tier.

**Interim CEO during search.** The outgoing founder-CEO remains in seat for the duration of the search in a planned succession. If a COO has been hired by that point (contemplated at Series B), the COO may assume day-to-day operational authority while the founder-CEO focuses on the search, board relations, and transition preparation.

**Board process.** The Nominating Committee (see BOARD-GOVERNANCE.md §6) drives the process. The Committee approves the search firm, defines the CEO specification (industry background, functional experience, cultural fit, compensation range), reviews the shortlist, conducts final-round interviews with each candidate, and delivers a recommendation to the full board. The full board votes to appoint. Shareholder ratification is not required for CEO appointment under Delaware law but is customary for founder-CEO transitions.

**Founder transition role.** Upon a planned voluntary transition, the outgoing founder-CEO assumes the role of Executive Chairman. This role includes: retained board seat as founder-director (see Section 6), retained equity (no acceleration or forfeiture — equity continues to vest per original schedule for any unvested tranches, though at Y5+ virtually all founder equity is vested), advisory role to the incoming CEO on product and brand, and no operational authority. Executive Chairman receives a nominal compensation of $1 per year plus board director fees per BOARD-GOVERNANCE.md, plus reasonable expenses. The founder does not receive incentive compensation as Executive Chairman; the intent is that founder returns are realized through equity appreciation, not ongoing salary.

**Compensation for outgoing CEO.** Beyond the equity continuation described above, the outgoing founder-CEO receives a transition-period base salary continuation for six months at the founder-CEO rate, plus an earn-out tied to two strategic milestones defined at the time of transition (typically: successful onboarding of successor and achievement of one board-defined strategic metric such as revenue target or product milestone within twenty-four months of transition). Earn-out is paid in cash from company operating funds, not from investor proceeds.

### 2.2 Unplanned CEO Succession (Crisis Path)

Three trigger scenarios: (a) founder incapacitated by medical event or death, (b) founder resigns abruptly for personal, family, or competing-offer reasons, (c) founder terminated for cause by board vote (fraud, breach of fiduciary duty, or gross misconduct — extremely rare in founder-led companies but must be planned for).

#### Pre-Series-A Interim Floor (Operative until Nominating Committee seats independent directors and a Board Chair is elected)

The company acknowledges that until the Nominating Committee has been constituted at Series A close, independent directors have been seated, and a Board Chair has been formally elected by the board, the Board Chair role does not exist as a functional office. Mark Kirk is presently the sole director beyond the pending build-out. In that window the unplanned-crisis cascade cannot rely on an internal Board Chair as the first fallback. The following Pre-Series-A Interim Floor is therefore the operational floor between the date of this memo and Series A close:

- The unplanned crisis path routes DIRECTLY to an external interim CEO firm — Cerius Executives or InterimExecs, both real firms specializing in emergency C-suite placement — engaged at $2,000 to $4,000 per day plus reasonable expenses via a standing letter of engagement retained by outside general counsel.
- Pre-signed emergency authorization is on file with outside counsel authorizing counsel to activate the engagement letter and place a professional interim CEO on-site within seven business days (target three to five days) upon written notification of a trigger event by (i) Mark Kirk's designated healthcare representative, (ii) a surviving spouse or adult family member holding a durable power of attorney, or (iii) outside counsel independently if counsel receives credible evidence of a Section 2.2 trigger and cannot reach Mark within twenty-four hours.
- The pre-signed authorization includes a spending cap (initial six-week engagement, aggregate $200,000, extendable only upon board vote once independent directors are seated) and a scope statement (assume operating authority, preserve cash, maintain vendor and customer relationships, coordinate with outside counsel on public communication, and stand up an emergency board build-out in parallel).
- Cerius Executives and InterimExecs are named in the alternative so that if one firm cannot place within the target window, outside counsel activates the other under the same pre-signed terms.
- This Pre-Series-A Interim Floor is the operational floor between now and Series A close. It sunsets automatically the moment (a) a Board Chair has been formally elected AND (b) at least two independent directors are seated. At sunset, the primary interim cascade below becomes operative and the Floor becomes a fallback rather than the first call.

#### Primary Interim CEO Cascade (Operative after Series A build-out)

Once the Board Chair role exists and independent directors are seated, the interim cascade operates as follows:

1. **CFO** if hired (contemplated Series B). The CFO is the natural interim because CFOs have institutional visibility into every part of the business, established relationships with investors, and no product-organization conflicts.
2. **COO** if hired (contemplated Series B). Same rationale as CFO with operational rather than financial emphasis.
3. **Board Chair with delegated executive authority, engaged in a dual-track posture with an external firm.** If no CFO or COO is in seat, the Board Chair assumes interim CEO responsibility as authorized by the board — but not as a single point of failure. The Board Chair (or the Pre-Series-A Interim Floor engagement per §2.2 above where the Floor is still operative) is expected to spend substantially full-time on Hearth for a maximum of THIRTY (30) DAYS from the trigger event, WITH an external interim CEO firm (Cerius Executives or InterimExecs) engaged in parallel from Day 1 of the crisis. This dual-track approach means the Board Chair is not a single-point-of-failure and an external professional interim CEO is on-site by Day 3 to Day 5 to assume operational authority while the Board Chair pivots back to governance work and executive search runs. Board Chair compensation during the interim period is the pro-rated CEO base salary for the days served in an operating capacity.
4. **External interim CEO firm as the operating executive from Day 3-5 onward.** By design, the external interim firm is engaged on Day 1 of every unplanned crisis regardless of Board Chair availability. This is not a fallback — it is the primary operational role after the first week — and it is what prevents the crisis from converting into a governance emergency.

#### Immediate steps within 48 hours

Hour 0: Trigger event occurs. Board Chair (or, if pre-Series-A, outside general counsel acting under the pre-signed authorization) is notified by whoever first learns of the event (attending physician, family member, General Counsel, or founder directly if the trigger is voluntary resignation).

Hour 0-4: Board Chair (or outside GC) convenes with General Counsel, Head of Comms (or outside PR advisor), and — critically — dispatches the notice that activates the standing engagement letter with Cerius or InterimExecs. This convening happens by phone or video; no in-person meeting is required. The purpose is to confirm the trigger, determine severity, review this succession document, engage the external interim firm, and prepare for emergency board meeting.

Hour 4-24: Emergency board meeting convened by conference call. All directors are required to attend or send written proxy. Quorum requirements per bylaws are noted; unanimous consent in writing is acceptable if immediate meeting cannot be assembled. The board formally acknowledges the trigger event, ratifies the external interim firm engagement, appoints Interim CEO (which may be the external firm's placed executive as a matter of ratification rather than search), authorizes activation of executive search, authorizes communication to team and stakeholders, and, if applicable, authorizes engagement of employment counsel and public relations advisors.

Hour 24-48: Interim CEO is announced internally to the team, then externally to investors and key customers, per pre-drafted templates. Where the interim is an external firm placement, the announcement makes clear that a permanent executive search is running in parallel and that the external interim is a defined-term engagement, not a permanent appointment.

#### Communication

Pre-drafted templates are maintained in the data room and updated annually. Three primary templates:

- *Internal team communication*: sent by Head of People within two hours of interim announcement. Emphasizes continuity, addresses natural anxiety about job security, and provides a mechanism for team members to ask questions (typically an all-hands within seventy-two hours).
- *Investor communication*: sent by Board Chair (or outside GC pre-Series-A) to lead investors first (phone, not email), then to the full cap table. Emphasizes governance controls, interim leadership qualifications, and continuity of strategic plan.
- *Customer and dealer communication*: sent by interim CEO or Head of Customer Success. Emphasizes uninterrupted service, warranty commitments, and continuity of product roadmap.

Two secondary templates are also maintained:

- *Press statement*: prepared but not released absent affirmative decision by Board Chair (or outside GC pre-Series-A), Interim CEO, and Head of Comms. Default posture is to avoid press until permanent successor is named.
- *Employment counsel notification*: if trigger involves termination for cause or contested resignation, the company's employment counsel is engaged immediately to advise on separation, non-disparagement, and litigation risk.

**Search process activation.** Same venture-native search firms as planned succession (True Search, Daversa Partners, Riviera Partners, or Bolton Partners consumer practice) at the Series A / early Series B stage; the Big Three (Egon Zehnder, Spencer Stuart, Heidrick & Struggles) are considered only where the search scope has already grown into the Series C+ band. Retention letter signed within seventy-two hours of the trigger event. Search timeline compressed to four to six months rather than six to nine, given emergency posture. Nominating Committee reviews shortlist and delivers recommendation to full board.

**Legal review.** Employment counsel reviews the founder's employment agreement, equity holdings, and any relevant restrictive covenants (non-compete, non-solicit, IP assignment). General Counsel reviews board resolutions and ensures the sequence is properly documented in board minutes. Both parties coordinate with outside securities counsel on any disclosure obligations to lenders, insurers, or contracted parties.

### 2.3 Named Contingency Executors (Archetype Register)

Per FOUNDER-NARRATIVE.md §Team-of-One, the following archetype thought-partners are referenced in Hearth's continuity documents. Actual named individuals are NOT listed in this main memo. The archetype descriptors below define the profile the Nominating Committee seeks when maintaining the register. They are governance signals, not legally binding contingency arrangements.

**Explicit disclaimer.** The individuals whose names sit against these archetypes in Appendix A are named as archetype thought-partners only. **Handling of Appendix A.** Outside general counsel is the sole custodian of Appendix A. The main memo therefore carries no hedged "or are being informed" language: either a named individual has been contacted and acknowledged, in which case the acknowledgment sits in Appendix A alongside the name, or the individual has not been contacted, in which case the name is absent from Appendix A and the archetype seat is treated as vacant in the register.

The CTO is Hearth's first Series A executive hire (per EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md §2). This individual owns firmware, hardware architecture, ML pipelines, and technical direction of the product.

Compensation package per EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md §2 with market adjustment at time of hire. Outgoing CTO transitions to Advisor role for twelve months at $5,000 per month plus retained equity per vesting schedule.

**Unplanned CTO succession.** Same forty-eight-hour activation sequence as CEO (Section 2.2), scaled appropriately, and including the Pre-Series-A Interim Floor logic where a CTO is present but no Board Chair yet exists.

## 5. Team-Below-Executive Succession

Succession discipline extends below the C-suite. Every functional lead should have a documented backup.

- **Head of Firmware Engineer.** Backup: Firmware Engineer 2 rises to interim Firmware Engineer Lead. Development environment, signing keys, and repo access documented in operational runbook. Cross-training between firmware engineers is a standing quarterly expectation.
- **Head of Product.** Backup: rotation from Product Manager tier. Product roadmap, customer research, and prioritization frameworks documented and accessible to at least two people at all times.
- **Standard tier (individual contributor roles).** First option is hire-from-within with clear internal promotion criteria; second option is external search through standard technical recruiter channels. Retention bonuses for critical individual contributors during executive transitions are pre-authorized up to $25,000 per person, per event, per year.

The general principle: no single individual below the C-suite should be the sole holder of any credential, key, contract, vendor relationship, or piece of institutional knowledge. Documentation and cross-training are enforceable through the operational runbook, reviewed quarterly by the CEO and Head of People.

## 6. Board Succession

Board continuity is governed by BOARD-GOVERNANCE.md §11 and further specified below.

**Independent director resignation.** Forty-five-day emergency recruitment cycle per BOARD-GOVERNANCE §3.5. Nominating Committee identifies replacement candidates from the board's standing bench of vetted independents. Interim board decisions during the vacancy are made by remaining directors with quorum adjusted per bylaws.

**Series A director change (investor-appointed director departs).** The Series A lead firm retains the right to designate a replacement director as long as the fund continues to hold at least the ownership threshold specified in the Series A stock purchase agreement (typically 5% of common on an as-converted basis). If the fund has distributed or sold below that threshold, the seat converts to an independent director seat filled through the standard Nominating Committee process. Founder consent to the replacement is not required but is customarily sought.

**Founder director seat (Mark Kirk).** Only vacated by: (a) Mark's own written resignation, (b) removal for cause by supermajority board vote as defined in bylaws and stockholder agreement (a very high bar — typically limited to felony conviction, uncontested fraud finding, or judicial declaration of incapacity), or (c) death or judicial determination of incapacitation. The founder director seat is protected by the stockholder agreement's board composition provisions negotiated at each financing.

**Board Chair succession.** The Board Chair is elected annually by the board. Chair succession is a scheduled agenda item at the first board meeting of each calendar year. In the event of Chair incapacitation, the Lead Independent Director (if the roles are separated) or the longest-serving independent director assumes Chair duties on an interim basis. Prior to Board Chair election at Series A close, the Pre-Series-A Interim Floor in §2.2 is the operative substitute.

**Committee chair succession.** Audit, Compensation, and Nominating Committee chairs are appointed annually by the Board Chair with full board ratification. Vice-chairs are designated for each committee to ensure continuity in the event a chair steps down mid-term.

## 7. Advisor Board Succession

The advisor board (formal advisors distinct from board directors) provides technical, industry, and category expertise without fiduciary responsibility.

- **Advisor term.** Two years, renewable by mutual agreement. Advisor equity vesting per standard advisor agreement (typically 0.10% to 0.25% over the two-year term with monthly cliff).
- **Advisor departure.** Ninety days' written notice absent controversy. Advisors leaving on good terms retain vested equity and are named as Advisor Emeritus on the company website for one year.
- **Advisor replacement.** Same profile per FOUNDER-NARRATIVE.md §5. The CEO and Board Chair jointly identify replacement advisors. Full board is informed but ratification is not required for advisor appointments (advisors are not directors).
- **Advisor conflict management.** Any advisor whose primary employment or investment activities become competitive with Hearth is asked to resign or transition to Advisor Emeritus status. This provision is included in the standard advisor agreement.

## 8. Institutional Continuity

The ultimate purpose of succession planning is that any successor — expected or emergency — can walk into any role and find the information they need to lead. This is achieved through disciplined institutional documentation, enforceable regardless of who holds any role.

**Product ownership documentation.** Complete bill of materials with vendor contacts, unit costs, lead times, and second-source options. Product roadmap with dated milestones spanning eighteen months forward. Architecture documentation for every subsystem — enclosure, mainboard, storage, cooling, firmware, ML pipeline, network stack, remote-support layer. Design files stored in redundant version-controlled repositories with access managed through role-based permissions, not individual accounts.

**Legal record of strategic decisions.** Every board meeting minuted with resolutions attached. Every material contract stored in the data room with clear owner and expiration date. Every equity transaction — issuance, cancellation, transfer — reflected in the cap table maintained by the corporate secretary (Carta or equivalent).

**Financial books audit-ready at all times.** Monthly close within ten business days of month-end. Quarterly review by outside accountants until CFO is hired and Big-Four or comparable audit is engaged (typically at Series B). Bank reconciliations completed monthly and reviewed by two people. Payroll processed through a third-party provider with clear delegation of authority.

**Complete HR files.** Every employee has a signed offer letter, signed IP assignment agreement, signed employee handbook acknowledgement, benefits enrollment records, and current W-4 (or state equivalent) and I-9 on file. Compensation history is documented, and equity grants are papered through Carta with correct 409A valuation applied.

**IP portfolio and licenses complete.** Per IP-ENFORCEMENT-PLAYBOOK. All trademarks registered in applicable jurisdictions with renewal calendar. Patents filed and maintained. Copyright registrations for firmware and creative assets. Open-source license inventory with compliance obligations tracked. Domain names, social handles, and app store accounts owned by the company entity, not by individuals.

**Every relationship documented.** Customer relationships in CRM. Dealer and distributor agreements in the data room with clear expiration and renewal terms. Investor relationships in a maintained investor CRM with quarterly touch schedule. Advisor relationships in the advisor board register. Vendor relationships in the operations vendor register with alternate suppliers identified for each.

The single most common mode of institutional failure at founder transition is that the founder held key relationships in their head and no one else has the context to sustain them. Section 8 exists to prevent that failure.

## 9. Key-Person Insurance

Insurance is not a substitute for succession planning but is a financial backstop that funds the succession process and reassures counterparties. The amounts below are calibrated to venture-backed consumer-hardware market norms at each stage; they are ceilings, not floors, and the Nominating & Governance Committee revisits them at each financing close.

- **Founder personal term life insurance.** $2M policy per EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md §1.6, already in force, funded by the company as a benefit. Beneficiary is the founder's designated personal beneficiary (family).

- **Founder-CEO key-person policy on the company balance sheet.**
  - **Series A close: $10M.** Standard market for a Series A consumer-hardware company with a single-founder concentration. Beneficiary is Hearth Technologies, Inc.
  - **Series B close: $25M.** Allowed to grow modestly above Series A to reflect a higher enterprise value and larger executive search / retention envelope in the event of a founder trigger.
  - **Cap at $25M through Series C.** Above $25M the underwriting shifts from routine to invasive: it requires a substantial personal financial statement, extended medical exams (potentially including cognitive and cardiovascular testing), multiple carriers stacking coverage, and a formally documented insurable-interest justification tied to enterprise value directly attributable to the individual. Building a case for $50M of founder-tied enterprise value contradicts the whole succession thesis this memo argues — that Hearth's value is engineered to survive any single human. The board therefore holds the cap at $25M through Series C and revisits only if actual company valuation and independent underwriting analysis clearly support a higher figure.
  - Purpose across all stages: fund executive search costs, retention bonuses to remaining team, potential customer credits during transition, and general working capital while a permanent CEO is identified and onboarded. Signals to lenders and investors that continuity is protected. Underwriting requires standard medical exam; premiums are a normal operating expense.

- **Named C-suite executive key-person policies.** $5M policy per executive on the company balance sheet from Series A close for the CTO, and adding each subsequent C-suite hire (Head Mfg Ops, Head CS, CFO, GC, VP International) at their respective hire dates. Purpose is the same as the founder-CEO policy scaled to the role. This tier is stable across Series A, B, and C absent a specific reason to increase (e.g., an executive whose departure would trigger customer-contract change-of-control clauses).

- **Board director and officer (D&O) insurance.** Separate from key-person and required for board recruitment.
  - **Series A: $10M primary limit.** Market at Series A is $5-10M primary; $10M is the correct market floor for a consumer-hardware company recruiting institutional directors. $5M is thin and reads as unserious to prospective independents.
  - **Series B: $15M+ primary + excess.** Market at Series B is $15-25M primary plus excess layers. Hearth targets $15M primary with a $10M excess tower ($25M total tower) as the practical floor at Series B close.
  - **Series C: $25M primary + excess.** Reviewed annually. Excess tower expanded to match the risk profile of a public-track later-stage company.
  - Covers directors and officers against personal liability for board actions, subject to standard exclusions (fraud, criminal misconduct).

- **Employment practices liability insurance (EPLI).** Separate from D&O. Typically $2M at Series A, revisited at Series B. Covers claims of wrongful termination, harassment, and discrimination.

Insurance is procured through a commercial insurance broker specializing in venture-backed technology companies. Broker relationships and policy documents are stored in the data room. The Compensation Committee and Audit Committee jointly review coverage adequacy annually alongside this memo.

## 10. Compensation for Successor CEO

External CEO recruitment commands a market premium relative to founder-CEO compensation. The board should expect and budget for this.

- **Base salary.** 20% to 40% above the outgoing founder-CEO's base at time of transition, benchmarked to consumer-hardware CEO comparables at similar revenue scale. Sources: Advanced-HR, Aon Radford, or a compensation consultant retained for the transition.
- **Annual bonus.** Standard executive package of 50% to 100% of base at target, tied to board-approved company goals. Payout ceiling of 200% of target for exceptional performance.
- **Equity.** 5% to 10% of the fully-diluted company for an external CEO, per EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md §7.4 refresh mechanic. Vesting four years with one-year cliff. Board may grant additional equity refresh at years two and three based on performance.
- **Sign-on.** Cash sign-on to offset unvested equity forfeited at prior employer, typical range $250,000 to $1M depending on candidate seniority. Sign-on subject to clawback if executive leaves voluntarily within twenty-four months.
- **Employment term.** Three-year initial term, renewable. Twenty-four-month severance if terminated without cause, twelve-month severance if terminated for good reason by executive. Standard change-of-control double-trigger acceleration on equity per EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md CoC provisions.
- **Relocation.** If relocation is required, standard executive relocation package (moving expenses, temporary housing up to six months, home-sale assistance, tax gross-up on taxable portions).

Total first-year target compensation for an external CEO at Hearth's Series B stage is anticipated to be $700,000 to $1,200,000 in cash plus 5% to 10% equity at then-current 409A valuation. This target-comp band is the primary reason the search-firm tier defaults to venture-native (True Search, Daversa Partners, Riviera Partners, Bolton Partners) rather than the Big Three: the fee economics of a $150-300k retained search fit a $700k-$1.2M target-comp CEO; the Big Three's fee economics assume a target-comp band substantially above that.

## 11. Board Fiduciary Duty Education

Annual session for the full board, typically at the January or February board meeting each year, delivered by outside counsel (corporate governance specialist). Session materials are retained in the data room.

**Topics covered every year:**

- *Delaware fiduciary duty of care.* Directors must inform themselves of material information reasonably available prior to making a business decision. Includes reading board materials in advance, asking questions, and documenting deliberation.
- *Delaware fiduciary duty of loyalty.* Directors must act in the best interest of the corporation and its stockholders as a whole, not any subset (including the constituency who appointed them). Investor-appointed directors owe duties to the company, not to their fund.
- *Business judgment rule.* Judicial presumption that directors acted on an informed basis, in good faith, and in the honest belief that the action was in the best interests of the corporation. Rebuttable only by evidence of self-dealing, bad faith, or gross negligence.
- *Corporate opportunity doctrine.* Directors and officers may not personally take opportunities that belong to the corporation without offering them to the board first and receiving a documented waiver.
- *Fair dealing in interested transactions.* Any transaction in which a director has a personal financial interest must be disclosed, approved by disinterested directors or stockholders, and be on terms fair to the company.
- *Conflict of interest management.* Standing conflict disclosure at each board meeting. Written conflict register maintained by corporate secretary. Recusal from votes where conflicts exist.
- *Documentation requirements.* Written board materials distributed at least seventy-two hours in advance. Minutes recorded contemporaneously and approved at the following meeting. Written consents in lieu of meetings signed by all directors and dated.

At Series B and beyond, the board education session also covers material non-public information handling, insider trading policies applicable to directors and officers, and pre-clearance procedures for director transactions in company securities.

## 12. Documented Emergency Succession Activation

The exact sequence for unplanned executive succession, formatted for use under stress.

**T+0: Trigger event.** Board Chair (or outside general counsel pre-Series-A, per §2.2 Pre-Series-A Interim Floor) is notified. Trigger may be medical, legal, personal resignation, or termination for cause. First priority is confirming the trigger with named witnesses (attending physician's report, written resignation, executed termination resolution).

**T+4 hours: Core response team convenes AND external interim firm engagement letter is activated.** Board Chair (or outside GC), General Counsel, Head of Comms (or outside PR), Head of People. The convening call is documented in writing with time, participants, and decisions. In parallel, the standing letter of engagement with Cerius Executives or InterimExecs is activated so a professional interim CEO is en route by T+24 to T+72 hours. Purpose is to review this succession memo, confirm trigger classification, engage the external interim firm, and prepare for emergency board meeting.

**T+24 hours: Emergency board meeting.** All directors present in person or by proxy. Formal resolutions to: (a) acknowledge trigger event, (b) ratify external interim firm engagement, (c) appoint interim executive per Section 2.2 or applicable section, (d) authorize executive search retention at the venture-native tier, (e) authorize communication to team and stakeholders, (f) authorize engagement of employment counsel and public relations advisors as needed. Board minutes drafted and circulated within twenty-four hours of the meeting.

**T+48 hours: Interim executive announced.** Internal team communication first (Head of People delivers), followed within two hours by investor communication (Board Chair, or outside GC pre-Series-A, delivers by phone to leads, then written to full cap table), followed within four hours by customer and dealer communication (interim executive delivers). Press statement held unless affirmatively released.

**Day 3-5: External interim CEO on-site.** Professional interim CEO placed by Cerius or InterimExecs assumes day-to-day operational authority. Board Chair (or outside GC) pivots back to governance work, ratification of board resolutions, and executive-search oversight. If a Board Chair had been running as interim in the first 72 hours, this is the handoff moment — no more than 30 days total in a personal operating role.

**Week 1: Executive search retained.** Retention letter signed with pre-selected venture-native search firm (True Search, Daversa Partners, Riviera Partners, or Bolton Partners consumer practice). Kickoff meeting held with Nominating Committee. Candidate specification drafted and approved.

**Month 1: First candidates presented.** Search firm delivers initial long list. Nominating Committee reduces to interview shortlist.

**Month 3: Shortlist finalized.** Nominating Committee delivers three-to-five candidate shortlist to full board. Board interviews scheduled.

**Month 6: Permanent successor named.** Full board vote on preferred candidate. Offer extended, accepted, and public announcement made. Interim executive returns to prior role or exits per pre-negotiated terms; external interim firm engagement concludes on the successor's start date.

Deviations from this timeline are permissible only by explicit board vote, documented in minutes with rationale.

## 13. Compensation for Outgoing CEO in Unplanned Events

Terminations and abrupt departures require compensation treatment distinct from planned transitions and distinct from change-of-control events.

**Termination without cause.** Two to three times annual base salary paid over the following twelve to eighteen months, subject to release of claims and continued compliance with non-compete and non-solicit obligations. Equity vesting acceleration of twelve months' worth of unvested shares, plus extended exercise window for vested options (typically twelve months from termination rather than the standard ninety days). Continued health coverage under COBRA at company expense for eighteen months.

**Termination for cause.** No severance. Vested equity retained subject to standard exercise window (ninety days). Unvested equity forfeited. Cause must be documented and voted on by disinterested board members.

**Voluntary resignation without good reason.** No severance. Vested equity retained subject to standard exercise window. Unvested equity forfeited.

**Voluntary resignation for good reason.** "Good reason" typically defined as material reduction in duties, material reduction in compensation, or required relocation more than fifty miles. Treated the same as termination without cause.

**Death or disability.** Insurance policies (Section 9) fund the appropriate portion of the compensation. Company continues health coverage for family per benefits plan. Vested equity retained by estate; unvested equity subject to acceleration per equity plan documents (typically one hundred percent acceleration for death, twelve-month acceleration for disability).

**Change-of-control provisions.** Change-of-control acceleration is a distinct category. Per EXEC-COMP-FRAMEWORK.md CoC provisions, executive equity accelerates on a double-trigger basis — acquisition closing plus involuntary termination or resignation for good reason within twelve months post-close. Change-of-control triggers do not apply to unplanned succession in the absence of an acquisition transaction.

Employment counsel reviews all separation terms in every case. Separation agreements are papered before any payment is made.
