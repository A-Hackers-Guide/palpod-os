## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Capitalization

Hearth closed the Shark Tank round at a **$13.33M post-money valuation** on a **$2.0M raise** (15.0% dilution to a founder-only cap table, plus the shark equity carveout modeled in the parallel Governance document). All compensation bands, equity percentages, and refresh mechanics in this document are anchored to two cap-table snapshots:

| Snapshot | Post-money | Fully-diluted shares | Option pool | FMV per share (409A) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shark Tank close (Aug 2026) | $13.33M | 10,000,000 | 10.0% (1,000,000) | ~$0.42 (30% of $1.33 preferred) |
| Series A close (Q1 2027, projected) | $60.0M | 13,300,000 | 16.5% (top-up to 2,194,500) | ~$1.80 (40% of $4.51 preferred) |

The 30%/40% common-to-preferred discount at each 409A is standard for a pre-revenue hardware company (Carta, *Q2 2026 State of Private Markets*, hardware 409A discount medians). Every offer letter cites the 409A in effect at grant date; every refresh grant is repriced to the most recent 409A.

**409A strike-date policy — critical clarification.** Every option grant is priced at the 409A FMV in effect **on the grant date**, not the FMV in effect at some notional future round. Hires with grant dates falling between the Shark Tank close and the Series A close (Sections 2.1–2.5 below) receive their grant at the **$0.42 strike**. This is a substantial candidate-facing incentive: the same 200,000-share grant that would cost $360,000 to fully exercise at the Series A strike costs $84,000 at the pre-Series A strike, and the delta becomes part of the recruiting story. The company does not backdate, forward-date, or hold grants for a favorable 409A — grants are dated to the offer-acceptance/start date and priced at the then-effective 409A. Carta enforces this at the workflow level.

Currency: all figures USD. Geography: base bands assume Bay Area (San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara counties); a **–7% remote adjustment** applies to fully-remote hires per Ravio's 2026 US Geo Differentials index (Tier 1 → Tier 2 spread). Hybrid (2+ days on-site in San Bernardino or a Bay Area co-work) sits at par.

Platform of record:
- **Cap table + option issuance + 83(b) tracking:** Carta (Founder tier through Series A; migrating to Growth tier at Series A close for waterfall modeling).
- **Payroll + benefits + 401(k):** Rippling (chosen over Gusto for hardware BOM cost-center allocation and Deel-compatible international contractor rails).
- **International contractors (industrial design consult, EU firmware review):** Deel Contractor tier.
- **Comp benchmarking:** Ravio (primary), Pave (equity refresh + secondary benchmarks), Carta public data (Peter Walker's quarterly cap-table datasets), Compensia 2025 Executive Compensation Report (VP+ and board), Radford Global Technology Survey (formal, quarterly), Kruze Consulting startup salary data (founder comp), Compare Ninja (sanity check on mid-band), levels.fyi (individual verification), Salary.com (baseline / court-defensible floor).

### 1.2 Bonus target

- **Target annual bonus:** 40% of base at plan, paid via the company multiplier × individual multiplier structure in Section 7.
- **Series A close milestone bonus:** **$50,000, one-time, paid within 30 days of Series A wire clearing.** The prior version of this document proposed a 100%-of-base ($280k) milestone bonus. That figure has been retired. A cash milestone bonus paid to the founder in the six figures out of a $14M raise reads to institutional LPs as founder-out-of-the-raise, and the Series A lead would strike it in diligence. $50k is inside the observed founder milestone-bonus range at Series A (Kruze data: $0–$50k modal), acknowledges the personal balance-sheet drag of the pre-close period without substituting for equity, and does not create a headline the lead can weaponize.
- **Company multiplier applies to annual bonus only,** not to the milestone bonus. Milestone is binary (paid or not).

### 1.3 Founder equity — starting position and dilution path

| Event | Founder % | Founder shares | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre–Shark Tank | 100% | 8,500,000 | Assumes 10M authorized, 1.5M option pool authorized but ungranted |
| Post–Shark Tank close | ~85% | 8,500,000 / 10,000,000 fully-diluted | 15% dilution absorbed by combined shark + pool top-up |
| Post–Series A close (projected) | ~60% | 8,500,000 + 109,725 refresh / 13,300,000 fully-diluted | Refresh of 5.0% of the *post-Series-A* pool = 109,725 additional options; blended founder position is ~64.7% |

**Refresh mechanic (Section 1.4):** at Series A close, the founder receives a **refresh grant of 5% of the post-Series-A option pool**, which at a 16.5% pool sizing on a 13.3M share base = **109,725 options at the Series A 409A strike (~$1.80/share)**. Exercise cost = $197,505; paper value at Series A preferred ($4.51/share) = $495k gross / $297k net of strike.

Not 5% of the *company* — 5% of the *pool*. This is the "ownership refresh target" convention documented in Pave's 2025 *Equity Refresh Benchmarks* and Carta's public refresh guidance (Peter Walker, "Founder Refresh Grants at Series A," Q4 2025). Pave's data on early-founder refreshes at Series A shows a modal 0.5%–1.5% of company; 5% of a 16.5% pool = 0.825% of company, mid-band.

**Board-facing framing:** the refresh signals that the founder's continued daily execution is worth diluting the pool by nearly a full point; the alternative — the founder walking at 60% with no fresh vest — creates a retention gap the lead VC will *require* to be closed as a condition of the term sheet. Ravio's 2026 hardware Series A term-sheet corpus: 71% of first-time-founder Series As included a founder refresh clause.

### 1.4 Vesting

- **Founder original 8,500,000 common shares:** already 100% vested (single-founder pre-institutional). No re-vesting at Series A. This is a defensible position: the founder built the company, closed the Shark Tank round, and holds the vast majority of common; Pave's 2025 data shows re-vesting of pre-Series A founder common is asked in fewer than 20% of Series As and conceded in fewer than 8%.
- **Founder refresh grant (109,725 options):** **4-year monthly vest, no cliff.** Standard. Board-approved at Series A close, dated to Series A close date.
- **Refresh sub-clause:** if founder is terminated without cause pre-vest, refresh vests **12 months forward** (single-trigger acceleration on the refresh only, on termination without cause; layered on top of the change-of-control provision in 1.5).

### 1.5 Change of control

The prior version of this document proposed single-trigger acceleration on both the founder's original 8.5M common shares AND the refresh grant. A Series A lead will refuse both. The framework below (Option A of the two negotiating postures the comp committee considered) preserves the more valuable protection — the original common — and concedes on the refresh grant.

- **Founder original 8,500,000 common shares — single-trigger 100% acceleration on a change-of-control event.** The founder's original common fully accelerates on close of any acquisition, regardless of whether the founder is retained. **Rationale for defending single-trigger:** the founder is fully vested pre-transaction; there are no unvested original shares to accelerate — the phrase "acceleration" here operates only on the change-of-control mechanic itself, ensuring that the acquirer cannot condition the founder's rollover on continued employment. This is the founder-pre-vest scenario the NVCA model docs contemplate, and it survives negotiation when the pre-transaction vest position is already 100%. We will defend this line.
- **Founder refresh grant (109,725 options) — double-trigger acceleration.** 100% of the unvested refresh grant accelerates only if (a) a change-of-control event closes AND (b) the founder is terminated without cause or resigns for good reason within 24 months post-close. This is industry-median for Series A executives and is the concession we make to the lead in exchange for retaining single-trigger on the original common.
- **CEO role protection:** if founder is removed from the CEO role within 12 months post-acquisition without cause, founder receives **12 months of base salary + target bonus as severance**, paid in a lump sum, plus 18 months of COBRA-equivalent healthcare (employer-paid).

The board's negotiating posture at Series A term-sheet stage: defend Option A; do not offer Option B (double-trigger on all shares) unless the lead makes it a walk-away term.

### 1.6 Insurance and benefits (founder-specific)

- 100% employer-paid healthcare for founder + spouse + all dependents (medical/dental/vision — Rippling PEO plan, Anthem Blue Cross Gold-tier).
- $2M term life insurance policy (employer-paid, portable at separation).
- Own-occupation long-term disability (60% of base to age 65, employer-paid).
- Executive physical benefit ($5k/yr, One Medical Executive Health).
- **D&O tail coverage extended 6 years post-departure (funded by company).** The 6-year tail is priced at 150%–250% of the final annual premium; on a projected post-Series A D&O annual premium of $25k–$45k, the tail cost is **$40,000–$110,000 as a one-time charge triggered at founder departure**. This is now budgeted as a discrete line in the People-team contingency reserve (funded from operating cash quarterly; carried as an accrual on the balance sheet from Series A close forward). Woodruff-Sawyer's 2025 *Private D&O Pricing Index* is the benchmark source; hardware/consumer-electronics rates were 15%–25% above SaaS in 2025 and we assume the same premium at 2026 renewals.

Ongoing founder benefits stack (excluding the tail accrual): approximately **$38k/yr all-in**, loaded into headcount cost for the pitch's unit economics (Objective 21).

### 1.7 Founder secondary rules (cross-referenced to Section 9)

Gated at Section 9: **max 5% of holdings liquidatable per event, only after 24 months of Series A tenure, subject to Series A lead's ROFR, blackout 12 weeks pre any financing close.** Section 9 also adds the community-property clarification (CA) and tax-cooperation clause requested in the v1.1 cleanup pass.

## 3. ISO vs NSO Election

Hearth defaults to **ISO (Incentive Stock Options)** for all US W-2 employees, subject to the mandatory partial-NSO conversion for the portion of any grant whose strike-value first vests over the $100,000/year ISO cap (IRC §422(d)).

### 3.1 The $100k rule in practice

Any single employee's ISO grants that vest within a single calendar year cannot represent more than $100k of strike value on the grant date. Strike value = FMV at grant × shares vesting that year. For our early hires vesting on a 1-year cliff at the $0.42 strike:

- Sr Firmware Engineer at 75,000 options × $0.42 FMV = $31,500 strike value at cliff. Well under $100k. Full ISO.
- CTO at 200,000 options × $0.42 FMV = $84,000 strike value at cliff. Under $100k — **full ISO on the initial grant** at the $0.42 strike. (Contrast: at the post-Series-A $1.80 strike, the same grant would have blown through the cap and required NSO bifurcation.) Any refresh at Series A repricing will re-test the cap; Carta handles the ISO/NSO bifurcation automatically at issuance.

### 3.2 Early-exercise election

- Employees who want to early-exercise any tranche must sign an early-exercise election letter, file 83(b) within 30 days of exercise, and pay ordinary-income tax on (FMV – strike) at exercise.
- **83(b) responsibility:** employee, always. Company provides:
  1. Pre-filled 83(b) form via Carta's template.
  2. Reminder emails at day 1, day 15, day 25 post-exercise.
  3. Copy of the executed 83(b) is uploaded to the employee's Carta portal by the employee.
  4. Certified-mail receipt is filed by the employee to the same portal.

Failure to file 83(b) is not a company liability, but Rippling's onboarding automation includes a mandatory acknowledgment.

### 3.3 Disqualifying disposition rules

For ISO tranches: to preserve long-term-capital-gains treatment, holder must not sell within 2 years of grant date *or* 1 year of exercise date, whichever is later. Any pre-Series-C secondary tender includes an FAQ authored by the People team, cleared by external tax counsel (Cooley or Fenwick), spelling out the ISO consequences. **We do not indemnify employees for disqualifying dispositions** but we do provide access to a CPA at company cost during any tender window.

### 3.4 Rule 701 threshold and disclosure

Under SEC Rule 701, private companies can grant securities to employees without §5 registration, but if aggregate sales in a rolling 12-month period exceed $10 million, the company must deliver enhanced disclosures. Our per-employee grant cap that triggers individual-recipient enhanced disclosure is **$1.5M in a rolling 12-month window** (aggregate exercise price + FMV realized). Carta tracks Rule 701 aggregate quarterly.

### 3.5 Cash vs cashless exercise

- **Pre-Series-A:** cash-exercise only. No cashless net exercise.
- **Post-Series-A:** cashless exercise allowed for existing employees (>12 months tenure) at exit windows and at tender events. Newly hired post-Series-A employees are still cash-only for their first 12 months of exercise activity.
- **Exercise cost financing:** we do *not* offer company-guaranteed exercise loans. We provide a referral to ESO Fund and Quid at the employee's option; the company is not counterparty.

### 3.6 International employees / contractors

- Non-US W-2 (via Deel EOR): NSO-only (ISOs are US-tax constructs). Non-US grants use the Carta "Cash-Settled RSU" template with a strike-price-equivalent design in jurisdictions where option grants are prohibitively taxed (Germany, France).
- 1099 contractors: NSO only. Vesting continues only during active engagement; 90-day post-termination exercise window.

## 4. Refresh Grant Program

### 4.1 Eligibility and cadence

- **VP+ and Directors** with ≥24 months tenure: eligible for annual refresh.
- **All other employees**: eligible for annual refresh at 36 months tenure, then every 24 months thereafter.
- **New hires**: not eligible for refresh in months 0–24 (the initial grant is the retention vehicle in the cliff period).
- **Refresh grants are issued at the year-end board meeting** (December), dated to the meeting date, priced at the most recent 409A.

### 4.2 Refresh formula — Target Refresh Value (TRV) method

We use a dollar-denominated **Target Refresh Value** method rather than an ownership-percentage-restoration method. The mechanic is common across the industry; our sizing is anchored to Pave's *2025 Equity Refresh Benchmarks* and Carta's public refresh guidance (Peter Walker, "Equity Refresh Grant Sizing at Series A/B/C," Q1 2026).

**TRV formula:**
> TRV = (unvested current grant shares × current 409A FMV) + (current preferred FMV × current unvested shares × 0.4 dilution-adjust factor)

**Target TRV per band** (calibrated to Ravio 2026 hardware post-Series-A and Pave's 2025 refresh benchmarks):

| Band | Target TRV | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CTO | $2.5M | Highest — mirrors CEO retention level |
| VP-level (Head Ops, Head CS) | $1.2M | |
| Director-level | $600k | |
| Senior IC (Sr Firmware, ID) | $400k | |
| IC | $200k | |
| Individual | Varies | |

**Refresh grant size = max(0, Target TRV – Current TRV) ÷ current 409A FMV,** rounded to nearest 500 shares.

### 4.3 Ownership creep — deliberate absence

The refresh **does not restore original ownership percentage** post-dilution. Pave's data shows a market-wide shift away from percentage-restoration refreshes over 2023–2025; the current best practice is dollar-denominated retention grants sized to a paper-value target. If the CTO joined at 2.0% and dilution takes ownership to 1.4% by Series B, the refresh restores the *dollar-denominated TRV* to $2.5M, not the percentage to 2.0%. Restoring percentage is aggressive dilution the pool cannot bear and would trigger cap-table friction with the Series B lead.

### 4.4 Approval flow

- Refresh grants ≤ 0.1% of company: **CEO + Board Chair co-approve pre-first-comp-committee.** Once the compensation committee is formally seated (targeted for the Series A board reconstitution), authority moves to **CEO + Compensation Committee Chair.** The prior draft named a "CEO + CFO" co-approval, but no CFO exists in the pre-Series-A org; the authority has been reassigned to the Board Chair (pre-comp-committee) and to the Comp Committee Chair (post-comp-committee).
- Refresh grants > 0.1% of company: comp committee approves (or, pre-comp-committee, full board approves); board ratifies at the next regular board meeting.
- All refreshes go through Carta issuance workflow with automated board-consent generation.

### 4.5 Refresh vesting

All refresh grants: **4-year monthly vest, no cliff.** No cliff because these are retention vehicles for tenured employees; a cliff creates a spike-year retention risk that defeats the point.

## 5. Post-Series-A Option Pool Top-Up

### 5.1 Sizing

- **Target post-Series-A pool: 16.5%** of fully-diluted shares post-round.
- Cooley *Q2 2026 Venture Financing Report* medians: Series A pool 12%–15%, hardware 15%–17%. We are in-band. Series A leads will push to 17%–20%; 16.5% is a reasonable negotiating anchor.
- **Rationale for 16.5%:** the first-five grants (v1.1 sizing) consume 440,000 options = ~20% of the post-Series-A pool. Founder refresh consumes ~5% of the pool. Remaining ~75% of pool supports roughly 12–15 additional grants at IC and senior-IC bands through Series B.

### 5.2 Series A pre-money adjustment (mechanical)

Standard term-sheet convention: pool top-up comes *pre-money* to bring the pool to 16.5% *post-money*. This dilutes existing shareholders (founder + sharks) and not the new investors — the founder eats the cost of the top-up in pre-money valuation math.

Example math on a $60M post at 16.5% pool:
- Pre-money target: $46M ($60M – $14M new capital).
- Post-money share count: 13,300,000.
- Post-money pool shares: 2,194,500.
- Existing pool at Shark Tank close: 1,000,000 (10%).
- Top-up: 1,194,500 shares, coming from pre-money dilution to existing shareholders.

### 5.3 Concentration limit

**No single non-CEO hire receives more than 4% of company at any single grant.** CTO's 2.0% is well below. Aggregate cap on any single non-CEO individual (across initial + refreshes + special grants): 5% of company. Above this requires board vote, not just comp committee.

### 5.4 Pool exhaustion trigger

If pool utilization crosses 75% before Series B, we bring a top-up recommendation to the board. Pool top-ups mid-cycle come from unallocated pre-money at Series B; if there is no round in flight, pool top-up requires a board-approved separate stockholder consent.

## 6. Special Equity Considerations

### 6.1 Advisor board (5 seats per founder narrative)

- **Grant size:** 0.05%–0.10% of company per seat (based on FAST — Founder / Advisor Standard Template, from Founder Institute).
- **Vesting:** 2-year monthly, no cliff, quarterly commitment (each quarter of engagement vests forward).
- **Cash honorarium (optional, at CEO discretion):** $1,000–$5,000/quarter for advisors who commit to a defined monthly engagement (typically 4–8 hours/month).
- **Advisor agreement:** Cooley advisor-agreement template with IP assignment, confidentiality, and non-solicit. Grant vests only during active engagement.

### 6.2 Consultants and contract-to-hire

- **Interim / contract-to-hire equity:** 0.02%–0.05% during the interim period, vesting over the contract length (typically 3–6 months, monthly).
- **Conversion:** on conversion to full-time, the interim grant vests fully and a new grant is issued at the full-time band from Section 2. Interim vesting is not credited to the full-time vest schedule.
- **Consultant cap (Carta-enforced):** aggregate consultant equity across all consultants ≤ **0.5% of company**. Carta blocks issuance above this line at the workflow level.

### 6.3 Co-founder / early technical advisor

Reserved for retroactive recognition of a pre-Shark-Tank contributor if such a person is identified. Grant range: **0.25%–1.0% of company**, priced at whichever 409A is nearest their contribution period, subject to board approval and IRS §409A anti-backdating rules. No retroactive vesting — vest starts at grant date.

### 6.4 Merger-related grants

Any grants issued as part of an acquihire or strategic-hire scenario are subject to change-of-control acceleration provisions in the acquired employee's original grant. Hearth's default is to honor the acquired grant terms and issue a supplemental Hearth grant at the standard band, with the supplemental grant subject to Hearth's standard vest.

### 6.5 Employee referral bonus

Referrer receives $2,500 cash on the referred hire's 90-day mark for junior roles, $5,000 for senior IC, $10,000 for director+, and $15,000 for exec. Paid via Rippling. No equity referral bonus.

## 7. Cash Bonus Program

### 7.1 Annual bonus formula

> Actual bonus = Target bonus × Company multiplier × Individual multiplier

- **Target bonus:** % of base as specified per role (10%–40% depending on level and function).
- **Company multiplier: 0.5x–1.5x**, set by comp committee at year-end based on performance against 4–5 objective company OKRs. Y1 OKRs (proposed):
  1. Y1 unit shipments ≥ 800 (target 1,000) — weight 25%
  2. Landed COGS ≤ $58k/unit at 1,000 units — weight 20%
  3. 12-month gross retention ≥ 97% — weight 20%
  4. Series A closed at ≥ $50M post — weight 20%
  5. Zero P0 field failures — weight 15%
- Multiplier ladder: 0 OKRs met → 0.5x; 1 → 0.7x; 2 → 0.9x; 3 → 1.1x; 4 → 1.3x; 5 → 1.5x.
- **Individual multiplier: 0x–1.5x**, set by manager. Rating scale: Below (0.5x), Meeting (1.0x), Above (1.25x), Exceptional (1.5x). Below-Meeting (0x) requires People-team concurrence and is documented.

### 7.2 Spot bonus

- Manager can approve up to **$2,500** per spot bonus per employee, no comp-committee sign-off, up to 2 per employee per year.
- CEO can approve up to **$10,000** per spot bonus.
- Above $10,000 requires comp committee.
- Paid via Rippling on the next payroll cycle.

### 7.3 Retention bonus

- **6-month vesting**, monthly, cash-only.
- **Standard tier:** 15% of base for critical hires flagged by comp committee at Series B or on the trigger of a public-signal-worthy event.
- **Enhanced tier:** 25% of base for individuals flagged as "cannot lose" during any confirmed acquisition process. Requires board approval.
- Retention bonuses are additive to annual bonus, not substitutive.

### 7.4 Payment timing

- Annual bonuses paid in first payroll cycle of March following the plan year.
- Spot bonuses on next payroll cycle after approval.
- Milestone bonuses (e.g., Series A close $50k founder milestone) within 30 days of trigger event.

## 8. Sabbatical + PTO + Benefits Framework

### 8.1 PTO

- **Unlimited discretionary PTO with a 4-week target minimum.** Rippling tracks time-off; if any employee logs <15 days by October 15, the People team schedules a required PTO conversation.
- **Company-wide shutdown weeks:** week between Christmas and New Year, plus the week of July 4. Not counted against PTO target.
- **Sick leave:** separate accrual per California law (24 hours/year mandatory statutory minimum; Hearth policy is unlimited sick leave).

### 8.2 Sabbatical

- **4 weeks paid sabbatical after 5 years continuous service.** Fully paid, benefits continue, no PTO usage.
- **8 weeks paid sabbatical after 10 years.**
- Sabbatical must be taken within 24 months of eligibility or is forfeited.

### 8.3 Retirement

- **401(k) via Rippling (Guideline-integrated).**
- Employer match: **4% match, structured as 200% match on first 2% of employee contribution.**
- Vesting: **immediate on employee contribution; 4-year graded on employer match** (25%/year from grant date).
- Roth option available.

### 8.4 Health, dental, vision

- **Medical: 100% employer-paid for employee, 75% employer-paid for dependents.** Anthem Blue Cross Gold-tier PPO (Rippling PEO plan; calibrated to preserve BOM cost margin while sitting at the top quartile of Ravio's 2026 hardware Series A benefits benchmarks).
- **Dental + vision: 100% employer-paid for employee, 50% for dependents.** Guardian.
- **HSA-compatible high-deductible option** with a $1,500/year employer HSA contribution as alternative.

### 8.5 Life and disability

- **Basic life insurance: 2x base salary, employer-paid,** up to $500k. Portable at separation for 30 days.
- **Voluntary life supplemental:** employee-elected, up to 5x base.
- **Short-term disability: 100% employer-paid,** 60% wage replacement, 90-day duration, integrates with California SDI.
- **Long-term disability: 100% employer-paid,** own-occupation for executives (VP+), any-occupation for the rest, 60% of base to age 65.

### 8.6 Parental leave

- **Primary parent: 16 weeks paid** (100% base, benefits continue).
- **Secondary parent: 8 weeks paid.**
- Adoption / foster: same as biological, dated from placement.
- Return-to-work: 4-week reduced-schedule ramp available.

### 8.7 Learning and development

- **$2,000/year per employee L&D budget** — conferences, courses, books, subscriptions, coaching.
- No pre-approval for spending under $500; manager approval $500–$1,500; VP-Finance (or CEO pre-finance-hire) approval $1,500–$2,000.
- Unused annual budget does not roll over.

### 8.8 Home office / equipment

- $1,500 one-time home office stipend at hire (chair, monitor).
- Laptop refresh every 3 years.
- $75/month home internet reimbursement for fully-remote employees.

### 8.9 Section 8 rollup — benefits cost per employee

Full loaded benefits stack: **~$28k/year per employee (average).** This loads into the Objective 21 headcount cost model at a 22% benefits load on base.

## 9. Founder Secondary Policy

### 9.1 Volume caps

- **Per-event cap:** ≤ 5% of founder's total holdings per single secondary event.
- **Annual cap:** ≤ 10% of holdings per rolling 12-month window.
- **Cumulative pre–Series C:** ≤ **$5M in aggregate proceeds** across all secondaries pre-Series-C.

### 9.2 Approval and process

- Advance board sign-off required at least 30 days before any secondary window.
- **Series A lead investor has right of first refusal** on any founder secondary tranche, at the same price as the tender window.
- Secondary can only be executed via a company-sponsored tender window (no side-deals with individual investors).

### 9.3 Blackout periods

- **12 weeks pre and 4 weeks post** any financing close (Series A, Series B, bridge round, tender offer).
- Blackout also applies for 4 weeks around any material product launch or earnings-equivalent disclosure to investors.

### 9.4 24-month waiting period

- **No founder secondary permitted until 24 months post–Series A close.**

### 9.5 Community property and spousal holdings (California)

Founder is a California resident and any shares held as community property with founder's spouse are treated as founder's holdings for the purposes of every cap in this section. Specifically:

- The 5%-per-event, 10%-per-year, and $5M-cumulative caps apply to the *combined* founder + spouse community-property position, not to the founder's presumed 50% interest alone.
- Any sale by founder's spouse of community-property shares counts against the founder's caps.
- Founder and spouse jointly execute a transfer-restriction agreement at Series A close acknowledging that community-property shares are subject to the company's ROFR, blackout, and lead-VC ROFR provisions.
- A separate-property carveout (any shares acquired by founder pre-marriage or by gift/inheritance) is documented in the founder's spousal-consent letter and is *not* subject to the community-property joinder; separate-property holdings still remain subject to the standard founder-secondary caps under §9.1–9.4.

### 9.6 Tax cooperation

Founder and company will cooperate on tax reporting and treatment of any founder secondary event:

- Company provides founder with a 1099-B (or equivalent) reflecting the tax character of the tender sale within 30 days of settlement.
- Company will not withhold on founder secondary sales unless required by IRS or California FTB rule; withholding, if triggered, is documented in the tender agreement and grossed up only by mutual agreement.
- Any adverse tax reclassification of the tender by the IRS or FTB triggers a good-faith cooperation obligation: company will provide records, respond to information requests, and share counsel where interests align. Company does not indemnify founder for tax outcomes.
- If founder elects a QSBS §1202 exclusion on any portion of the secondary proceeds, company will provide the required §1202 certification letter within 45 days of request, subject to counsel review.

### 9.7 Board-facing rationale

Founder secondary is a retention tool, not a moral hazard. Bounded, board-supervised liquidity ($5M pre–Series C is roughly a paid-off mortgage + college fund + 18-month personal runway) is the cheapest founder-retention purchase the board can make. Our policy is bounded, disclosed to all preferred holders, and subject to lead-VC ROFR.

## 10. Compensation Philosophy Statement

*Signed and issued by Mark Kirk, CEO — to be included in every offer letter and in the employee handbook.*

> **Hearth Compensation Philosophy**
>
> We are building a category-defining product, and how we pay reflects what we believe about the people building it.
>
> **1. We pay at the 50th percentile of Bay Area hardware companies at our stage on cash, with role-adjusted premiums up to the 75th for scarce-supply roles.** For a first-exec CTO, senior firmware engineer working on offline-AI stacks, and (when we hire it) a functional-safety EE, we pay above the median. For every other role, we sit at the median. We don't try to beat FAANG on cash and we won't try. We aim to be the best equity-value bet you can take, cash-competitive enough that the choice isn't painful.
>
> **2. We differentiate on equity.** Executives weight equity-heavy. Engineers weight cash-heavy but still receive a real equity component. Customer-facing leaders weight bonus-heavy. This isn't a fairness statement — it's a legibility statement. We pay in the currency each role optimizes on.
>
> **3. We refresh equity annually.** Retention is not a five-year cliff event at Hearth. We refresh VP+ every year at the year-end board meeting; we refresh senior ICs at 24-month intervals. If you're doing the work, you're being re-vested.
>
> **4. We are transparent about bands.** Every employee can request their comp band from the People team. Manager conversations about promotion and raise are anchored to published band midpoints. We don't negotiate against employees who don't know the number.
>
> — Mark Kirk, CEO, Hearth Inc.
