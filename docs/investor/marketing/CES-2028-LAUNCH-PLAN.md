## 1. Y2 CES strategy — why CES 2028 matters differently than CES 2027

CES 2027 answered "who is Hearth?" The 30×40 booth, the recreation of a $12M great room, the 3-minute hero demo, the Shark Tank narrative fresh in the room — all of that was category-definition work. It moved us from "the guy from the TV show" to "the luxury-AV household-compute category, at $95k, category leader."

CES 2028 answers a different question: **which Hearth is right for you?** Y1 sold one SKU into one country with one voice preset and one household-wide personality. Y2 has an international SKU (230V, German + French regional presets for Q2 2028 launch), a per-family-member personality layer, HomeKit + Matter bridges that let a Crestron or Josh.ai integrator stop asking "does it play nicely" and start asking "which room," and a peer-reference set of ~600 delivered households across Newport Beach, Palm Beach, Aspen, Nashville, Bel Air, Greenwich, Miami, Silicon Valley, and now (Q1 2028) London. The story is no longer "let me explain what we do." It is "let me show you three configurations and introduce you to two customers who bought this last year."

**Category-defense mode, not category-invention mode.** Y1 was a debut. Y2 is where the debut either compounds into a defensible position or dissipates. Every luxury-AV brand that made a Y1 CES splash and failed to reappear in Y2 became a cautionary tale — not because Y1 didn't work but because Y2 didn't happen. Showing up in Y2 with a bigger, better-installed, deeper-referenced footprint is the single most important thing we do at CES 2028.

**Audience mix shifts.** Y1 was press-heavy. Y2 shifts weight to dealers and investors: US dealers who signed in Y1 need territory-expansion conversations; UK/DE/CH/AT/BeNeLux/IT dealers are new to us and need in-market concierge-model walkthroughs. Press cycle stays real but is depth-in-existing-relationships rather than net-new — the Verge/Wired/NYT reporters preserved from Y1 want a Y1-anniversary retrospective ("what happened when 600 households actually got these"), a v1.1 reveal, and a category-scope story ("why $95k AI-in-furniture is now a category, not a curiosity"). Press dinner headcount stays flat; dealer dinner nearly doubles.

**Investor posture shifts.** Y1 was Series B *conversations*. Y2 is Series B *diligence*. Series A closed at $60M post Q1 2027 as planned; Series B lands somewhere in the $150M-$200M pre band around Q2-Q3 2028. CES 2028 falls squarely inside the Series B roadshow window. The 30-investor breakfast on Day 3 is a real diligence event, not a courtship — Series B leads and their partners come expecting revenue numbers and a live product tour, not a pitch deck.

**What we still don't chase.** CES is not where luxury clients themselves show up; the private-client program surface at the booth is for integrator- and interior-designer-referred hand-raises, not direct end-consumer capture. It is not where mass-market brands announce. And it is not where the international dealer program actually closes — CES 2028 opens dealer conversations that ISE Barcelona (February 2028) closes. Sequencing matters: CES is dealer-signature-ready for US, dealer-conversation-ready for international.
