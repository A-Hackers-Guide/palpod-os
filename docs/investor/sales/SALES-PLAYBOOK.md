## 1. Sales Philosophy

Luxury sales is not about closing — it is qualifying, matching to fit, and building a relationship the customer will still be inside five years from now. A $95,000 sphere is not a transaction; it is the beginning of a decade-long service arrangement. Our job on the first call is not to persuade — it is to determine whether this household is a fit. If they are not, we say so. That single act — turning away $95k of revenue when the fit is wrong — is what earns the referral chain that drives our long-term unit economics. A customer gently told "you're not ready for this yet" refers three friends who are; a customer closed against their instincts refunds at day 45 and tells eleven friends the product isn't real. Every no we say early saves us the ten no's the market would say back later.

The concierge team is the sales team AND the retention team — not two departments handing a customer across a wall. The same voice that answered the discovery call on day 0 answers the "my extender lost audio" call on day 400. This is not a staffing convenience; it is the entire moat. Josh.ai and Crestron cannot match it because their model routes every post-sale interaction through a dealer, and dealers optimize for the next install, not the last one. Referral-rate targets: 20% Y1, 30% Y2, 40% Y3. Reps are compensated on retention, not just close, because a customer who cancels in month 4 costs us the same as a customer we never signed — and costs us the next three warm intros besides.

## 2. Ideal Customer Profile

### Primary ICP (target 275 of Y1's 350 DTC units — 79% of DTC volume)
- **Financial:** US household with $5M+ liquid net worth (investable assets excluding primary residence), household income $1M+/yr
- **Geographic:** Primary metros — NYC (Manhattan, Brooklyn Heights, Westchester, Greenwich), San Francisco Bay (SF proper, Marin, Peninsula, Palo Alto, Los Altos, Atherton), Los Angeles (Bel Air, Beverly Hills, Malibu, Pacific Palisades, Brentwood, Hollywood Hills), Miami/Palm Beach (Star Island, Fisher Island, Coral Gables, Palm Beach proper), Boston (Back Bay, Beacon Hill, Wellesley, Weston), Chicago (Gold Coast, Lincoln Park, North Shore)
- **Demographics:** Ages 42–68, married or long-partnered (both principals must be aligned — see Anti-ICP), 60% primary residence + 40% second-home purchases
- **Tech comfort:** Intermediate to advanced — owns and actively uses 3+ smart-home devices, iPhone/iPad household, has a home network with dedicated infrastructure
- **Purchase behavior:** Has purchased $10k+ discretionary tech in last 3 years (Peloton Row, Sonos Arc + Sub + surrounds full setup, McIntosh amp, Kaleidescape Strato, Steinway Spirio)
- **Content:** Has a home library (>500 physical books) OR a personal media collection (>2,000 tracks, >500 films/shows, family photo archive >20 years)
- **Occupancy:** Primary residence occupied >200 days/yr OR second home >90 days/yr (must be a "lived-in" property — Hearth does not fit a rental-property or trophy-empty-house profile)

### Secondary ICP (target 50 of Y1 DTC units — 14%)
VoC-persona-driven — each rep should be able to name and describe these five personas from memory. Note: personas are composites drawn from pre-launch VoC interviews with 40 HNW households and are used for training, not as marketing claims:
- **The Cardiologist** ($3–7M net worth, 55–65, values reliability and privacy above all, spouse makes final decision, referred by friend at country club)
- **The PE Retiree** ($15M+ net worth, 58–70, spent career analyzing operations, will interrogate the technical stack, wants exclusivity and proof it's not "just Alexa"; second-home purchases common)
- **The Tech Founder** ($8M+, 38–52, competent DIY-er in a past life but time-starved now, wants offline story, will trial-run and evangelize if it works)
- **The Gallery Director / Curator / Museum Board Member** ($4–10M, 48–65, aesthetic sensibility, evaluates the physical object as art object, resistant to "gadget" positioning)
- **The Concert Artist / Recording Producer** ($5–20M, 40–70, ear-driven, will A/B the sound system in the discovery call, wants the studio-adjacent experience at home)

### Tertiary ICP (target 25 DTC units, 250 dealer units — the bulk of dealer volume)
- Household with $2–5M liquid, referred by wealth manager or existing dealer relationship
- Configured at $85k price point (dealer-negotiated discount + dealer-included support tier)
- Slower conversion cycle (90 days DTC → 120 days dealer)
- Buy through dealer relationship they've had for years — trust is transferred, not earned first-party

### Anti-ICP — Do NOT sell to
1. **Households where either spouse rejects the "AI in the home" concept.** Pre-launch VoC (n=40) named spouse-veto as the #1 self-reported churn driver on comp-set purchases (Crestron, Kaleidescape, Josh.ai). If one spouse is enthusiastic and the other silent or skeptical, we do not push. Schedule a joint call. Unresolved concern → walk. No exceptions.
2. **Households under $2M liquid net worth.** LTV math does not close — attach, replacement, and referral value all collapse below this threshold. A $95k purchase >5% of liquid net worth is a stressor purchase, and stressor purchases cancel.
3. **Retail-shopping mindset** — "I want to configure it myself, save on install, tune it my way." Wrong ethos for a concierge product. Redirect to Roon + Kaleidescape + Sonos DIY.
4. **Under-18 as primary user.** COPPA exposure (ROADMAP.md non-goals). Redirect "for the kids' movie room" to extender-only or walk.
5. **Unmanaged security concerns** — stalker situation, ongoing legal with data-discovery implications, high-profile principal without existing personal security infrastructure. We are private-by-default, not witness-protection. Refer to security firm first, revisit in 12 months.
6. **Retirement-community / assisted-living** where the principal lacks autonomous purchase authority. Estate + warranty-continuity concerns dominate.

Sales enforcement: every discovery call ends with a HubSpot "fit" checkbox — Primary / Secondary / Tertiary / Anti-ICP: reason. Anti-ICP flag → polite decline template. Head of Sales reviews Anti-ICP flags weekly and revisits financial-reason declines where the customer offered cash upfront.

## 3. DTC Concierge Sequence — 90-Day Conversion

### Day 0 — Inbound
- **Source:** Website inquiry form, referral from existing customer, referral from wealth manager, dealer overflow (dealer had inquiry outside their metro), press-response inbound
- **Response SLA:** 4 hours business, 12 hours weekend. Every inbound gets a same-business-day text-confirmation within 30 minutes ("Thanks for reaching out — a concierge advisor will call you within 4 hours").
- **Owner:** Regional Sales Rep for the metro (round-robin if metro-ambiguous). Head of Sales personally handles inbounds from named referral sources (existing customer name, board member intro, Shark Tank clip response).
- **Discovery call structure — 15 minutes, non-negotiable format:**
  1. **1 min — Warm open.** Thank them, confirm they have 15 minutes, name the referral source if any.
  2. **3 min — Their story.** "Tell me about your home and what got you interested." Listen for ICP signals: property type, occupancy, tech stack, spouse alignment, purchase trigger (new home? renovation? gift?).
  3. **4 min — Their pain.** "What's not working today?" Listen for: fragmented systems, privacy concerns, hate-cloud sentiment, dealer disappointment, content-loss anxiety (photo archives, ripped CD libraries).
  4. **4 min — Fit-first framing.** "Here's what Hearth is and isn't. It might be a fit; it might not. Let me tell you both." Deliver 60-second product frame + 60-second anti-frame ("not for you if…").
  5. **2 min — Qualifying questions.** "Who else is part of this decision? What's your timeframe? Have you looked at Josh.ai or Crestron?"
  6. **1 min — Close the call.** "I'll send a short followup with three next-step options: schedule a Fremont visit, schedule a home site-survey call, or a video tour of a customer install."

### Day 3 — Fork
- **Strong fit + strong intent:** Deposit prompt. $10,000 refundable deposit locks metro delivery slot. Email + call combo. Deposit link points to Stripe-hosted secure page.
- **Fit but not yet ready:** 30-day nurture sequence begins. Weeks 1, 3, and 5 touches (never more; luxury audience is spam-averse). Content: customer story, competitive teardown one-pager, offline-first white paper.
- **Not a fit:** Decline email + suggested alternatives + door-open-for-later ("timelines change; we'd welcome a followup in 12 months").

### Day 7 — Followup call
- 20 minutes, focused on unanswered questions from Day 0. Bring in Sales Engineer if any technical question surfaced. Spouse should be on this call if not on Day 0.

### Day 14 — Site survey scheduled
- Only occurs post-deposit. Site survey is on-site (regional rep + installer partner) or Matterport-scan-plus-video-call for out-of-metro or high-privacy customers. Duration: 90 minutes on-site, 45 minutes remote.

### Day 21–45 — Site survey completed + install scheduled
- Deliverable to customer: written install plan with room-by-room layout, wiring plan, extender count recommendation, timeline. Customer signs plan before we build.
- Build begins in Fremont on plan-sign-off.

### Day 45–60 — Install day
- White-glove: 2-person install team, arrives via unmarked van, single-day install, 6-hour typical. Customer walkthrough at end. Concierge advisor present or on video for the walkthrough — first "handoff" from sales to concierge happens live.

### Day 90 — NPS + extender upsell
- 30-min video check-in. NPS score captured (target 4.6+). Second-room extender conversation begins — targeted attach rate 40% by month 6, 65% by year 2.
- Referral ask happens here, not earlier. Rule: never ask for referral before month 3. Ask twice per year after, not more.

### Conversion targets — base case + sensitivity

Base case is what we staff and underwrite the P&L to. Sensitivity low end is stress-case (no Shark Tank tailwind, softer inbound quality). Prior playbook set discovery→deposit at 25%; comparable luxury inbound at $30–60k (Peloton Row, Steinway Spirio, McIntosh + Sonus Faber) closes 10–15% of qualified. At $95k with a mandatory spouse-alignment gate, 15% is the honest base case; 20% is achievable with a strong Shark Tank clip in market; 25% is the accelerator target, not the plan.

| Stage | Base case | Sensitivity band | Threshold for review |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website inquiry → Discovery call | 40% | 32–45% | Below 32% triggers inbound-quality review |
| Discovery call → Deposit | **15%** | 15 / 20 / 25% | Below 12% triggers rep-coaching + inbound requal |
| Deposit → Delivery | 92% | 88–95% | Below 88% triggers post-mortem on cancellations |
| Delivery → 1-year NPS 4.6+ | 78% score 9–10 | 70–82% | Below 70% triggers product-experience review |
| Delivery → Y2 extender attach | 40% | 30–50% | Below 30% triggers upsell-script review |
| Delivery → referral within 12 mo | 22% | 18–30% | Below 18% triggers concierge-touchpoint audit |

### Inquiry-volume dependency on Shark Tank

At base case (15% × 40% × 92%), delivering 350 DTC units requires ~6,300 qualified inquiries in Y1. That volume needs the Shark Tank tailwind (comparable episodes drove 3,000–8,000 web inquiries in the 72-hour post-air window alone). **Explicit dependency:** without the episode driving a meaningful spike, organic Y1 inquiries land near ~3,500, and we either (a) double marketing spend to buy the missing volume, roughly doubling CAC, or (b) revise Y1 delivery down to ~180–200 DTC units. The plan does not assume a graceful failure mode; it names the dependency.

## 4. DTC Sales Team Structure

### Headcount + roles (Y1) — sized to 350 DTC units

- **Head of Sales (1)** — Sets strategy, owns dealer relationships at C-level, personally handles the top 6% of leads (highest LTV, most complex, referral-critical), owns quarterly business reviews. Personal DTC quota: 20 units.
- **Sales Engineer (1)** — Technical qualification, competitive teardown deep-dives, site-survey plan review, dealer training co-lead. Supports 120 "SE-led" deals routed through inside-sales / dealer overflow / out-of-metro remote flow (see quota table §10).
- **Regional Sales Reps (2)**
  - **East Coast Rep** — NYC + Boston + Miami + Palm Beach (~$400k travel budget). Quota: 90 DTC units.
  - **West Coast Rep** — SF Bay + LA + Beverly Hills (densest revenue region). Quota: 120 DTC units.

The 2-rep footprint is deliberate. Prior draft had 3 regional reps with a "Central + South" territory of 5 metros. We consolidate those under dealer-led sourcing + SE + concierge support — we justify a full-time rep only in metros generating ≥60 DTC-direct units/yr, and Central/South are dealer-first Y1. If Central/South dealer performance underdelivers by end of Q2 2027, we add a third regional rep (~$400k Y1 opex, Head of Sales + CEO decision).

### Concierge overlap — pre-deposit is sales, post-deposit is concierge

The concierge team (4 advisors, separate P&L) owns the customer from deposit through 5-year warranty. Sales rep remains "shadow-CC'd" on concierge touchpoints for 12 months so relationship warmth carries through. Sales rep re-owns the customer at month 12 for referral cycle + extender upsell reactivation. Where the concierge team receives a pre-deposit inbound that the regional rep cannot reach (weekend, travel, load-balancing), the concierge advisor executes the discovery call under Sales Engineer supervision and the deal counts against the SE-led quota bucket.

### Territory management
- **CRM:** HubSpot Enterprise (~$150k/yr all-in with Sales Hub Enterprise + Marketing Hub Enterprise + integrations). Salesforce was evaluated; HubSpot's marketing-sales bridge is stronger for our SKU volume. Full pipeline visibility across reps + dealer channel.

- **Nurture cadence:** 30-day intervals for 6 months, then quarterly. All nurture ends at 24 months if no re-engagement.
- **Lead scoring:** ICP-fit (0–40 points) + intent (0–30 points) + timeline (0–15 points) + referral-source (0–15 points). 70+ is a "hot lead." Below 40 is nurture-only.

## 5. Dealer Channel Structure

Dealer program modeled on Josh.ai (rep program), Crestron (technical certification), and Kaleidescape (exclusivity + luxury positioning). Y1 sizing was reset: established CI dealers do not commit $250k stocking to a Y0 luxury product with zero shipped-and-served units in-market. Y1 is smaller and less capital-intensive; the "30-dealer" number is earned across Y2 as Certified conversions layer in.

### Dealer classes

#### Founding Dealer (Y1, 15–20 targeted — reset from 30)
- **Exclusivity:** Metro-exclusive Y1 (renewable annually based on volume + NPS)
- **Stocking commitment:** **$150k minimum** inventory ($97,500 dealer cost) — approx. 1 sphere + 2 extenders held in showroom or on-hand for demo/install. Reduced from prior $250k floor: Y0 CI product without shipped reference installs realistically clears at $150k; $250k is a Y2 Certified-tier commitment.
- **Margin:** 35% dealer margin (customer pays list, dealer keeps 35%, Hearth ships direct-from-Fremont on order)
- **Payment terms:** Net 90 on inventory, Net 30 on order-through units
- **Support included:** 3 years white-glove concierge shared with Hearth (dealer handles first-touch, Hearth handles escalation)
- **Marketing:** Co-branded materials (limited to templates, no free-form dealer creative on brand), quarterly dealer-name-included customer case studies with mutual approval
- **Program fee:** $35,000 one-time (reduced from $50k to reflect stocking-floor reset — goes toward Fremont training + first showroom demo unit at 50% off list)
- **Dealer agreement:** Cooley-drafted 22-page master agreement with named exit terms — 90-day termination notice from either side, brand-integrity clauses, dealer non-solicitation of Hearth staff, buyout-of-inventory clause on Hearth-initiated termination
- **Volume floor:** 6 units Y1 to renew Founding Dealer status (reset from 8); below 3 units and dealer downshifts to Certified

#### Certified Dealer (Y2+, non-exclusive)
- Stocking commitment: $100k ($65k dealer cost)
- Margin: 30%
- Payment terms: Net 60 inventory, Net 30 order-through
- Support: 2 years shared support
- Program fee: $20k
- Volume floor: 4 units/yr

#### Referral Partner (no stocking obligation, any metro)
- Referral commission: 10% of net revenue on customer they source, paid on delivery
- No inventory, no exclusivity, no marketing rights
- Use case: wealth managers, interior designers, architects, luxury real-estate teams
- Target: 100 referral partners by end of Y1

## 6. Dealer Training Program

### Initial certification — 3-day Fremont visit
- **Day 1 — Product deep dive.** Factory floor tour, unbox-to-power-on demo, hardware architecture walkthrough with founder + engineering lead, competitive teardown session (Josh.ai + Crestron + Kaleidescape units side-by-side in demo lab)
- **Day 2 — Install training.** Hands-on install of both sphere and extender in mock room. Wiring, network, extender pairing, common installation edge cases (plaster walls, radiant floors, no-ethernet homes). Certification exam prep.
- **Day 3 — Sales training.** Discovery call role-play, objection handling (all 25 SHARK-TANK-REHEARSAL.md objections), site-survey walkthrough, dealer-portal training. Founder joins for closing dinner + "why we're doing this" narrative session.

16 Y1 dealers × cohorts of 4 = 4 sessions across Feb–Aug 2027 — a manageable Fremont cadence. Prior 30-dealer plan required 8 cohorts and consumed founder + engineering time.

### Ongoing training
- Quarterly 1-day training (rotating: 3 in-person at Fremont, 1 virtual), covering new features, seasonal sales themes, and refreshed competitive intel
- Weekly office-hours video call with Sales Engineer (voluntary, well-attended)
- Slack Connect channel with Hearth engineering for real-time install issues

### Certification requirements
- Pass 20-question technical exam (80% pass; three-attempt cap; failure resets to Referral Partner tier)
- Complete one supervised install (Hearth SE on-site or on-video)
- Sign Dealer Code of Conduct addendum (brand-integrity, customer-privacy, no-substitute-selling clauses)

### Dealer portal features
- Real-time inventory (Fremont on-hand + dealer-held)
- Order management with delivery tracking
- Service ticket system routed to Hearth concierge
- Customer NPS reporting on their delivered units
- Marketing asset library (approved templates only)
- Commission statements + payment history
- Referral-partner sub-portal for their downstream referrers

## 8. Objection Handling in the Sales Flow

Each of the 25 objections in SHARK-TANK-REHEARSAL.md was framed for the founder facing Sharks. Sales reps face customers with softer, more personal variants. Reps memorize the 10 below and can improvise the remaining 15.

**Rule on customer stories:** At playbook date (August 2026), Hearth has zero delivered production units. Every customer story below is either (a) an anonymized composite drawn from pre-launch VoC research with 40 HNW households, explicitly labeled "illustrative composite," or (b) an anonymized real testimonial from a named pilot participant, labeled "pilot testimonial." Reps do NOT tell composite stories as if they are real customers. When we accrue real installed testimonials, they replace composites one-for-one.

### Objection 1 — "Is this really $95k?"
- **Rep reply:** "Yes. For context, households evaluating this category typically quote a Crestron + Josh.ai + Kaleidescape build at around $280,000 across five years. Hearth is roughly a third of that all-in, and it's one bill instead of five."
- **Follow-up:** "Can I show you the 5-year comparison — it's a single page?"
- **Illustrative composite (pre-launch VoC):** A Bay Area PE partner interviewed pre-launch had three integrator quotes on the table. His strongest signal on the Hearth architecture was that "the number in the proposal wouldn't grow between the proposal and the invoice." We're underwriting the Hearth quote to match that behavior — flat pricing, no change-order arithmetic.

### Objection 2 — "How does this beat my Crestron?"
- **Rep reply:** "Crestron is best-in-class if you have a full-time integrator on retainer. If you don't — or if you've fired one and don't want another — Hearth is what happens when you take the good parts of Crestron and put them in a system that doesn't need a specialist to work."
- **Follow-up:** "Tell me about your last three service calls with your Crestron integrator."
- **Illustrative composite:** A Manhattan cardiologist interviewed pre-launch had spent $340k on a Crestron install in 2019. By 2023 his integrator had been acquired twice and the system was, in his words, "guessing at what he wanted." He said he'd replace the entire stack with a single-vendor system if the guarantees held. Hearth's swap program and 3-year included concierge are the guarantees.

### Objection 3 — "What about privacy?"
- **Rep reply:** "Everything runs on the sphere in your home. Nothing goes to the cloud. No Anthropic, no Google, no Amazon — the model runs on-device. If your internet goes out today, Hearth still works. That's the design, not a marketing line."
- **Follow-up:** "Have you had a moment recently when a cloud outage disrupted your home?"
- **Illustrative composite:** A Palm Beach tech founder had walked away from three "smart home" evaluations because none could answer "where does the audio of my kids' voices go?" A packet-capture walkthrough of a demo Hearth — zero outbound in normal operation — landed the pilot-list ask.

### Objection 4 — "My spouse says no."
- **Rep reply:** "That's the most important information you could have shared. In our pre-launch research with 40 HNW households, spouse-veto was the #1 self-reported cancellation driver on comparable purchases — Crestron and Kaleidescape included. Let's not put you in that position. Can we set up a call with both of you — no pressure — and if it's still a no, it's a no?"
- **Follow-up:** "What's driving the concern? Is it the price, the AI, the disruption of installing it, or something else?"
- **Illustrative composite:** A Greenwich couple: she chaired a museum board, he was ready to buy. She flagged "AI in the walls" as invasive. What shifted her posture in the interview was walking through the offline architecture — voice never leaves the sphere. The Fremont spouse-visit protocol is built from that conversation.

### Objection 5 — "What if it breaks?"
- **Rep reply:** "White-glove 3-year support is included, and we run a swap program — a replacement sphere is at your door within 48 hours if anything ever fails. Our pre-production stress-test protocol modeled 5-year MTBF at 47,000 hours (documented in the engineering reliability analysis in the data room), and every unit ships from Fremont after burn-in that exercises the full thermal envelope."
- **Follow-up:** "What's your current backup plan when your existing home tech fails?"
- **Illustrative composite:** In concierge-model design, we walked a Chicago retiree through the swap flow. Her feedback drove the 48-hour SLA — 72 hours read to her as "the old dealer story"; 48 was the number that would make her refer her sister.

### Objection 6 — "How does it install?"
- **Rep reply:** "One day, two of our people, mostly your existing wiring. If you have Cat6 to the rooms you want extenders in, we're fully non-disruptive. If you don't, we work with your GC or ours, and it's still a one-day install at the end of the prep."
- **Follow-up:** "Is there a renovation on your calendar we should coordinate around, or are we working with the home as-is?"
- **Illustrative composite:** Los Altos households in the VoC set had scheduled comparable Crestron replacements over full weekends. Their pain wasn't the install — it was the second and third tuning visit. Hearth's site-survey protocol commits to a single install day because that was the most emphatic VoC ask.

### Objection 7 — "What about upgrades? Won't this be obsolete in three years?"
- **Rep reply:** "The sphere is designed as a 10-year asset. Firmware and model upgrades ship monthly and are free for the life of the device. When we release next-generation hardware, existing customers get a trade-in credit — we don't want to sell you obsolescence, we want to sell you a decade of use."
- **Follow-up:** "How long do you expect to be in this house?"
- **Illustrative composite:** A pre-launch interviewee: "I don't buy anything that will feel old in five years." Our answer was the firmware roadmap + trade-in policy + customer advisory board reviewing the roadmap quarterly. He asked to be on the board.

### Objection 8 — "Can we try before we buy?"
- **Rep reply:** "Yes, two ways. Option A — visit a Founding Dealer showroom or a customer host home in your metro; we facilitate. Option B — 30-day home trial extender in your primary room, ships back at our cost if you don't move forward. Trial conversion is modeled at 55–65% from comparable-luxury home-audio trial programs; actuals will update this."
- **Follow-up:** "Would you rather see it in a fully-lived-in home or in a controlled demo environment?"
- **Illustrative composite:** A Boston-area interviewee committed to a full-system evaluation only conditional on a bedroom-extender trial for a month first. The trial-extender program exists because of that conversation.

### Objection 9 — "Is it truly offline? Really?"
- **Rep reply:** "You can unplug your router right now with a Hearth in your home and every feature except the software-update pull continues to work. Voice, media, photos, controls, model conversation — all local. The one thing that touches the internet is optional software updates, and you approve every one."
- **Follow-up:** "Would it be useful for me to show you the network trace from a demo Hearth?"
- **Illustrative composite:** A Miami-based recording producer had lost sessions to three cloud outages during Verizon incidents: "will your box work when Verizon is having opinions?" The offline architecture is the direct answer.

### Objection 10 — "What's your track record? Is this company going to be around in five years?"
- **Rep reply:** "Fair question. We've closed a Series A, we're revenue-positive on unit economics from day one, and our founder built and shipped the pilot units personally. We also underwrite the concern — every unit ships with a source-code escrow arrangement: if Hearth ever ceased operations, existing customers have a documented path to keep their units running, including firmware maintenance rights. That's in the customer agreement."
- **Follow-up:** "What would give you the confidence you'd need — investor list, unit shipments to date, or something else?"
- **Illustrative composite:** A PE-retiree interviewee spent an hour quizzing the founder on burn rate, gross margin, and cap table. The source-code escrow clause exists because his question — "what happens to my $95k box if you go under?" — had no good published answer from any competitor.

## 10. Sales Compensation Nuances

### Regional Rep commission structure
- 40% of variable pool on personally-closed DTC deals in their metro
- 15% on dealer-referred deals in their metro (rep supported the dealer closing)
- 5% on team-shared deals (referred from another rep's metro or handed to SE-led)
- Accelerator: 1.25x multiplier for closes above quota, 1.5x multiplier for closes above 130% of quota

### Sales Engineer commission
- 10% team commission on all DTC closes (proportional to time invested per HubSpot activity logging)
- Direct-attribution commission on SE-led deals: 25% of variable pool on the SE-led 120-unit quota bucket
- Quarterly bonus of $15,000 for team above 90% of quota, $30,000 for team above 110%

### Dealer bonuses (paid to dealer, on top of margin)
- 5% bonus at $500k dealer volume in a calendar year
- 10% bonus at $1M dealer volume
- Bonus paid Q1 of following year, one-time cash

### Clawback (critical for retention alignment)
- 100% commission returned if customer cancels within 30 days of delivery
- 50% commission returned if customer cancels within 30–60 days
- 25% commission returned if customer cancels within 60–90 days
- Zero clawback after 90 days
- Reason: prevents rep from pushing an Anti-ICP customer over the line to hit quota

### Retention SPIF
- $2,500 bonus to rep per customer who scores 4.6+ NPS at month 12
- $1,000 bonus per customer who refers a delivered new customer within 12 months
- $500 bonus per customer who purchases second extender within 12 months
- Paid quarterly, uncapped

## Appendix B — Objection-Handling Rules of Engagement

- Never disparage a competitor by name in a customer conversation. Facts, not sneers.
- Never promise a feature that isn't shipped. Never.
- Never negotiate price below list on DTC channel. Ever. (Dealers negotiate; DTC is fixed. This is a brand-integrity rule, not a policy rule — violating it means losing dealer trust the next week.)
- Never sell above the ICP fit. If you're not sure it's a fit, decline and route to nurture.
- Never tell a customer story as if it is a real installed customer when it is a pre-launch VoC composite. Composites are labeled "illustrative composite"; pilot testimonials are labeled "pilot testimonial." When we accrue real installed testimonials post-launch, they replace composites one-for-one and are labeled with metro + install month, name withheld.
- Always CC the concierge team when a customer moves to deposit. Handoff is a live warm handoff, not a form.
