## 1. Program philosophy

Referrals are the highest-quality demand channel for luxury products. This is not a claim. It is arithmetic. At Hearth's $95k ASP with a 45-day sales cycle and a mandatory spouse-alignment gate, a referred inquiry arrives already predisposed: the referred household has heard the price, seen the sphere in use, watched their friend's daughter interact with the face, absorbed the privacy story from someone who has lived with it. Discovery → deposit conversion at 15% is the base case for cold inbound (see `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §3). For referred inbound the hypothesized conversion is 30%+, a 2x multiplier. That is why referrals compound LTV and drop blended CAC by ~$2,000 for every 10 points of referral-rate improvement (see `FINANCIAL-MODEL-SENSITIVITY.md` §6, ρ = −0.45 between CAC and referral rate).

But the reason to build the program well is not the conversion number. It is a posture question, and the posture question is the whole thing.

At Hearth's price band, a $10,000 referral gift certificate makes the transaction feel transactional. It says: your friend told us about you because we paid them. The referred household hears that. The referring household hears it more. The friendship gets a slight film of commerce on it. That is a mechanism that destroys the very asset — social trust inside private-wealth circles — that is doing all the work.

A $500 concierge gift, on the other hand, makes the referrer feel gratuitous. It says: your friend told us about you because they like us. We noticed. We would like to thank them, but we would never presume to compensate them. The referred household hears that too. It reads as respect for the relationship rather than a commission on it.

The right mechanic depends on the household's own dignity and relationship posture. Some households will decline a personal gift and prefer a charitable donation in their name to an institution they support. Some will decline anything at all and simply expect that Hearth remember them well. A very small number will want the credit against their own service invoice because they have a family-office CFO who books such things line-by-line. The program has to accommodate all three postures without embarrassing any of them.

**The organizing principle:** at Hearth's price band, referrals are *not* about incentivizing referrals. They are about respecting them. The mechanics that follow reflect that principle. They are also — because we are underwriting this to LPs — mechanics that compound honestly against the LTV build published in `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §9 line 375.

## 2. Program structure — dual-sided benefits

The program provides distinct benefits to the referrer household and to the referred household. Both sides are calibrated to Hearth's brand posture and to the tax and legal ceilings identified in §5.

### 2.1 Referrer benefits — tiered by cumulative referrals per calendar year

Each tier is a menu, not a mandate. The referring household picks the form that fits how they want the relationship to feel. The concierge presents the menu at the appropriate moment (§3 Step 6, not before).

| Cumulative referrals in a calendar year | Benefit menu (referring household picks one) |
|---|---|
| **Referral #1** | (a) $1,000 credit against next Hearth service invoice; OR (b) $1,000 charitable donation to a 501(c)(3) of the referrer's choice, made in the referring household's name; OR (c) $1,000 **discount on the referrer's own next concierge renewal** (reduces the $1,899/yr Y4+ renewal invoice to $899 — structurally a price reduction on the referrer's own future purchase per Rev. Rul. 76-96 line of reasoning, NOT a service-for-action grant). Prior draft framed option (c) as "1 additional year of concierge extension" which is structurally compensation (service given in exchange for a referral action); reframed here as a discount on the referrer's own renewal so it clears IRS price-reduction posture per Cooley memo (Q1 2027 target, moved forward from Q3 2027 to close the 90-day margin before program launch). |
| **Referrals #2 – #3** | Same three-option menu, cumulative — each additional referral adds another $1,000 credit / donation / concierge year |
| **Referral #4+** | Tier bump: (a) $2,000 credit against next Hearth service invoice; OR (b) $2,000 charitable donation in the referrer's name; OR (c) additional concierge year *plus* a custom letterpress print commissioned for the household (Y2 print series, curated by Hearth's brand studio — see `docs/investor/brand/` reference materials) |
| **10-referral lifetime milestone** | (i) Dedicated concierge dinner at the household's residence or a location of their choosing, hosted by Hearth's CEO and Head of Customer Experience; (ii) a custom household portrait commissioned from a Hearth-curated portrait photographer (or, if preferred, a painted portrait from a shortlist of three artists in the Hearth atelier program). Lifetime, not per-year. |

Design notes on the tier structure. The $1,000 anchor is deliberately below the IRS de minimis fringe benefit ceiling ($600 threshold triggers 1099-NEC obligations, but a credit against future Hearth services is a price reduction rather than compensation — see §5). The $2,000 step-up at four referrals recognizes materially productive referrers without introducing a per-referral compensation curve that reads as commission. The 10-lifetime milestone is scarce by construction (in Y5 base case, we project 30–50 households at or above this threshold system-wide) and is intended to convert Hearth's most productive referrers into brand-atelier members whose social presence anchors the community.

Referrer benefits activate on delivery confirmation (Step 6 of §3), not on deposit. This matters — a deposit that later cancels does not credit the referrer, which prevents dispute cascades if a referred household withdraws during site survey.

### 2.2 Referred customer benefits

The referred customer receives three concrete, non-cash benefits designed to signal that being introduced by an existing household places them in a different service tier than cold inbound:

- **Priority delivery.** 2 weeks from deposit to install for referred households, versus 4–6 weeks standard delivery per `ONBOARDING-PLAYBOOK.md` Phase 1. Operationally, this is achieved by reserving 15% of monthly Sanmina production allocation for a priority queue and by scheduling the site survey within 5 days of deposit rather than the standard 7 days.
- **Concierge match bonus.** If requested, the referred household is assigned to the same concierge advisor as the referring household. This is the highest-value operational benefit — it means the concierge already understands the friendship's context, the referring family's household patterns, and any shared travel calendar. The concierge takes on this second household inside their existing 50-household book (concierge caps are elastic to 55 in this case, per `ONBOARDING-PLAYBOOK.md` §7 staffing model).
- **First-year experience package.** A Hearth gift package arrives at the referred household within the first week of install. Contents: a leather-bound Household Guide notebook (custom monogrammed with the household name), a set of Hearth-branded correspondence cards, and a hand-written welcome note from the CEO. Loaded cost per package: ~$285. Not sold. Not on the website.

Neither referrer nor referred benefits are advertised on the Hearth website beyond a single line on the `/household` page (§9). A referred household is told about the priority delivery and gift package by the concierge during the discovery call, at which point it functions as a small ceremonial recognition that they came in via a trusted introduction.

## 3. Referral flow — from mention to sale

The referral flow is deliberately hand-managed. There is no self-serve portal. No referral link with a UTM. No trackable code. The mechanism is: the referring household mentions Hearth to a friend, the friend calls the concierge, and the concierge asks — carefully — who told them.

Explicit sequence:

1. **Referrer household mentions Hearth to friend.** Usually organic, most often at a dinner, an install-visit walk-through, or a house tour. Occasionally the concierge nudges the referrer at the Day 90 or 12-month touch (see `ONBOARDING-PLAYBOOK.md` Day +90 and `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §3 Day 90 line 85: "never ask for referral before month 3. Ask twice per year after, not more.").
2. **Friend visits `hearth.co` OR calls the concierge line.** The website inquiry form asks "How did you hear about us?" as an optional field. The concierge line always asks it in the first 3 minutes of the call.
3. **Optional: referrer sends direct referral email.** For households that prefer it, the concierge provides a template referral email the referrer can send to the friend directly, CC'ing the concierge. The template is short — three paragraphs — and does not mention the benefit menu on the referrer side.
4. **Discovery call scheduled with referred household.** Within 24 hours of the inbound. Referred households are routed to the Head of Sales or the assigned Regional Rep for the metro; SE-led routing (see `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §4) is only used if geography forces it. If the referrer's concierge advisor is expected to become the referred household's concierge (§2.2 concierge match), the concierge sits in on the discovery call in a listening role.
5. **Deposit paid.** The concierge places a verification call to the referring household within 48 hours of the deposit, confirming that they in fact referred the friend and that the referral is real (not a manufactured claim from the referred side). The verification call is not a formality — see §4 attribution.
6. **Delivery confirmation → referrer benefits activate.** On install-day + 7 days (post-install NPS confirmation window, per `ONBOARDING-PLAYBOOK.md` Day +7), the concierge contacts the referring household to present the tier-1 (or applicable tier) benefit menu. Referrer picks a benefit form; concierge processes it within 14 days.
7. **30-day post-install follow-up with both households.** The concierge makes a joint call — with prior consent from both — to check in and to signal that both households now form a small Hearth community. Optional and often declined by the more private end of the customer base. When accepted, it is a strong retention signal on both sides.

Time from friend's first inquiry to referrer benefits paid: typically 90–110 days (28-day discovery-to-deposit cycle plus 2-week priority install plus 7-day post-install window). The referrer is *not* thanked for the referral prior to Step 5; premature thanks reads as commercial.

## 4. Attribution + tracking

Attribution is verified, not asserted. The mechanism:

- **Inquiry-form field.** The `hearth.co` inquiry form and the concierge line intake both capture "referring household name" as an optional field. When present, the concierge treats the field as a claim, not a confirmation.
- **Verification call.** Within 48 hours of the referred household's inquiry, the concierge calls the referring household on file and confirms intent: "We spoke with the Chen family last night — they mentioned you shared the Hearth story with them. Wanted to confirm that on your end." Two outcomes: confirmed (referrer acknowledged the introduction, attribution stands) or unconfirmed (referrer did not intend to make an introduction — attribution voided; concierge may still service the referred household but the referrer earns no benefit).
- **System of record: HubSpot.** Attribution recorded on the referred household's contact record with a required "Referring Household ID" foreign key. Migration to Salesforce contemplated at Y2 scale (see `KPI-DASHBOARD-FRAMEWORK.md` §D3 tooling stack). HubSpot workflow auto-flags a referred household from lead through deposit through delivery, and triggers the Step 6 benefit menu email to the concierge on install-day + 7.
- **Attribution window.** Referrer is credited for referred households that pay a deposit within **12 months of the referral event** (defined as the first documented mention captured on either side). A friend-of-a-friend chain — Referrer A introduces B to Hearth, B declines but talks to C two years later — attributes to whoever most recently reintroduced Hearth to the closing household. This is a policy judgment: the household that reactivated the interest gets credit. Multi-referrer attribution splits are not supported; only one referrer credited per closing household.
- **Dispute resolution.** Any dispute — competing attribution claims, referrer who feels uncredited, referred household who claims the referral field was miscoded — is escalated to the Head of Customer Experience for goodwill review within 5 business days. Standing policy is to resolve ambiguous cases in favor of the referring household. Documented ledger of dispute outcomes maintained by the concierge ops lead; quarterly review by Head of Growth.

Attribution is not treated as a bounty system. The concierge is not incentivized on referral volume. The Regional Rep receives 5% team-shared commission on referred deals per `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §10 line 407, which is the lowest bucket in the compensation structure and specifically calibrated so that reps do not compete for referral attribution.

## 5. Legal + tax posture

Every mechanic in this program has been calibrated to remain inside the safest legal envelope available. The default position at every ambiguity is to structure the benefit as a price reduction on Hearth services rather than as compensation. This section is written to the legal review standard, and a full memo from Cooley LLP Employment Practice and Wilson Sonsini corporate counsel is filed in the compliance data room prior to program launch.

- **Referral credit against future Hearth service invoice.** Treated as a discount on future service (concierge subscription, warranty renewal, upgrade cycle), which is a price reduction, not a payment. No 1099 issued to the referrer, no W-9 collected, no compensation reported. Cooley confirmed treatment on 2026-Q2 memo.
- **Concierge extension form.** Same posture — it is a service credit against a future subscription line, not a cash equivalent. Fair value ($1,899/yr subscription retail) documented on the household portal but not reportable to the referrer as income.
- **Charitable donation form.** Hearth writes the check directly to the 501(c)(3) named by the referrer. The referrer does not touch the funds. The donation is Hearth's charitable contribution, not the referrer's; the referrer does not receive a tax-deductible gift receipt. If the referrer wishes the donation to appear on their own return, they may accept the credit form and make the donation themselves; this is disclosed on the benefit menu at Step 6.
- **Aggregate ceiling per referrer per calendar year.** The three-tier structure caps a single referrer at 5 referrals per year (§10 anti-manipulation controls) and thus at a maximum aggregate benefit of ~$8,000/year (one referral at $1k + two at $1k + two at $2k = $7k, or the same in donation form, or the same in concierge-year fair value). Above the $600 IRS 1099-NEC threshold for cash payments, but structurally not cash — all forms are either service credits or third-party donations. **If aggregate cash-equivalent value in any calendar year exceeds $600 per individual referrer** (e.g., a household that accepts service credits later requests a partial cash refund), Hearth collects a W-9 before processing and files a 1099-NEC per IRS requirement. Concierge tracks aggregate value per referring household in HubSpot with an alert threshold at $500 cumulative.
- **Household staff referrals.** All staff-initiated referrals flow through the household employer per `HOUSEHOLD-STAFF-KIT.md` §11.2. Hearth pays a $2,500 referral bonus to the *referring household* (not the individual staff member), only after written consent from the principal employer is on file. This mechanism was chosen specifically to avoid joint-employer exposure under the Fair Labor Standards Act, IRS 1099-NEC complications on domestic-worker payments, and violations of household-employer employment agreements that commonly prohibit outside compensation. The compliance review of this mechanic was completed by Cooley Employment Practice in 2026-Q2; see `HOUSEHOLD-STAFF-KIT.md` §11.6 for coordination protocol.
- **International jurisdictions.** No referral benefit is issued outside the US in Y1. In Y2+ international rollout (§13), the referral benefit is issued locally through the in-market dealer, denominated in local currency, and subject to local tax opinions coordinated by Cooley's international correspondent network. Middle East markets, per §13, will not use cash-equivalent benefits — donation or service-credit forms only.

Program launch is gated on legal review sign-off from both firms. Draft memos in the compliance data room; final counsel sign-off targeted for Q3 2027 (60 days before soft launch per §12).

## 6. Program governance

The program is a live system that adjusts to observed data. Governance rhythm:

- **Monthly referral pipeline review.** Head of Growth chairs; Head of Customer Success, Head of Sales, and concierge ops lead attend. Reviews: verified referrals in the month, benefit forms selected by referrers, benefit disbursement backlog, any attribution disputes. 30 minutes.
- **Quarterly effectiveness analysis.** Cost-per-acquisition via referral vs. DTC vs. dealer channel, computed against fully-loaded S&M opex per `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §9 CAC build. Referral CAC is expected to be materially lower than DTC ($18k Y1) because the marginal cost is the loaded value of the referrer benefit plus incremental concierge time; the goal is to demonstrate that a referred delivery lands at ~$3,500 CAC by Y2 and ~$2,500 by Y3, consistent with the −0.45 CAC × referral rate correlation in the sensitivity model. Reported to Head of Growth and CFO; input to board packet §E.
- **Semi-annual program refresh.** Head of Growth + Head of Customer Success review the tier thresholds, benefit forms, and any friction points reported by concierges. Refresh decisions require CEO sign-off; substantive tier changes require Board of Directors notification.
- **Board packet §E.** Referral rate by cohort, referral CAC by channel, referral-derived pipeline value, and any change to the assumption stack are reported to the board each quarter as part of the standard KPI packet.

## 7. Referral rates by cohort

The program has a graded ramp — Y1 is a controlled test, Y2 is scale learning, Y3+ is the mature system underwritten in the LTV build.

| Cohort | Delivered units (cumulative or annual) | Referral rate target | Absolute referrals target | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Y1 (200 GA units delivered)** | 200 units | 15% baseline, 20% stretch | **30 verified referrals** (per user brief) | Controlled test cohort. Small-N; program mechanics validated, benefit-form preferences captured, attribution workflow debugged. |
| **Y2 (1,900 units annual)** | ~1,900 units in Y2 | 25% blended (mid-Y2 acceleration) | ~200 verified referrals in Y2 | Scale test. Formal program governance in place. First charitable-donation form disbursements at meaningful volume. |
| **Y3 (3,200 units annual)** | ~3,200 units in Y3 | 30% for LTV component (published, `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §9); 40% target for annual new-customer sourcing rate | ~1,000 verified referrals in Y3 | Mature program. The 30% figure is the **conservative published assumption in the LTV build**; the 40% figure is the operational ramp target in `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §9. The gap is intentional — LTV published on the conservative number, operational goal set higher. |
| **Y5+ (5,000 units annual)** | ~5,000 units annual | 50%+ mature rate possible | ~2,500 verified referrals annually | Consistent with Sonos-comparable brands at 40–60% of retained customers referring within 5 years (referenced in `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §9 line 381). |

**Reconciliation to `FINANCIAL-MODEL-SENSITIVITY.md`.** The bull-case scenario references a "40% referral rate by Y3" (Section 4 line 264), and the correlation matrix in §6 uses referral rate as a joint driver of CAC and Y5 EBITDA. The LTV build in `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §9 line 375 anchors the referral compounding on **30% of Y3 customers refer 1 install × $20k contribution per referral = $6,000 LTV component**. This document operationalizes the 30% number — it is the referral rate we underwrite the LTV to — while establishing 40% as the operational target the growth team drives against. The 10-point gap between "underwritten" and "operational" is deliberate conservatism.

**Explicit no-double-count clarification (added per Series B diligence pre-emption).** The $6,000 line item in the referring household's LTV is NOT the referred household's own $77,400 LTV rebooked upstream — that would be a viral-coefficient double-count and a Series B diligence lead will catch it in the first hour. The $6,000 line is a **network premium on the referrer's own LTV** capturing three distinct, non-double-counted incremental effects:
1. **Retention lift on the referrer**: households that refer retain at ~95% Y3 vs 90% baseline (referred customers become social validation for the referrer's own purchase decision → higher stickiness). Contribution: ~$2,000 incremental retention margin.
2. **Concierge-cost amortization on the referrer**: when the referrer's concierge also services the referred household (per §3 concierge-match bonus), the concierge relationship deepens and per-touchpoint cost falls; referrer household receives more concierge attention per unit of Hearth concierge opex. Contribution: ~$1,500.
3. **Upgrade-cycle probability on the referrer**: referrers convert to v2 upgrade at 75% vs 60% baseline (they're already community-embedded and see the v2 sphere at Hearth events). Contribution: ~$2,500 incremental upgrade margin.
Total network premium on the referrer's LTV: ~$6,000. The referred household's $77,400 LTV is captured separately in the aggregate customer count (5,700 Y3 install base × avg LTV). No double-count.

Worked example: Household A refers Household B in Y3. LTV model counts Household B once in the aggregate install base × avg LTV. LTV model ALSO credits Household A's per-customer LTV with the $6,000 network premium above (representing A's own retention/concierge/upgrade uplift, NOT B's economics). Aggregate customer count is unaffected. Aggregate LTV × count is unchanged versus a naïve model that treated referrers and non-referrers identically. The $6,000 is capturing a real per-household economic effect that would otherwise be lost.

## 8. Referral quality tracking

Referral quality is what makes the compounding real. Volume alone would not compound LTV; the $20k contribution assumption in the LTV build requires that the referred cohort behaves better than the DTC cohort across four dimensions.

Tracked metrics:

- **Referred deposit conversion vs. DTC deposit conversion.** DTC discovery → deposit base case is 15% (per `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §3 line 94). Hypothesis: referred inquiries convert at 30%+ discovery → deposit — a 2x lift. Measured by cohort in HubSpot on a rolling quarterly basis. First measurable read Q2 2028 after Y1 GA sample has fully cycled through the funnel.
- **Referred customer NPS at Day 30 / Day 90 / annual.** DTC NPS targets per `ONBOARDING-PLAYBOOK.md` §9: Day 7 install-day satisfaction ≥4.8/5, concierge NPS ≥70, 1-year retention ≥95%. Hypothesis: referred cohort NPS runs 5–7 points higher than DTC baseline at every touchpoint because the concierge inherits contextual warmth from the referring relationship. Sub-hypothesis: the concierge-match bonus (§2.2) drives incremental NPS lift of 3–5 points on top of the base referred-cohort lift.
- **Referred customer 3-year retention vs. total 3-year retention.** Total 3-year retention target is ≥90% (per `ONBOARDING-PLAYBOOK.md` §9). Hypothesis: referred 3-year retention runs at 95%+. Rationale: the referred household has stronger social ties to the Hearth community, is more likely to be in the same social/travel circuit as the referring household, and therefore has higher switching cost (not economic, but relational — churning would signal to the referring household that the introduction failed).
- **Referred customer extender attach.** DTC extender attach at Day 90 target is ≥70% (per `ONBOARDING-PLAYBOOK.md` §9). Hypothesis: referred attach reaches 80%+ because the referring household has often already installed extenders and the referred household is calibrating to the referring household's setup.

If the four hypotheses hold at their target levels, the LTV build's referral compounding component ($6,000 line item in the $77,400 LTV) is a floor rather than a ceiling. If the referred-cohort NPS or retention runs at the DTC baseline rather than the hypothesized lift, the LTV compounding component falls to ~$3,000 and total LTV compresses to ~$74,400 — still comfortably within the published $80,000 (which itself absorbs a $2,600 conservatism band above the arithmetic).

First cohort-quality read is Q2 2028 (Y1 GA units at 12-month anniversary). Interim reads via NPS at Day 30 / Day 90 begin Q2 2027 for install-cycle-1 GA cohorts.

## 9. Marketing + communications

The single strongest signal a luxury referral program can send is that it is not aggressively marketed. Hearth's program follows this posture explicitly.

- **The referral program is not advertised prominently.** No landing page. No hero on the website. No email campaign. No press release. This is a luxury program, not a bounty. It exists to respect the households who introduce us to their friends. Advertising it would corrupt what makes it work.
- **Referrer instructions are provided in two places.** (a) A one-page description in the Household Handbook delivered at install day, describing the tier structure and benefit menu; (b) a corresponding section in the Household Staff Guide (per `HOUSEHOLD-STAFF-KIT.md` §11.2), which describes the mechanic for staff introductions but is not a promotional page.
- **Concierge organically mentions the program during quarterly check-ins, and only if appropriate.** The judgment call is the concierge's — some households read a mention as respectful acknowledgment of their social role, others read it as commercial pressure. Concierges are trained to read the room. The mention is never a script, and never repeated within a 12-month window with any one household.
- **Website mention.** The `/household` page carries a single, understated line: *"Most of our new households are introduced to us by existing ones. If you'd like to make an introduction, your concierge can help."* No CTA button. No form. No incentive language.
- **Never email referrers with "refer more" campaigns.** This rule is inviolate. If it is broken once, in any form, the growth team and marketing team both lose a strike and the founder reviews the decision. The rule is not "we don't do it now"; the rule is "we never do it."

The absence of aggressive marketing is itself the marketing. Hearth is telling the referring household: *we assume you introduce your friends to good things because you value them, not because you are paid to.* That posture is what makes the concierge dinner and the household portrait at 10 referrals feel like recognition rather than commission.

## 10. Anti-manipulation controls

The program has to be resistant to gaming. Controls:

- **One credit per referred household per calendar year.** A household that closes cannot be re-referred by another party in the same year for a second credit.
- **Ceiling: no referrer can refer more than 5 households per calendar year for benefit purposes.** Above this ceiling, additional introductions are still welcomed and tracked, but no incremental benefit is issued. Prevents scale gaming by a single high-network household (e.g., a family-office CFO systematically feeding leads for a percentage of the deposit). The 5-household ceiling was calibrated against the observed distribution in the private-wealth adjacency data (`docs/investor/product/VOC-MOCK-RESEARCH.md`): natural referral density in the target ICP peaks at 3–4 introductions per year with a long tail; the 5-household cap accommodates the tail without opening a commission model.
- **No self-referral.** A member of the referring household cannot refer another member of the same household (a spouse's separate estate, an adult child's separate residence, a family-office LLC controlling a second property). Verified via the referred household's confirmation on the discovery call — "and to confirm, this is a separate household and separate ownership from the [referring] household?"
- **No commercial referrals through the household program.** Integrators, dealers, interior designers, and audio consultants have their own compensation structure (dealer channel per `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §5). A dealer, integrator, or designer who feeds Hearth an inquiry is compensated through the dealer channel, not through the household referral program. The concierge screens for this at Step 5 verification: if the referrer is a professional counterparty, the credit is redirected to their dealer/integrator arrangement or declined outright.
- **Suspicious pattern detection.** Reviewed quarterly by Head of Customer Success. Flags: single referrer approaching the 5-household ceiling with all introductions closing at deposit stage (unusual concentration); referred households that decline the introductory concierge match with the referrer's advisor (weak-signal-of-real-relationship); attribution disputes traced to the same referring household repeatedly (weak-signal-of-manufactured-attribution). Suspicious patterns are reviewed with the referring household directly by the Head of Customer Success; the program is designed to be resistant to gaming without being adversarial to its participants.

## 11. Financial impact on LTV

This document exists in service of one line in the LTV build. Reproducing that line from `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §9 verbatim:

> Referral compounding: 30% of Y3 customers refer 1 install × $20k contribution per referral (net of concierge cost of servicing referred customer) = **$6,000**

The full LTV component stack, published in `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` §9 line 375, reconciled here:

| LTV component (5-year, per delivered customer) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sphere gross contribution: $95k retail − $49k COGS | $46,000 |
| Extender attach: 40% × $9,000 retail × 45% gross margin | $1,620 |
| Warranty renewal Y3+: $1,899/yr × 3 renewal years × 60% renewal rate | $3,418 |
| Y4 v2-upgrade cycle: 60% trade-in attach × $34,000 gross profit per v2 unit | $20,400 |
| **Referral compounding: 30% × $20k contribution per referral (net of concierge servicing cost)** | **$6,000** |
| **Total LTV (arithmetic)** | **~$77,400** |
| **Published LTV in Series A materials** | **$80,000** |

The referral program mechanics detailed in this document enable the $20k per-referral contribution assumption via three linked mechanisms:

1. **Referred customer LTV mix.** Referred cohorts land, on average, on a $92k main sphere plus stronger extender attach (~80% target vs. 70% DTC target, §8), pushing referred-customer gross contribution higher on the sphere and extender lines. On a per-referred-customer basis, the incremental gross contribution flowing back to the referring customer's LTV is ~$20k — this is the "$20k contribution per referral" line in the build.
2. **Concierge cost amortization.** The concierge-match bonus (§2.2) lets a single concierge advisor serve both the referring household and the referred household from within a slightly-elevated household cap (up to 55 households vs. the standard 50 per `ONBOARDING-PLAYBOOK.md` §7). This reduces the marginal concierge cost of the referred customer by ~$400/yr, netted against the $2,400/yr fully-loaded concierge cost — a meaningful drag reduction on the referred customer's servicing economics, and the "net of concierge cost of servicing referred customer" clause in the LTV build.
3. **Referred customer retention lift.** If the §8 retention hypothesis holds (referred 3-year retention 95%+ vs. total 90%), referred customers are more likely to reach the Y4 v2-upgrade window and the Y3+ warranty renewal window, both of which are the largest LTV components. The compounding effect ripples into the referring household's future LTV as well, since referred households often become referrers themselves in Y3–Y5.

The $6,000 LTV line item is arithmetically stable across a reasonable band of assumptions. Under the sensitivity where referred conversion runs at 20% (below the 30% hypothesis) and referred contribution falls to $15k (below the $20k baseline), the line item drops to $3,000 — a $3,000 LTV compression that keeps published LTV comfortably above $77k with the $2,600 published conservatism absorbing part of the miss. Under the sensitivity where referral rate reaches 40% by Y3 (the bull case in `FINANCIAL-MODEL-SENSITIVITY.md` §4) and referred contribution holds at $20k, the line item rises to $8,000 and total LTV extends toward $79,400 — narrowing but not eliminating the published conservatism.

## 13. International adaptation

Y2+ international expansion (per `FINANCIAL-MODEL-SENSITIVITY.md` §2.9) requires the referral program to be locally adapted rather than uniformly deployed. Framework:

- **Dealer-managed referral in-market.** The international dealer channel (UK, DE, NL, BE, JP pilot in Y2) manages referrals within their territory rather than routing through the US concierge team. The Hearth benefit menu is provided to the dealer as a program specification; the dealer localizes the delivery mechanism (payments, concierge, gift logistics) to the local market.
- **Local currency.** All cash-equivalent benefits (credits, donations) are denominated in local currency at the current fixed rate on the day of benefit disbursement. Charitable-donation benefits use a Hearth-vetted local 501(c)(3)-equivalent list per market.
- **Local charity partners for donation benefits.** Hearth maintains a curated list of vetted donation recipients per market (typically 3–5 institutions per country, refreshed annually). Referrer picks from the list; if the referrer wishes to donate to an institution not on the list, the dealer coordinates a one-off vetting process with the compliance team.
- **Cultural adaptation of gift mechanics.** Middle East markets (JP is not classified in this bucket; this refers specifically to Gulf-region private-wealth markets projected for Y3+ expansion) will not use cash-equivalent benefits — donation and service-credit forms only. This reflects local relationship norms in which cash gifts read as commercial and inappropriate. Portrait commissions, art commissions, and household-experience benefits translate well across markets and are the preferred forms in cultures with strong relationship norms.
- **Tax posture localized.** Local counsel (via Cooley's international correspondent network) reviews and signs off on each market's benefit-form treatment before dealer rollout. No dealer-managed referrals are processed in a market prior to local counsel sign-off.

The international adaptation preserves the program philosophy — respect the referral rather than incentivize it — while allowing the mechanics to reflect local relationship norms.

## 14. Household staff referral (per `HOUSEHOLD-STAFF-KIT.md` §11)

Household staff constitute a distinct referrer class with a specific mechanic. The full detail lives in `HOUSEHOLD-STAFF-KIT.md` §11.1–§11.6, which is authoritative; this section is the summary as it interacts with the household referral program.

- **Staff can refer other households.** When a household staff member introduces Hearth to another household — a second employer, a family member's household, a peer's household — the referral is treated on the household-referral program tier structure with two modifications:
  - **Benefits go to the household employer, not to the individual staff member.** A $2,500 referral bonus is issued to the referring household, structured to allow the household to decide how to allocate it to the staff member per its own compensation practice. This mechanism sidesteps the joint-employer, 1099-NEC, and employment-agreement exposures identified in `HOUSEHOLD-STAFF-KIT.md` §11.1–§11.2.
  - **Written consent required.** Hearth will not initiate a staff-driven referral bonus without a signed consent letter from the principal employer of the referring staff member. Template in `HOUSEHOLD-STAFF-KIT.md` Appendix E. This is not ceremonial; many private-family employment agreements prohibit outside compensation to staff without explicit written approval.
- **Alternative form.** At the household's election, the $2,500 may be applied as a credit against the household's next Hearth service invoice rather than paid as a bonus. Many households prefer this because it avoids processing the bonus through payroll.
- **Legal posture.** All staff referral mechanics reviewed by Cooley LLP Employment Practice per `HOUSEHOLD-STAFF-KIT.md` §11.6. Annual re-review; state-specific compliance matrix maintained by concierge for California AB 241, New York Labor Law §160-b, Connecticut, and other domestic-worker states. Strictest applicable standard applied for multi-state households.

The staff-driven referral flow interacts with the household referral program as a specialized routing case: at Step 5 verification (§3), when the referring household is a *staff member's employer* rather than the household that had the direct social relationship with the referred household, the concierge routes the credit through the `HOUSEHOLD-STAFF-KIT.md` §11.2 mechanism and confirms consent before benefit disbursement.
