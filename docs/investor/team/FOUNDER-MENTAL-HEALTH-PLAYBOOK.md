## 1. Why this document exists

You are a solo, first-time, hardware, public-facing founder with kids in the home, mid-forties, coming off a Shark Tank close at $2M on $13.33M post-money, entering an eighteen-month public phase.

Look at what the research says about that combination:

- **Baseline (peer-reviewed):** Michael Freeman's 2015 study out of UCSF / Stanford (Freeman et al., *Small Business Economics*, 2015) surveyed 242 entrepreneurs against a matched general population and found **49% self-reported at least one lifetime mental health condition, approximately 2× the general-population rate.** Depression specifically was ~30% among founders vs. ~15% general population. This is the load-bearing peer-reviewed citation.
- **Public-phase amplification (industry survey):** Startup Snapshot's 2023 report on 296 founders — not peer-reviewed but widely cited — found **72% of founders said their startup impacted their mental health**, with the most cited stressors being investor pressure, personal finances, and the loneliness of decision-making. Public-phase founders (post-press-cycle, post-funding-announcement) reported higher on all three.
- **Compounding factors — internal estimate, not peer-reviewed:** Every founder-panel and executive-coaching-firm conversation with hardware founders, solo founders, and founders parenting minor children in the home describes each as an additional stressor on top of the Freeman baseline. Quantifying that stack precisely is not something the published literature has done. What matters for our purposes: you sit at multiple stress axes simultaneously, and every one of them is well-documented as a real risk factor individually. The specific multipliers cited in earlier drafts of this document (3x/2x/2.5x/4x with a "McKinsey 2023" attribution) were not sourced correctly and have been retired. The direction is right; the precision was not defensible.

You sit at every one of those intersections. If we don't build the scaffolding now, before Shark Tank airs, we are asking you to build it during a hurricane. That is not going to work.

This document is not a wellness handbook. It is an operational readiness document — the same category as our SOC 2 controls, our supply chain redundancy plan, our cap table hygiene. The founder is a load-bearing component of the company. Load-bearing components get engineered, monitored, and reinforced. Nothing else in Hearth survives if you break.

One page. That is the whole rationale. Everything from here is the actual scaffolding.

## 2. The founder's baseline check — completed before Shark Tank airs

We need a photograph of you at rest before the storm. Not because we expect problems, but because when things drift, the only way to know is to compare against a known-good baseline. Every fighter pilot and every astronaut has a physiological and psychological baseline on file. So do you now.

### 2.1 Physical baseline
Complete before the air date. Schedule these in a single week if possible.

- **Comprehensive blood panel:** CBC, CMP, lipid panel, fasting glucose, HbA1c, hs-CRP, homocysteine, ferritin, vitamin D, vitamin B12, folate, magnesium RBC. Insurance covers most; concierge medicine covers the rest.
- **Hormone panel:** total testosterone, free testosterone, SHBG, LH, FSH, DHEA-S, cortisol AM + PM, TSH, free T3, free T4, reverse T3. Founders in their mid-forties commonly drift on total T and cortisol curve without noticing. Catch it now.
- **Cardiac stress test:** treadmill or CPET with an EKG. If accessible, add a CT coronary calcium score. Family history matters here; document it.
- **Sleep study:** in-lab polysomnography or at minimum a validated home study (WatchPAT or equivalent). Rule out sleep apnea. Undiagnosed apnea in a founder is a career-ending mistake — it presents as depression, low libido, brain fog, and irritability, and it is trivially treatable once diagnosed.
- **Body composition:** DEXA scan. One number to anchor movement decisions for the year.
- **Dental + optical:** get the boring appointments done now. You will not do them in Q3.

### 2.2 Mental baseline
Complete with your named therapist, in the same week if possible.

- **PHQ-9** — depression screen. Score baseline.
- **GAD-7** — anxiety screen. Score baseline.
- **Maslach Burnout Inventory** — three subscales (emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, personal accomplishment). Score baseline.
- **Adult ADHD self-report scale (ASRS-v1.1)** — many founder profiles carry undiagnosed ADHD. If positive screen, follow up with a clinical evaluation. Treated ADHD is a superpower; untreated ADHD in a public phase is a landmine.
- **AUDIT-C** — alcohol use short screen. Score baseline honestly, in front of your therapist not your board.
- **PCL-5** — if you have any prior trauma history (military, medical, family-of-origin), get it on the record now, because activation events during the public phase can retrigger.

Store all scores in a private clinical file. Retake all six instruments every ninety days for the first year, and any time a warning sign in §10 triggers.

### 2.3 Marital baseline
This one is critical and it is the one founders most often skip.

- **Intake session with a licensed couples therapist** — not because there is a problem, but because a therapist who already knows your relationship's baseline can catch drift in one session that would otherwise take four. If you show up in crisis at month nine, you will lose two months to intake alone. Do the intake now.
- **Explicit agreement in that session:** the therapist is retained, on file, and available at 48-hour notice for the next 24 months. Not couples-therapy-as-crisis; couples-therapy-as-maintenance.
- **Written shared understanding** between you and your wife on: (a) what happens during airing week, (b) what happens if you're asked to travel more than 4 days in a row, (c) what happens if a public event triggers a media pile-on, (d) how disagreements about scope get resolved (see §7 and §9).

### 2.4 Financial baseline
Financial fear is a founder's most reliable dysregulator, and it is the one you can most cleanly remove.

- **Personal financial planner** reviews family cash flow, savings runway, education funds, and lifestyle burn rate. Target: 18 months of family runway independent of Hearth salary.
- **Emergency reserves** in a HYSA at 6 months minimum, entirely separate from Hearth.
- **$2M term life insurance policy** on you (per EXEC-COMP §1.6). Named beneficiary is your wife. 20-year level term. Get it now, at your current health baseline — premiums only get worse.
- **Disability insurance** — own-occupation, if underwritable. This is the policy founders forget until they're the ones who needed it.

### 2.5 Documentation baseline
Not fun. Take an afternoon.

- Named beneficiaries on every account (401k, IRA, brokerage, HSA, insurance).
- Will updated with children's guardianship named, executor named, and Hearth equity handling specified.
- Healthcare proxy signed. Your wife is primary; a second is named.
- Durable power of attorney signed.
- HIPAA release naming your wife and your named therapist (so that in a crisis, they can talk to each other and to your physician without a court order).
- Password vault with legacy access configured. Your wife has recovery access.
- A single-page "if I'm hit by a bus" document listing key contacts at Hearth, the board chair, the general counsel, and the location of the will.

If any of §2.4 or §2.5 is not done by the air date, the calendar gets rearranged until it is. This is the price of admission.

## 3. The daily maintenance stack

The following practices are not aspirational — they are the minimum viable version of the person who can carry the next eighteen months. Miss one occasionally; do not miss two on the same day; do not miss any two for three days in a row without a check-in with your therapist.

### 3.1 Sleep
- **7.5 to 8 hours minimum.** Non-negotiable except by prior agreement with your therapist.
- **In bed by 10:30 PM. Wake by 6:00 AM.** The schedule is boring on purpose. Boredom protects you.
- **No screens after 9:30 PM.** Phone charges in the kitchen. Paperback books, physical journal, or conversation with your wife are the only permitted activities.
- **Bedroom is dark, cool (65-68°F), and quiet.** Blackout curtains if needed.
- **Wearable tracking:** Oura Ring (Gen 4) or Whoop 4.0. Not for judgment — for data. If HRV drops below your rolling 30-day average for four consecutive nights, treat that as a warning sign and follow §10.
- **Sleep debt is not repayable in a single weekend.** If you owe more than 4 hours across a week, you owe them for the month.
- **Travel sleep:** melatonin 0.5mg for time-zone shifts >3 hours only. No sleep meds without your physician's involvement.

### 3.2 Movement
- **30 minutes daily target.** Not "most days" — the aim is daily. A missed day is a data point, not a failure; two missed days in a row is a signal to check what else has slipped.
- **Strength training 3x per week.** 45-60 min. Compound lifts. Not to be a bodybuilder — to preserve bone density, testosterone, and the felt sense of physical competence that regulates the nervous system.
- **Walking meetings preferred over sit-down.** Any 1:1 that does not require a screen becomes a walking meeting. This alone adds ~30 minutes of movement most days.
- **Sunday long walk with wife.** 60-90 minutes. Separate from planning session (§4). Talking or silent, either is fine.
- **Zone 2 cardio 2x per week.** 30 minutes each. Rowing, cycling, brisk walking on incline. This is the boring one that pays back the most.

### 3.3 Nutrition
- **Protein target: 0.8g per pound of body weight, daily.** For most founders in your bracket, this means 140-180g. Boring, achievable with three protein-forward meals and one snack.
- **Low-carb pre-appearance meal** per FOUNDER-SPEAKING-PLAYBOOK. Eggs + greens + protein; avoid pastries, orange juice, and heavy carbs in the 4 hours before stage time. This is not for weight; it is for glucose stability, which is for cognitive stability, which is for the version of you that shows up on stage.
- **Hydration:** 100 oz water daily minimum, more with travel or caffeine.
- **Caffeine:** no caffeine after 12:00 PM. If you cannot execute the afternoon without stimulants, the problem is sleep, not caffeine.
- **Alcohol:** no alcohol Sunday through Thursday. Moderate on Friday and Saturday (defined: two drinks per evening maximum, not both). No alcohol in the 48 hours before a stage appearance. No alcohol as a coping mechanism after a bad day — that is when you call your therapist instead.
- **Meal timing:** try to eat with your family at dinner at least four nights per week. This is a marital investment as much as a nutritional one.

### 3.4 Meditation / mental clearing
- **20 minutes daily minimum.** Morning is preferred; evening is acceptable; both is ideal.
- **Insight Timer or Headspace** — pick one and commit for 90 days before switching.
- **If skeptical:** the mechanism is not spiritual. Cortisol regulation, prefrontal cortex activity under load, and interoceptive accuracy all improve on the timescale of weeks in RCT-grade studies. See Kabat-Zinn's Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) research corpus (multiple RCTs from UMass Center for Mindfulness), and the Goyal et al. 2014 meta-analysis in *JAMA Internal Medicine* (47 trials, ~3,500 participants) showing moderate evidence for anxiety and depression reduction. Twenty minutes is cheaper than the antidepressant you'll otherwise need.
- **Breathwork alternative:** if seated meditation does not stick, box breathing (4-4-4-4) for 10 minutes twice daily is a valid substitute.
- **Cold exposure is optional, not part of the mandate.** Do it if it helps you; skip it if it doesn't. It is not the load-bearing practice.

### 3.5 Journal
- **5-10 minutes in the morning: intention, gratitude, one worry surfaced.**
- **5-10 minutes in the evening: what went well, what didn't, what you are letting go of tonight.**
- **Wife-safe language.** Write as if she may read the notebook after your death. Not censored — kind. This forces you to name things with the compassion you would want if the roles were reversed.
- **Paper, not app.** The friction is the feature. A Moleskine soft-cover, one per quarter.
- **Do not review your own old entries obsessively.** Once per quarter is enough.

## 4. Weekly rhythm

The week has a shape. Protect the shape.

- **Sunday.** 90-minute planning session for the week. First 30 minutes with your wife (family calendar, kids' commitments, out-of-town nights, emotional temperature). Next 60 minutes solo (business calendar, top three outcomes, what would make the week a success, what would make it a failure). No screens for the wife portion.
- **Monday.** No external meetings before 10:00 AM. Breakfast with kids. School drop-off if the schedule allows. Deep work block 10:00 AM - 12:00 PM. This is the anchor day. Monday sets the pace of the week.
- **Tuesday through Thursday.** Peak execution days. Meetings, travel, investor calls, media. Load the week toward these three days on purpose.
- **Friday.** Off at 3:00 PM. Personal reset. Errand, workout, coffee with a friend, whatever restores you. Not a fake half-day where you are still on Slack.
- **Friday 6:00 PM through Saturday 6:00 PM.** Unplugged. Phone in a drawer or on the kitchen counter. Wife has emergency access via her phone. Board chair has emergency access via a separate escalation path. Everyone else waits 24 hours. This is a Shabbat-adjacent discipline; it is a real practice, well-documented among high-performing founders regardless of religious framing.
- **Saturday 6:00 PM through Sunday morning.** Family time. Whatever the family wants. You are a father and a husband first during these hours.
- **Sunday afternoon.** Long walk or hike. Then the planning session begins the next week.

The 24-hour unplug on Friday night is the single most protective practice in this document. Under stress, it will be the first one you try to erode. Do not.

## 5. Monthly rhythm

- **Once per month:** 60-minute individual therapist session (not couple's). Minimum. Weekly is better during the airing month and the Series A pitch cycle.
- **Once per month:** 60-minute executive coach session. Business-focused, but the coach knows the personal is in the room. See §7.
- **Once per month:** date night with your wife, out of the house, no kids, no phones. Same day of the month, standing reservation.
- **Once per month:** dinner with a peer founder circle (see §7). No spouses. No investors. No press.
- **Once per quarter:** 3-day retreat with the family. Real vacation. No laptop; phone in airplane mode. Book them for the year in January. Preserve them like board meetings.
- **Once per quarter:** 60-minute session with a founder-peer network like YPO Forum, EO Forum, or a Reboot cohort. Structured confidentiality; shared burden.
- **Once per quarter:** physical follow-up with your primary physician. Repeat the abbreviated blood panel semi-annually.
- **Once per quarter:** re-take PHQ-9, GAD-7, Maslach, and AUDIT-C. Send scores to your therapist ahead of the next session.

## 6. Crisis protocols

Specific moments deserve specific protocols. Print this section separately and put it in your wife's phone.

### 6.1 Shark Tank airing night
- Family together in the house. Order in.
- Phones face-down for the 8 hours after the episode airs. Head of Comms is the only inbound channel; she funnels only true emergencies.
- One glass of wine with dinner. Maximum.
- No live-monitoring of social. Head of Comms delivers a summary the next morning at 9:00 AM.
- Go to bed at your normal time.
- Wife's input has strong weight over any evening activity that would break these rules — treat her call as consent required, not a rubber stamp.

### 6.2 First negative press piece
- **24-hour "do not read" rule.** Comms team summarizes the piece with the actionable content and the emotional pitch level. You read the summary, not the piece.
- Response, if any, is drafted overnight and reviewed by comms + counsel + your executive coach before it goes out.
- No public response within 24 hours except a factual correction if there is a factual error.
- Call your therapist within 48 hours. Not because the piece will break you; because getting the reaction on the record early prevents rumination.

### 6.3 First customer complaint that goes viral
- **4-hour blackout.** You do not open the app. You do not read the thread.
- Concierge Program Lead handles the operational response.
- Head of Comms manages the public thread.
- You get a written summary at the 4-hour mark and decide whether to respond personally. Default: do not respond personally. Founders responding to viral complaints in real time is a well-documented way to double the story's lifespan.
- Debrief with the team the next morning; process into product decisions the following week.

### 6.4 Board meeting stress spike
- 20 minutes of meditation before the meeting. Not optional.
- 10-minute walk immediately after the meeting, alone. Phone off.
- Dinner with family that night. No follow-on investor drinks, ever.
- If the meeting was bad, call the executive coach within 24 hours.

### 6.5 Major hire decision
- **24-hour sleep-on-it rule.** No offer letter goes out same-day, no matter how compelling the candidate.
- If you cannot articulate the reason for the offer in two sentences the next morning, do not send it.
- Second-check with your board chair on any C-level hire.
- Third-check with your wife (not to weigh in on the candidate, but to weigh in on your excitement level — she can see hypomanic drift before you can).

### 6.6 Founder feels acute mental crisis
- Pre-selected therapist emergency line: **[NUMBER]** — programmed in your phone as "Dr. [Name]."
- Wife's cell: **[NUMBER]** — programmed as "Wife."
- Board chair's cell: **[NUMBER]** — programmed as "Board Chair."
- 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline in your favorites.
- If you cannot reach the therapist, you can go to the emergency room. There is no shame in an ER visit for a mental health crisis. Hearth's medical coverage covers it. Your board chair has pre-agreed that the operating plan tolerates a 3-day founder absence with zero explanation required to investors.

## 7. Support network

You are not alone. You are, at most, "solo" in the cap-table sense. The following people know each other's names, know they are on your team, and have pre-negotiated confidentiality with each other where it matters.

### 7.1 Wife
Primary support. But note the word **primary**, not **sole**. The most damaging pattern among solo founders is treating the spouse as the entire emotional infrastructure. It burns them out, it degrades the marriage, and it makes the founder more fragile, not less.

- Weekly date night, standing reservation.
- Sunday 30-minute alignment session together (from §4).
- **No business talk after 8:00 PM in the house.** Not because business is bad, but because the marriage needs a container that is not the company.
- Two people, one house, one bed: the marriage is a distinct entity from either of you individually. Protect it as its own thing.
- Wife's input has strong weight over any commitment that would break the weekly rhythm without 30-day notice — treat her call as consent required, not a rubber stamp.

### 7.2 Named therapist
- Ph.D. or MD-level. Ideally trained in ACT, CBT, or IFS, and comfortable with high-performing clients. Ideally has treated other founders.
- Meets monthly minimum; more during airing month and Series A pitch cycle.
- Full confidentiality. Employer has no access. Investors have no access. The therapist's notes are protected by law.
- Named in this document; contact info in §14.

### 7.3 Executive coach
- Named individual. Recommended profiles: Jerry Colonna's Reboot.io practice (specifically built for founder-coaching), Josh Waitzkin's Art of Learning Project cohort, Bob Rotella's sport-psychology lineage adapted for high-pressure decision-makers, or an equivalent operator-coach who has personally coached at least three founders through Series A → Series C. Not a generic "leadership coach." (Prior draft named "Steve Jamison's practice" — Jamison is a ghostwriter and author, not a founder-coach practice; retired.)
- Monthly minimum. Focus is 60% business decisions, 40% personal capacity.
- Coach is a paid vendor of Hearth, but reports to no one. Coach's notes are not company property.
- Coach and therapist know each other's roles and do not overlap; if boundary questions arise, therapist takes precedence.

### 7.4 Board chair
- Separate from the lead investor. This is critical.
- Weekly informal check-in call, 20-30 minutes, unstructured. Not a status report — a temperature check.
- Confidentiality respected. What is said on the call does not go to the board without your consent, except in the narrow contingency defined in the crisis section below.
- If founder becomes unable to work for more than 5 business days, board chair activates the contingency operating plan per FOUNDER-NARRATIVE Team-of-One §. Board chair has pre-drafted messaging.

### 7.5 Founder-peer circle
- 4-6 other solo founders at similar stage (seed-through-Series-A, hardware or hardware-adjacent, ideally at least two who are also parents).
- Monthly video call, 90 minutes.
- Rotating facilitation. Structured Chatham House rules.
- This is the room where you can say the thing you cannot say to the board, to your team, or to your wife. Every founder needs this room.

### 7.6 Physician team
- Primary care physician: named.
- Cardiologist: on file, given family history.
- Endocrinologist: on file, given the hormone panel results.
- Sleep specialist: on file if sleep study is anything other than fully clean.
- Concierge medicine option: if budget allows, a concierge PCP dramatically reduces friction on all of the above. Cost is modest relative to the return.

### 7.7 Your wife's support system
This is the section founders forget.

Your wife carries an invisible load. She holds the emotional water when you cannot. She parents solo during your travel weeks. She fields "how is Mark doing" from her friends and from your relatives. She is the one who notices when the drift starts.

She needs:
- **Her own therapist** — separate from your therapist, separate from the couples therapist. Fully confidential to her. Paid by Hearth via family benefit.
- **Her own support network** — friends, family, a peer group of other founder spouses if she wants one (Founders Wives Circle, or an informal group).
- **Her own outlet** — hobby, exercise, part-time work, whatever she chooses.
- **Real time away from the family** — at least one weekend per quarter, alone or with friends, no children, no you. Childcare arranged in advance and paid by Hearth or by the household without debate.
- **Explicit permission** — from you, in writing if it helps — that all of the above is expected, not indulgent.

If you do not protect your wife, none of the rest of this document matters. She is the load-bearing wall of your load-bearing wall.

## 8. Family safeguards

The kids do not choose to be founder's kids. Do not make them pay for it.

### 8.1 Kids' well-being monitoring
- Monthly family check-in dinner — no phones, no work talk, each person names one high and one low from the month.
- Watch school performance, sleep, moods, social behavior. Any material change is worth a mention to your wife within 24 hours.
- Kids have their own primary care physician, on schedule for their annual visits.
- Kids' school counselor knows your name and knows the parent situation involves a business build. That single line of context prevents a lot of misunderstanding at parent-teacher conferences.

### 8.2 Family therapy option
- Pre-negotiated with a therapist willing to see the whole family at short notice (ideally the same practice as your couples therapist).
- Trigger: any of the kids showing sustained behavioral change for 30+ days; any acute event (bullying, family loss, a public event about Hearth that reaches the schoolyard).
- Nobody is stigmatized for going to family therapy; it is a tool, not a diagnosis.

### 8.3 Spouse burnout risk
- Real. Watch for it. Signs: withdrawal, over-drinking, hyper-scheduling, chronic irritability at the kids, weight change in either direction, sleep disruption.
- If two or more show up for 30 days, gently name it, and ask what she needs. Do not fix; ask.
- Her therapist relationship is protected. You do not get to ask what she talks about there.

### 8.4 Marriage maintenance
- Weekly date night (§7).
- Monthly out-of-town weekend or in-town-away weekend (childcare arranged in advance, on the calendar 60 days out). Not optional. Book them in January.
- Non-business talk on these weekends is the rule; if business talk is happening, one of you names it and it stops.

### 8.5 Kid-safe workspace
- **No work in kids' bedrooms.** Ever.
- **No work at family meals.** Phone in the kitchen.
- **"Closed door = do not enter unless emergency."** Emergency is defined narrowly and in advance with the kids ("blood, fire, or Mom says so").
- **Physical office at home has clear boundaries.** Kids should be able to knock, but should also see the door close and know what that means.
- **Work travel is announced in advance** with a return date, on a family calendar the kids can see. Kids handle predictable absence far better than surprise absence.

### 8.6 Kids' Hearth exposure
- Kids do not need to be involved in the product. Their role is to be kids.
- If Hearth is going in the family home (which it is, by product design), the kids get the same product experience as any user, not a special beta program. Their input is welcomed as users, not as a product team.
- Kids' faces, names, and voices are off-limits for Hearth marketing without their explicit consent at age-appropriate maturity. Enshrine this now.
- Grandparents and extended family are similarly off-limits without consent.

## 9. The public-phase gauntlet

For each public appearance category, a pre-agreed shape:

### 9.1 Shark Tank airing week
- Family together the night of the air date. See §6.1.
- Airing week: no travel, no evening events, no investor calls after 7:00 PM.
- Two hours per morning reserved for inbound triage with Head of Comms.
- Standing therapist appointment during airing week.

### 9.2 CES / trade show week
- **Trip length capped at 4 days.** No exceptions.
- **Wife travels for 1 day** if childcare allows, even just to have dinner with you mid-week.
- **Home by end of Day 5.** If the trip is longer, the schedule is wrong.
- On-site: two press meetings per morning max, two customer/partner meetings per afternoon max, dinner reserved as either sleep-early or a single social with the team.
- Alcohol per §3.3. Trade show culture will push. Push back.
- Debrief with executive coach within 72 hours of return.

### 9.3 Podcast circuit
- Cluster 2-3 podcasts in the same city when possible.
- Home the same night when possible. If not, one hotel night maximum.
- Prep block the day before (60 min per show).
- Recovery block the day after (90 min, no meetings).
- Podcast host and topic pre-approved by Head of Comms; you do not commit on the spot.

### 9.4 Board meetings
- Preserved as work, not as personal. Wife is not present, kids do not attend.
- 20-minute meditation before, 10-minute walk after, family dinner the same evening. See §6.4.
- Materials prepared 5 business days in advance so the day of is execution, not last-minute prep.

### 9.5 Investor dinners
- Home by 10:00 PM. Non-negotiable.
- No more than 2 per week.
- Alcohol per §3.3 — a single drink signals sociability, more than that costs you the next day.
- No investor dinner during airing week or Series A pitch closing week.

### 9.6 Founder retreat weekends
- Agreed with wife in advance, 60+ days out.
- Family takes their own weekend as a substitute — this is a swap, not an addition.
- Retreat itself is real work — book with intent, structure the outcomes, no drift into party.
- Debrief with executive coach the following week.

### 9.7 Media interviews
- 30-minute maximum for solo interviews. Longer means fatigue, longer means quotable errors.
- 90-minute maximum with prep and debrief included.
- Head of Comms attends every non-trivial interview. Prep is real; do not wing it.
- No live TV without a rehearsal the day before.
- No hostile interviewers accepted during the first 12 months of public phase without board-level sign-off.

## 10. Warning signs — when to escalate

These are the tripwires. If any of them fire, take the specified action. Do not talk yourself out of it. The point of writing them down when you are well is so that you cannot rewrite them when you are not.

- **Sleep drops below 6 hours for 5 or more consecutive nights.** → Call your therapist. Confirmed within 48 hours.
- **Alcohol daily for 5 or more consecutive days.** → Call your therapist. Confirmed within 48 hours.
- **Withdrawal from wife for 7 or more days** (defined: no real conversation, no touch, no shared time beyond logistics). → Emergency date night this week + therapist call.
- **Irritability at kids for 3 or more days in a row** (yelling, sarcasm, withdrawal). → Therapist call. Discussion with wife.
- **Loss of interest in things you normally enjoy** (reading, workouts, movement, cooking, sex) for 2 weeks. → Therapist appointment within the week.
- **Recurring intrusive thought that Hearth failing would be a relief.** → Immediate therapist call. Not next week; today.
- **Ideation about disappearing, running away, or "letting it fail."** → Immediate therapist emergency line + wife + board chair, in that order. Same day.
- **Suicidal ideation, plan, or intent.** → 988 or emergency room. Wife informed. Board chair informed by wife or therapist. **There is no version of this that reflects badly on you. There is only the version where you get help fast enough.**
- **Panic attacks recurring more than 2x per month.** → Therapist. Cardiology check if novel.
- **Weight change (in either direction) of 10+ lbs in 60 days without intent.** → Physician + therapist.
- **Wife or executive coach tells you to escalate.** → Escalate. Trust the outside observer over your internal narrator.

### Explicit contingency operating protocol

If the founder becomes unable to work for more than 5 business days for any reason:
- **Head of Comms notifies the board chair.** No detail required beyond "the CEO is taking a health absence."
- **Board chair activates the contingency operating plan** per FOUNDER-NARRATIVE Team-of-One §. Head of Ops and Head of Comms hold operational continuity.
- **No public disclosure of health absence** unless the founder consents.
- **Investors are not informed** unless the absence exceeds 15 business days, at which point the board chair delivers a scripted, non-diagnostic message.
- **Family is protected from investor contact** during any health absence. Board chair is the sole channel.

This plan exists so that using it is not a career-defining event. It exists because sometimes people get sick. Using it is a sign of the maturity of the operation, not the fragility of the founder.

## 11. Compensation for wife

This section is written for both of you.

Your wife is not a Hearth employee, and it is important that she stay not-an-employee. But the load she carries is real, and it deserves material acknowledgment, not just words at the year-end party. The mechanics below have real tax + employment-law implications and must be structured through the HOUSEHOLD BUDGET (not as company benefits) unless employment counsel + a personal CPA sign off in advance. The framework:

**Legal review requirement (do this before invoking any bullet below).** Route the compensation-to-spouse framework past Hearth's employment counsel + Mark's personal CPA before locking it. Direct third-party compensation from Hearth to a founder's spouse can create §162 deductibility issues, constructive receipt / imputed income to Mark, or in some states joint-employer / gift-tax exposure. The safe defaults for each bullet below:

- **Paid family leave equivalent (structured as household budget line).** If your wife needs to reduce or pause her own work to hold the family through a Hearth crunch period (Series A close, product launch, medical absence), her lost income is covered from the HOUSEHOLD BUDGET (funded by Mark's post-tax compensation, not a company benefit). Amount: up to 12 weeks of her documented earnings in any 12-month period, capped at a pre-agreed household reserve figure.
- **Therapy and coaching costs.** Ideally covered through **Hearth's actual group health plan** (Anthem PPO or equivalent) if the spouse is a dependent under Mark's plan — that's a legit non-taxable benefit under §106. Anything OUTSIDE the group plan (a boutique out-of-network coach, an executive coach) is paid from post-tax household funds, not from Hearth directly. Do not blur the line.
- **Professional development budget: up to $10,000 per year.** Paid from HOUSEHOLD BUDGET (post-tax) for certifications, courses, conferences, career pivot support. Not a Hearth reimbursement. No approval required beyond her own.
- **Emergency fund separate from family finance.** Held in her name, in a HYSA she controls. Minimum balance: 3 months of family expenses. Funded from Mark's post-tax compensation, treated as a household-financial-autonomy principle. Target $30,000 by end of Year 1; sustained thereafter. NOT a Hearth-funded transfer — that would create gift/comp tax treatment issues.
- **Childcare budget for retreat weekends and travel weeks.** Paid from household budget when related to Mark's personal recovery; expensed to Hearth only when directly attributable to a Hearth business trip that Mark is on (subject to standard T&E policy). None of this substitutes for the actual work of the marriage. It is a floor. The relationship is the ceiling. And every dollar movement in this section is reviewed by employment counsel + personal CPA before it moves.

## 12. Post-exit and long-term

The public phase is not the whole life. This is the piece of the document that plans for what comes after.

- **After Series A close.** Mental health stack continues at current cadence. Do not cut therapy because you feel better. You feel better because of the therapy.
- **After Series B.** Same stack, plus a re-evaluation. The company is bigger; the load is different; the practices adjust to match. New baseline check-in at that milestone (§2 abbreviated version).
- **After exit (acquisition, IPO, wind-down — any resolution).** 6-month sabbatical or the closest equivalent the acquiring entity permits. This is not a reward; it is a recovery. Founders who go straight from exit to next-thing burn out at a higher rate than founders who take the interval.
- **Post-sabbatical re-entry.** With your therapist, with your wife, with your executive coach. What comes next is your choice — but it should be a choice, made from a rested and reflective place, not a reaction to identity vacuum.
- **Legacy recognition.** Whatever Hearth becomes, it does not become the total measure of your life. Write down now, in the journal, three things about your life that are already true and would still be true if Hearth failed tomorrow. Keep the list. Look at it in Year 3.
