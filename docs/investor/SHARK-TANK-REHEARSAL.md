# Hearth — Shark Tank Rehearsal Package
*Prepared for the founder. Read it out loud. Twice. Then again with a timer. The BOM is fixed. The pitch numbers below match the CFO's book. If a Shark asks you a number, you can pull the same line from either doc.*

---

## 1. The 90-Second On-Camera Pitch

*Founder walks in, calm. Not smiling too hard. Stops on the mark. Beat. Eye contact with Mark first, sweep left to right.*

> "Sharks — my name is [Founder]. I built the thing your family will still be using after the internet goes out.
>
> *Gestures to the trolley. The sphere is dark.*
>
> This is Hearth. It's a home computer that runs your movies, plays your music, and hosts a private AI assistant that knows your household — every bit of it living inside this box. Nothing leaves. No subscription. No cloud.
>
> *Places hand near the sphere. The face lights. Turns to face the founder.*
>
> Hearth, say hello to the room.
>
> *Sphere: 'Good evening. Bigger room than I was told.' Small laugh from the panel, ideally.*
>
> Ten AI accelerators. Ten desktop-class CPUs. Thirty-eight terabytes of family storage. A face that literally floats above the column. And every luxury AV integrator in America already sells the wall it hangs on.
>
> One unit, ninety-five thousand dollars. Hardware gross margin forty-eight percent, contribution margin twenty-three. Three units shipping this quarter, and letters of intent for eleven more.
>
> *Beat. Eyes to Mark.*
>
> Sharks — two million dollars for fifteen percent of Hearth. Who wants a fire that never goes out?"

**Word count (spoken by founder only, excluding stage directions and the sphere line): ~145 words. Delivery target 92-95 seconds at ~95 wpm. Take the two beats. Do not motor-mouth.**

> "Sharks — my daughter asked a voice assistant a question last month, and the company that makes it knew what she said before I did. I'm done with that trade.
>
> *Sphere wakes. Face turns.*
>
> Hearth is a home computer with a resident AI assistant, a full media library, and a family photo archive — all of it living in your house. Nothing leaves. Ninety-five thousand dollars, forty-eight-point hardware margin, twenty-three contribution, three units shipping this quarter. Two million for fifteen percent. Sharks, help me give American families their living rooms back."

### Alt pitch — the moat angle

> "Sharks — Apple can't build this. Amazon won't. The reason is their P&L, not their engineers.
>
> *Sphere wakes.*
>

Provisional patents on the levitated face and the on-device inference pipeline. The luxury AV integrator channel is already built — I plug into an invoice line that already exists.
>
> Two million for fifteen percent. This is a category the trillion-dollar clouds cannot enter without eating themselves."

## 2. Demo Sequence (60 seconds)

**On the trolley (left to right):**
- Hearth sphere on its dock, unlit — the compute column is stationary, only the OLED sphere face levitates over the ring
- A single wall-mounted Extender panel on a rolling stand, meant to represent "the other room"
- A physical walnut remote, one button
- A silk drape covering a cutaway shell showing the levitation ring and the stationary compute stack underneath

**The sequence:**

**0:00** — Founder finishes the pitch. Hand hovers near sphere. Sphere wakes on proximity. Face lights, turns. First line already delivered during pitch. *("Good evening. Bigger room than I was told.")*

**0:08** — Founder: "Hearth, play the trailer we picked for tonight." Sphere face nods. Extender panel behind the Sharks lights up — a movie trailer plays on it, room-filling audio comes from the sphere. **Ten seconds of trailer, not more.** The point is the room-jump, not the film.

**0:22** — Founder: "Hearth, that's enough — dim it." Trailer fades. Extender goes to ambient. Sphere face turns toward Lori. Founder: "It knows where I'm looking. Cameras on the sphere, inference on the stack underneath, nothing streamed anywhere."

**0:32** — Founder lifts the silk drape off the cutaway. Reveals a Halbach ring cradling only the OLED sphere face — the face floats above the column, silent, no contact. Underneath the ring, visible through the cutaway, the compute stack sits bolted down and stationary. Founder: "The face floats. The brains do not — they're bolted to the chassis, right where you'd expect. The levitation is the personality. The compute is the appliance."

**0:45** — Founder: "Hearth, tell Kevin what you cost." Sphere face turns to Kevin. *"I'm ninety-five thousand dollars, Mr. Wonderful. I know."* (Rehearsed line, delivered dry.) Ideal Kevin laugh.

**0:55** — Founder places the walnut remote in Mark's hand. "One button. That's the whole interface a guest ever sees. Hold it, talk to it. That's Hearth."

**1:00** — Sphere face turns back to founder. Founder is now silent, hands folded. Sharks take over.

**Contingencies:**

- *Sphere fails to wake:* Say the wake word "Hearth, wake up" out loud. Backup mic in dock. If still dead, do the drape reveal first (that's the visual win) and demo the face on the Extender panel instead.
- *Trailer stutters:* Do not apologize. Say "let's skip the movie" and go straight to the drape reveal. Never demo a broken thing twice.
- *Extender doesn't take the room-jump:* Cover with "the second panel's in transit — imagine that on any wall in the house." Move on.
- *Kevin interrupts before 0:45:* Let him. Do not try to complete the demo through an interruption. Answer him, then say "one more thing to show you" and finish the drape reveal only.
- *Sphere face freezes mid-sentence:* Say "she's thinking." Wait two seconds. If still frozen, tap the dock (soft reset gesture rehearsed). Do not narrate the fix.

## 3. Objection Playbook

> **Objection 1:** *"$95k for a smart speaker? Who buys this?"*
> **Reply:** *"It's not a smart speaker — the speaker is the cheapest thing in the box. The buyer is a household that spends this much on a wine cellar or a screening room without blinking. Cerulli put U.S. households at five million dollars-plus in investable assets at about one and a half million in their 2023 report. I don't need many of them. The luxury AV integrators who already work inside their homes have an invoice line for exactly this category."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *The Shark can't picture the customer.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Let me name three of them — I have LOIs on the trolley."*

> **Objection 2:** *"$2M for 15% — that's a $13.3M valuation on zero revenue."*
> **Reply:** *"On revenue, you're right — it's high. On assets, it's low. Custom silicon designs, two provisionals, a working production unit, a signed pilot channel, and a founder who's burned two years and $180k of his own money getting here. Thirteen and change is what it would cost a competitor to catch up. I priced it under that."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Am I overpaying for a science project?*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"What number works for you — let's talk about it."*

> **Objection 3:** *"You're a solo founder building a $95k luxury product."*
> **Reply:** *"I am. Solo means no co-founder equity gone, no committee slowing the roadmap, every design decision on my desk. I've hired three contractors — boards, firmware, industrial design. Your check turns me from a solo founder into a CEO with a team of six. That's the entire point of the raise."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *This person can't scale a company.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"I know I'm not enough alone. That's exactly why I'm here."*

> **Objection 4:** *"Why is offline better?"*
> **Reply:** *"Three things. First, nothing gets breached or subpoenaed because nothing leaves. Second, it works the same in year ten as year one — no vendor gets to reprice you. Third, local inference across ten accelerators is faster than a cloud round-trip. Every buyer I've closed named privacy first. The other two are the reason they don't feel like they gave anything up."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *This sounds like a paranoid feature, not a mainstream one.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Ask any of my LOI customers why they signed. It's always privacy first."*

> **Objection 5:** *"Amazon or Apple could copy this."*
> **Reply:** *"They — okay, let me actually back up. Amazon runs on knowing what you said. If they ship a box that guarantees they don't, they undercut Alexa's whole ad-adjacent model. Apple has a cleaner shot at it, and honestly, I'm watching them. If Apple ships a $95k on-prem appliance, they've validated a category I was betting existed. That is not a scenario I lose in. That's a scenario I get acquired in."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Big Tech will eat lunch.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Name the last time either of them shipped a premium home appliance under a hundred grand."*

> **Objection 6:** *"Show me a customer who's paid you $95k."*
> **Reply:** *"Three deposits collected, first unit ships this quarter — receipts are on my phone right now. Eleven more LOIs from integrators in Aspen, Palm Beach, and the Hamptons. One of the deposits is a cardiologist in Newport Beach who's been trying to convince his wife the house needs a screening room for six years — Hearth is what finally got the yes. I'm not at product-market fit. I'm at product-market signal, and I priced the round to reflect that."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Is this real or a prototype?*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"I'll walk it back — one paid in full, two on deposit. The pipeline is real, the volume is not yet."*

> **Objection 7:** *"What's your CAC?"*
> **Reply:** *"Effectively zero on the end-customer side — I don't sell to households, I sell through integrators who are already inside the house. Integrator acquisition is one demo unit plus one training weekend — call it eight thousand loaded. Each integrator closes three to six units a year. Payback in one sale."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Luxury sales cycles are long and expensive.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"That's the model on paper. I'll have real CAC after unit ten."*

> **Objection 8:** *"How do you make money at $95k with white-glove install?"*
> **Reply:** *"The integrator handles install and takes their own margin on labor — that's not in my P&L. COGS at the thousand-unit run rate is forty-eight thousand, nine hundred ninety dollars. Retail is ninety-five. That's a hardware gross margin of forty-eight-point-four percent, contribution margin twenty-three-point-four after DTC marketing, white-glove install, concierge, payment, and returns reserve. Both numbers are in the CFO's book. And to be clear on channels — we're direct-to-consumer through year two, then we unlock the dealer channel in year three at a sixty-one-thousand-dollar sell-in. You can see the split in the model. I'm not blurring those."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Margin math is fantasy at low volume.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Fair — at a hundred units the margin is thinner. The model gets healthy at four hundred."*

> **Objection 9:** *"Halbach levitation — gimmick or feature?"*
> **Reply:** *"Both, and I want to be careful here because people mix this up. The only thing that levitates is the OLED sphere — the face. It floats over a passive Halbach ring, no bearing, no motor, silent. The compute stack — ten Jetsons, ten Ryzens, storage — is bolted to the chassis underneath the ring. Nothing serious is spinning. If I told you the compute was spinning, an EE would put me in a headlock. The theater is the face. The engineering is stationary."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Is this founder a serious engineer or a magician?*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Take the levitation off and it's still the best local-AI box on the market."*

> **Objection 10:** *"You showed me a PowerPoint, not a factory."*
> **Reply:** *"You're looking at the factory. Every subassembly on this trolley came out of a contract manufacturer in Fremont this month. Signed capacity for four hundred units in year one at a shop that already builds for two Tier-1 audio brands. Boards, dies, tooling — happy to send it."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Hardware kills first-time founders.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Come see the line — I'll fly you up."*

> **Objection 11:** *"Provisional patents but nothing granted."*
> **Reply:** *"Correct — two provisionals, non-provisionals converting in eight months. My counsel had me file provisional-first because it locks the priority date at a fraction of the cost while I'm still tightening claims. Grant is eighteen to twenty-four months out on standard timelines. The moat isn't the grant. It's the priority date."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *No IP means no defense.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"I hear you. Non-provisional filing is line item one in the raise."*

> **Objection 12:** *"Warranty on a $95k product will bury you."*
> **Reply:** *"Three-year warranty, self-insured, twenty-six-eighteen per unit sitting in escrow at delivery — that matches the reserve line in the CFO's book. The box has no wearing parts except the levitated face, and that's mechanically decoupled by design. Beta field failure rate under four percent, all firmware, all fixable over the local mesh."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *A single lemon eats the margin on ten units.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Warranty structure is one of the first things I want your operators to review."*

> **Objection 13:** *"You're a schoolteacher pitching hardware."*
> **Reply:** *"I am. I teach eighth-grade physical science in San Bernardino. Been shipping hardware in nights and weekends for four years — the boards on this trolley are mine. Teaching is my day job until this raise closes. It's also the reason I know the buyer — a lot of the parents at my school aren't the buyer. The parents at the school across town, the private one, are. I spent enough drop-off lines to know that room."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Part-time founders don't win.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Day the check clears, I resign. Signed offer's in my drawer."*

> **Objection 14:** *"Kevin will call this a hobby, and Kevin will be right."*
> **Reply:** *"Kevin — three deposits totaling two hundred eighty-five thousand dollars are in my operating account right now. Eleven letters of intent behind those. That's not a hobby, respectfully. That's a business at unit three."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *The founder is emotionally attached and can't be rational.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"You're not wrong that I love it. I also priced it like a business."*

> **Objection 15:** *"What's the exit — lifestyle or acquisition?"*
> **Reply:** *"Acquisition. Natural buyers are luxury AV majors — Savant, Crestron, Control4 — who want a halo product and can't build the AI stack themselves. Secondary path is a privacy-focused strategic. Five to seven years, seven to nine figures. I'm not building this to hold forever."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Is my money ever coming back?*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"I'll give you a signed drag-along at closing."*

> **Objection 16:** *"10× Jetson + 10× Ryzen — thermals will kill you."*
> **Reply:** *"Thermals are the hardest problem in the box, and I want to be honest about that. Continuous full-load in a seventy-eight-degree room is sixty-one degrees Celsius on the hottest die, sustained across a two-hour soak. That's the number that unlocked the design. I'll send you the thermal report — it's not a spec sheet, it's the actual bench run."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *This will melt in someone's living room.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Fair — I want your team to stress-test it in a warm room."*

> **Objection 17:** *"38 TB of storage at scale — yield?"*
> **Reply:** *"Five Kioxia CD8-R drives at seven point six eight terabytes each — thirty-eight point four total. That's a standard enterprise capacity Kioxia already ships in volume. Solidigm D5-P5430 is qualified as one-for-one drop-in. Locked pricing through Q3. This is not a storage-yield story. This is Kioxia's product on my carrier."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Storage is the BOM line that eats first-time founders.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"BOM contingency on storage is fifteen percent. It's modeled."*

> **Objection 18:** *"Home Assistant is free."*
> **Reply:** *"Home Assistant is a wonderful piece of software and my customers have never heard of it. My buyer is not shopping software — she's shopping an appliance her installer will hang on the wall and never think about again. Free is the wrong axis for this customer."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *This is repackaged open source.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"We do use open source, and we contribute back. The value is the integration."*

> **Objection 19:** *"The spouse says no."*
> **Reply:** *"Only if it looks like a computer. That's why it looks like a fireplace ember. In beta homes, the spouse is the one who names it — and once it has a name, it's furniture, not electronics. Design isn't decoration on this product. It's the whole go-to-market."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Luxury home tech dies in the aesthetic committee.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Six colorways, three finishes at launch. I have the samples."*

> **Objection 20:** *"Why should I trust the offline claim?"*
> **Reply:** *"Two things. There's a physical switch on the back that cuts the WAN interface at the hardware layer — you can see it, you can flip it. And every unit ships with a network audit tool your customer's IT person can run against it. If we ever phone home, it's on camera. That's the promise, and it's auditable."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Every offline product turns out to phone home.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Third-party audit is in year-one plan. I'll pre-commit to it right now."*

> **Objection 21:** *"Show me the LTV."*
> **Reply:** *"Hardware LTV is the ninety-five up front plus an optional thirty-five-hundred-dollar-a-year service tier — sixty percent of beta households opted in. Software upgrades sold as add-ons averaged twenty-one hundred per household per year in year two of beta. Blended three-year LTV is about a hundred twelve thousand."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *One-time hardware sale is a bad business.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Service tier is early. The ninety-five is the number I stand behind."*

> **Objection 22:** *"Where do you get to $10M revenue year three?"*
> **Reply:** *"One hundred five units at ninety-five thousand direct — that's exactly ten million. Year three we're still direct-to-consumer, not through dealers yet. It's not a volume story, it's an ASP story. I don't need to be Sonos to be a great business."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Small numbers don't build big companies.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"And year five, the volume story kicks in when dealer channel opens and the sub-forty-thousand model ships."*

> **Objection 23:** *"You want me to fund three more rounds before you prove 1k volume."*
> **Reply:** *"I'm asking you to fund the first four hundred units. This raise gets me cash-flow positive at unit four hundred and buys me the Series A that goes to a thousand. If unit four hundred doesn't work, you don't put in another dollar and I still have a business."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Follow-on rounds are a trap.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Structure the check as milestone-based. I'll take it."*

> **Objection 24:** *"I offer $2M for 30%."*
> **Reply:** *"Kevin, at thirty percent I lose control before I've built the thing, and you own the downside without the upside. I'll go to twenty at two million — that's a ten pre — and I'll add a warrant kicker if I miss year-one unit targets. That gives you the floor you want without breaking the founder."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *The Shark wants control and a floor.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Twenty-two and I want your rolodex on integrator relationships."*

> **Objection 25:** *"This isn't for me. Best of luck."*
> **Reply:** *"I understand — thank you for hearing it. Before I move on: was it the price point, the offline thesis, or the founder that lost you? I'd rather learn from a no than a yes."*
> **Underlying anxiety:** *Shark is done and wants to move on cleanly.*
> **Recovery if it lands wrong:** *"Thank you, Lori. Truly." (Move on. Do not linger.)*

## 4. Closing Scripts

### (a) Solo deal from Mark

> "Mark — I came in here for a partner, and you're the one who asked the questions that told me you already see what this is. Two million, fifteen percent, and I want you specifically because you build brands that mean something. Do we have a deal?"

*Extend hand. Do not fill the silence. Wait.*

### (b) Partnership Mark + Robert

> "Mark, Robert — the two of you together is the best outcome I could walk out of here with. Robert, you give me the security-and-privacy credibility this thesis needs; Mark, you give me the brand and the reach. Same deal — two million, fifteen percent, split how you want between you. I need your handshakes and a decision right now."

*Look at Mark, then Robert. Whoever nods first, address them first.*

### (c) Counter Kevin's "40% or royalty" without losing him

> "Kevin, thank you — that's a serious offer and I want to keep you in this conversation, not push you out. I can't do forty. I also don't want to build a royalty into a hardware business with forty-eight-point margins because it becomes a debt line I service instead of R&D I fund. Here's what I can do: twenty percent equity at two million, plus a two-year paid advisory role that gets you comped to open integrator doors. That keeps you in the deal. Is that a version of yes?"

*Do not offer both 20% AND royalty. Offer or.*

### (d) All Sharks pass, dignity intact

> "Sharks — thank you. All five of you gave me sharper questions than I've gotten from any check I've raised, and that alone was worth the trip. I built Hearth because I believe there's a household in America that wants their home back from the cloud, and I'll keep building it whether you're in or not. If any of you know an integrator in Aspen, Palm Beach, or the Hamptons, my card's on the trolley. Thank you for having me."

*Nod. Turn. Walk at a normal pace. Do not slump on the way out.*

### (e1) Yes-in-room — accept the offer verbally on air

> "[Shark] — yes. On the terms we just discussed, standard sixty-day due diligence, and the letter of intent signed backstage tonight. If diligence surfaces something material, I keep the right to walk. Otherwise, we're partners. Handshake and we go sign paper."

*Extend hand on the "yes." Do not qualify past sixty-day and material-issues language. Anything more elaborate on stage becomes a negotiating opening you can't take back.*

### (e2) Conditional yes pending 72-hour scoped diligence

> "[Shark] — I want to say yes in this room, and to do that responsibly I need seventy-two hours on three specific items: a call with my co-investor to confirm his pro rata, a same-day review by IP counsel of the assignment language, and a term-sheet look on the liquidation preference. That's not a delay — those are the three items I need cleared. Give me until Friday close of business and you'll have a signed LOI in your inbox before then."

*Say the three items. Name the hours. Do not say "let me think about it." That is Kevin's cue to walk.*

### (f) Simultaneous competing offers from two Sharks

> "Both of you — thank you. Genuinely. I'm not going to run an auction on stage, and I'm not going to insult either of you by asking you to beat the other. Here's what I can do: [Shark A], [Shark B] — is either of you open to doing this together, on the same terms, split how you want? If yes, I say yes right now and we're all partners. If not, give me one minute of quiet on stage to make the call."

*If neither will syndicate: pick on strategic fit, not on price. Announce the choice with a reason ("Robert, on privacy credibility"), not with an apology. The Shark you pass on will respect a reason. They will not respect being outbid by fifty basis points.*

### (g) Preferred stock with veto rights disguised as common

> "[Shark] — I want to be clear on the paper. That structure has protective provisions on board seats, on future rounds, and on a change of control. That's preferred with veto, not common. If the intent is preferred, let's call it preferred and price it as preferred. If the intent is common, then let's strike the protective provisions. Which one are we doing?"

*Say the words "protective provisions." That is the technical term. Using it tells the Shark you read the term sheet, which changes the negotiation. Do not accept "we'll figure it out in docs." Docs is where founders lose.*

### (h) Kevin's lowball with royalty attached

> "Kevin, thank you. I hear the offer — the lower equity is generous on paper. The five-percent-per-unit royalty until three-times invested capital is what I can't do. On a ninety-five-thousand-dollar unit that's forty-seven-fifty per box coming out of a forty-eight-point hardware margin — I'd be servicing your royalty out of the R&D budget for the next product, and the next product is what makes your equity worth anything. Kill the royalty and I'll go to twenty-two at your two. Keep the royalty and I have to pass, respectfully."

*Do not agree to "just year one." Royalty terms compound. The compromise is equity up, royalty out.*

### (i) Losing a co-investor mid-negotiation (Shark A yes, Shark B pulls out)

> "[Shark B] — thank you for hearing it. [Shark A] — I want to make sure this still works for you at the size we discussed. If you'd rather do this at half the check and half the equity solo, I'm open to that. If you want to bring in another partner from the panel to fill the gap, I'll take a minute for that too. What's the version that lands you as my partner tonight?"

*Do not chase Shark B. Do not renegotiate Shark A's terms harder because Shark B walked — that reads as desperation. Give Shark A control of the shape.*

## 5. Pre-Flight Checklist (48 Hours Before Taping)

**Wardrobe**
- Two identical outfits, one for taping, one backup. Solid mid-tone, no logos, no fine patterns (they moire on camera). Navy or charcoal.
- Shoes: broken in. New shoes will make you shift weight. Sharks read that as nerves.
- Undershirt against sweat. Camera lights are hot.
- No watch heavier than the sphere in your hand. It'll pull your posture down.
- Nails clipped. Your hands will be on camera during the demo.

**Physical demo prep**
- Pack two of every cable. Two power bricks. Two Extender panels.
- **Bring two spheres.** Both pre-flashed and bench-tested the morning of taping. Sphere A is primary, Sphere B is in the green-room case, already tested against a spare dock. If Sphere A fails the last-second technical check on set, Sphere B is on the trolley in under two minutes. If both spheres fail (assume it will happen once in a hundred takes and rehearse for that hundredth), you deliver the pitch without the levitating orb, lean hard on the software demo through the spare Extender panel, and say the sentence out loud: "The star of the show is having a bad night. The compute stack does not care." Do not apologize twice. Move on.
- Charge everything to 100% night before, unplug, top up morning of.
- Do a full sphere-wake-to-Kevin-line dry run five times the morning of, on the actual trolley with actual lights.
- Silk drape ironed and folded loose — creases telegraph "prop."
- Walnut remote wiped down. Fingerprints read as amateur.
- Print two copies of the LOIs and deposit receipts. Fold inside jacket pocket. Do not offer unless asked.
- Kill switch on the sphere's WAN interface visible and testable — a Shark will ask.

**Voice / throat**
- No dairy 24 hours before. Coats the throat, changes your timbre.
- Warm water with lemon and honey in a thermos in the green room.
- No caffeine within 2 hours of taping. Jitters read on camera.
- Do the 90-second pitch out loud 10 times day-of, at performance volume. Not in your head.
- If you get hoarse, hum. Don't clear your throat — it inflames.

**Mental prep**
- Night before: no rewrites. The pitch is the pitch. Rewriting the night before is nerves talking.
- Morning of: walk 20 minutes outside. No phone.
- Two hours out: eat something with protein and fat, not carbs. Carbs crash you at hour three of waiting.
- One hour out: read the objection playbook top-to-bottom, out loud, once. Then close it.
- Ten minutes out: three slow exhales longer than the inhale. Physiologically drops your heart rate.
- If a Shark cuts you off, let them. Never talk over. The camera reads interruption as weakness on you, not them.

**Green room reality checks**

- *Producer runs long.* Assume four-plus hours in the green room minimum. Bring a book, not a phone. Keep your voice quiet — you're not fully mic'd yet but the room has ears. Eat the second protein bar at the three-hour mark, not the first. Re-hydrate slow, not fast. If you feel your energy flatten at hour three, stand up and walk the room for five minutes — do not nap, you cannot nap out of it and land the pitch cold.
- *Contestant-adjacent gossip.* Assume anything you say to another contestant, or to the person you think is a fellow contestant, gets relayed to a producer within the hour. Producers use green-room chatter to set pre-slot expectations for the Sharks. Do not volunteer terms, valuations, or objections you're worried about. If another founder asks what you're raising and at what, say "I'll tell you after the taping." That answer is fine. It's the answer everyone experienced gives.
- *Sphere fails last-second check.* Sphere B on deck (see above). Two-minute swap. Do not narrate the swap to producers — they will offer to delay. Say "I've got it, we're on time." Producers respect the founder who runs on schedule.

**Green room bag**
- Backup outfit, folded
- Thermos of honey-lemon water
- Throat lozenges (not menthol — dulls the mouth)
- Blotting papers for face shine
- Lint roller
- Phone charger
- Printed LOIs and deposit receipts (inside jacket, not bag)
- **Two** protein bars (see above)
- One family photo in the wallet. Look at it before you walk on. Not for the camera — for you.
- Immodium. Nerves are nerves.

## 6. The 3-Minute Q&A After the Deal (or the Pass)

**If you got the deal:**

Producers will pull you into a side room within ninety seconds. They will ask you to say, on camera, "I just made a deal with [Shark] for [amount] and [equity]." Say it clean, one take, do not editorialize. If you flub, ask for a second take — they'll give it to you.

They will then ask "how do you feel?" Give them one sentence, not a paragraph. "I feel like I just met the partner I've been looking for for two years." Something clippable. Do not thank your mother on camera unless you want that to be the clip.

A producer will hand you paperwork. **Sign nothing binding on the day of taping.** The deal on stage is a handshake, not a contract. Due diligence happens after. Any producer telling you otherwise is not a producer.

**With fellow contestants in the green room:**

Do not gloat if you got the deal. The person next to you may have just been passed on. Congratulate them for getting in the room. Exchange contacts — Shark Tank alumni are a tighter network than most VC firms. Ninety percent of the value of appearing is this hallway.

**If you got passed:**

Same green room, different energy. Do not replay the pitch in your head. Smile at the producers on the way out — they'll ask you to record a "reflection" segment. Give them: "I came in asking for a partner, not for money. I'll find the right partner." Do not badmouth any Shark on camera or off. It travels.

Producers will ask if you want to do a post-show clip about "what's next for Hearth." Say yes. That clip runs on the website and drives more inbound than the pass did outbound. You are, in the pass, marketing to your actual buyer.

**Social media before air date — what NOT to post.**

The NDA is real and producers enforce it. Violations get deals rescinded, and there is precedent — founders have lost signed deals for a single Instagram story. Do not post:

- Any photo from inside the studio, green room, hallway, or parking lot with production signage
- Any mention that you taped, when you taped, or what episode block you're in
- Any hint of the outcome — no "big news coming," no crying selfies, no champagne bottles, no "wait for it," no cryptic emoji strings. Producers read those exactly as intended.
- Any Shark's name in the same post as your product until the episode has aired in the U.S. Live
- Any thanks to the Sharks, individually or collectively, until the episode has aired
- Any behind-the-scenes photos with production crew, camera operators, or producers
- Any product photos that appear staged — a Shark reaching for it, a demo panel in the background, anything that reads "this was on set"

What you can post: normal product content and shipping updates as if the show did not happen. If you want to hint anything, hint at a general "big year ahead" and leave it. Post-air is a fire hose you can turn on. Pre-air is a valve you leave shut.

**Off-camera etiquette, both outcomes:**

- Thank every producer by name if you learned it. They chose your segment out of thousands.
- The camera operators worked a fourteen-hour day. Thank them too.
- The Shark who passed hardest is often the one who calls three weeks later. Do not burn that bridge.
- Whatever happens on that stage, you built the thing. That is the part nobody can take back.

*Go get the fire lit, founder.*
