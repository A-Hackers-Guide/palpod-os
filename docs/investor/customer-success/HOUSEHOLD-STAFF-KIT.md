## 1. The household staff relationship

Every Hearth ships to a household that is, functionally, a small organization. There is an owner, and there are the people who make the owner's life work: the housekeeper who has been with the family for eleven years, the property manager who runs the Palm Beach compound six months of the year, the nanny who is the person the seven-year-old actually asks when he can't sleep, the personal chef who plans Tuesday dinner around the daughter's soccer schedule. When we install a Hearth in a $5M+ household, we are not installing it into the owner's life alone. We are installing it into a workplace with a small staff of professionals whose careers are built on knowing this house better than anyone else does — including, on any given day, the owner.

We build staff-facing materials because the housekeeper is the customer's proxy. She is the person who signs for the delivery when the crate arrives on the driveway. She is the person who calls our concierge line when the sphere has been sitting silently for three hours and no one can figure out why. She authorizes the service technician into the house when the extender fails at 6 a.m. and the owner is on a plane. She is the one dusting around the Halbach ring and deciding whether to use the microfiber cloth or the reflex reach for glass cleaner. If she does not know that ammonia damages the OLED, we own that damage. If she doesn't know the ring is a magnetic hazard, we own that injury. If she thinks Hearth is a nuisance — one more thing to work around, one more thing that will get her yelled at when it breaks — she will tell the owner, quietly and consistently, and Hearth will be gone within six months.

Beta signal suggests that staff sentiment is a leading indicator of household retention. This is directionally consistent with what luxury AV integrators (Josh.ai, Control4) have reported informally for a decade, and it matches the common-sense observation that the person who dusts a device weekly forms an opinion of it that eventually reaches the person who bought it. We are not yet in a position to publish a defensible retention delta with sample size, methodology, and confidence interval; formal measurement will launch Q3 2027 with an LP-visible NPS methodology, prospective cohort tracking, and third-party survey administration. Until then, we treat the staff relationship as strategically load-bearing on judgment and craft, not on a number.

The staff onboarding program is built on three commitments we make in writing and in practice: (1) staff receive their own materials, their own contact channel, and their own training — not a hand-me-down of the owner's; (2) staff time is compensated through the household employer, at the household's standard rate, so that no one has to navigate a third-party pay stub; (3) staff privacy is protected from the owner, not just from the outside world. Every material in this kit follows from those three commitments.

## 2. Roles served

Hearth serves six household staff archetypes. Each gets a role-specific one-page cheat sheet inside the master Household Staff Guide, plus a role-specific fifteen-minute follow-up session in the week after install.

**Housekeeper (full-time; typically one or two per household).** The daily-interaction anchor. Cleans in and around the sphere and the extenders, receives and stages deliveries, coordinates with the concierge on service visits, and is generally the first person to notice when something is wrong. Authorized to: clean per protocol, sign for shipments after verifying seals, contact concierge for any issue, use her own voice profile for basic sphere interaction (media, timers, "call the concierge"). Not authorized to: authorize service technician entry without owner or property-manager sign-off, add guest voice profiles, disable the sphere, receive replacement units without concierge coordination. Escalation path: concierge first, always, twenty-four hours a day.

**Property manager (large estates; often one per property, or one across several properties).** Full operational authority delegated by the owner. On a Palm Beach compound or a Boston Back Bay townhouse being run while the owner is in Aspen, the property manager is functionally the owner for Hearth's purposes. Authorized to: everything the owner is authorized to do inside the house, including authorizing service visits, coordinating installs, making maintenance decisions, and adding guest voice profiles for household staff. Not authorized to: change owner-level privacy settings, add non-staff guest voice profiles without owner consent, decommission the sphere. Escalation path: concierge for operational matters, founder line for anything privacy-adjacent.

**Nanny (part-time or live-in).** The person who is with the children when the owner is not. Authorized to: use her own voice profile, invoke child-safety mode explicitly ("Hearth, kids are in the room"), turn off screens in shared spaces, trigger emergency-contact flow if a child is in distress. Not authorized to: modify child voice profiles (parental consent required, per Privacy & Compliance Manual §3.3), add children as household voices, disable sensitive-topic filtering. Escalation path: concierge for anything technical; direct household emergency protocol for child safety, with Hearth as secondary.

**Personal chef (typically two to three nights per week).** Uses Hearth primarily for kitchen displays, timers, recipe playback, music, and hands-free calls to grocery vendors or the household. Authorized to: kitchen-scoped voice interaction, control of kitchen extender displays, timers and recipe playback, hands-free calls within the owner-approved contact list. Not authorized to: guest voice add, sphere-face interaction, cleaning of the sphere or Halbach ring. Escalation path: concierge for anything beyond the kitchen extender.

**House sitter (seasonal residences; typically two to four weeks at a time).** Runs the house during owner absence. Authorized to: use a time-boxed voice profile (auto-expires at end of sitting engagement), interact with the sphere for security and household coordination, receive routine deliveries, coordinate scheduled service. Not authorized to: authorize unscheduled service entry, add guest voices, sign for replacement units without concierge on the line. Escalation path: concierge line and a dedicated house-sitter emergency number that reaches concierge and property manager simultaneously.

**Security detail (rare; only in the highest-risk HNW households).** Authorized to: device authentication for entry, guest access approvals under owner's pre-set rules, security-mode invocation. Not authorized to: any privacy setting, any child-related setting, or any voice-profile changes without owner. Escalation path: dedicated security-detail concierge line with two-minute SLA.

## 3. The Household Staff Guide — content specification

The Household Staff Guide is a twenty-six-page, six-by-nine-inch, linen-bound booklet with a matte foil-stamped Hearth mark on the cover. It is delivered in the install-day kit, one copy per household staff member, plus one master copy left in the house's operations binder. The paper is uncoated warm-white 100 gsm, the typography is set in a serif at 10.5 on 15, and the tone throughout is the tone of a respectful colleague — not a legal document, not a manual, not a marketing brochure. Below is the copy for every page.

### Pages 1–2 — Welcome + who we are

*Welcome.*

If you are reading this, you work in a household that has chosen to bring Hearth into its home. You are part of why this house works, and Hearth cannot work well in a house without the people who actually run that house. We wrote this guide for you specifically.

Hearth is a home computer. It looks like a sphere floating above a small ring on a shelf or console, and it listens for its name — the family will have chosen one. It plays music, answers questions, controls the household media, keeps calendars aligned, and connects to a private network the family uses. It is not a listening device for us. It is not a device that reports back on you. We designed it, from the beginning, so that the people who live and work in the house are the only ones who ever hear what happens in it. That includes you.

We built this booklet for you. It is not the owner's manual. It is the staff's book. It has your contact numbers, your privacy commitments, your cleaning protocols, and your role. Please keep it somewhere you can find it — in a drawer, in your bag, wherever you keep the things that matter to your work here.

If anything in this book is unclear, or if anything about Hearth ever feels wrong to you, call the concierge line on page 25. That line exists for you specifically. You do not need the owner's permission to use it. You do not need to explain why. It is yours.

Thank you for the care you bring to this household. Hearth is only as good as the people around it, and this house is fortunate to have you in it.

— The Hearth Customer Experience team

### Pages 3–4 — The sphere

The sphere is the visible part of Hearth. It is a small dark orb, roughly the size of a grapefruit, and it floats above a metal ring called the Halbach ring. It floats because the ring is a powerful arrangement of permanent magnets, and the sphere is designed to be suspended, silent, in the air above it. It is doing this all the time, quietly.

What it does: listens for the wake word, shows a face when spoken to, plays audio through the extenders, displays images and text when there is a screen nearby, coordinates household media and calendar, and connects family members and pre-approved staff to each other and to the concierge.

What it needs: a clean line of sight, a stable shelf, room-temperature air, and to be left alone when it is not being spoken to. That is essentially all.

What it does not need: to be dusted more than once a week, to be unplugged at night, to be picked up, or to be tested. The sphere is on. It has been on since install and will remain on. If it appears off, it is not off — it is resting. If it appears broken, please call the concierge and do not touch it.

An important note before you go further: the Halbach ring is a strong permanent magnet. If you have a pacemaker, an implanted defibrillator, an insulin pump, a cochlear implant, a deep brain stimulator, or any other implanted electronic medical device, please turn to page 15 and read the medical-implant safety page before continuing.

### Pages 5–8 — Daily interaction: the one-page cheat sheet by role

Each role gets a single facing page with a short list of what to do, what to say, and what to avoid. The layout is a two-column grid: DO on the left, DO NOT on the right, with a boxed "if in doubt, call concierge" line at the bottom.

*Housekeeper.* Do: dust the sphere face weekly with the provided anti-static microfiber cloth (dry); wipe the ring base with the provided cloth (dry); sign for scheduled deliveries after checking the seal; report anything unusual on the concierge line. Do not: use glass cleaner, ammonia, or alcohol on the sphere; place metal tools or jewelry on or near the Halbach ring; touch the sphere during levitation; sign for unscheduled deliveries. If in doubt, call concierge.

*Property manager.* Do: coordinate all service visits through the concierge; verify service tech credentials against the concierge-provided ticket; sign as agent-of-owner for replacement units after seal verification. Do not: authorize non-scheduled entry without concierge confirmation; add non-staff guests without owner consent; adjust privacy settings. If in doubt, call concierge.

*Nanny.* Do: use your voice profile freely; invoke "Hearth, kids in the room" to activate child-safe mode when children arrive; use the sphere to reach the parents quickly. Do not: add child voice profiles; disable filtering; discuss adult topics near the sphere when children are present. If in doubt, call concierge.

*Personal chef.* Do: use the kitchen extender for timers, recipes, music, and hands-free calls; place your voice profile once with the concierge. Do not: clean the sphere face or the ring; place magnetic knife strips or magnetic tools near the ring. If in doubt, call concierge.

*House sitter.* Do: verify daily that the sphere is present and responsive; use your time-boxed voice profile for household coordination; contact concierge for any service need. Do not: authorize any entry unless it is on the concierge-provided ticket; sign for anything unscheduled. If in doubt, call concierge.

*Security detail.* Do: use device authentication for entry approvals; use owner-defined guest rules; treat sphere as one of the household presence signals. Do not: interpret sphere silence as absence; treat sphere as a surveillance device. If in doubt, call concierge and the security-detail line.

### Pages 9–10 — Deliveries + shipping

Hearth sends things to the household on a regular basis: replacement extenders, cables, fabric wraps, wake-word audio boxes, and occasionally a full sphere. These arrive in a blue-void wrapped crate. The crate has an external seal and, once opened, an internal seal.

If the external seal is broken when the crate arrives on the driveway: do not sign, do not touch, do not accept. Call the concierge from the driveway before the courier leaves. We will handle it from there.

If the external seal is intact: sign, move the crate into a staging area (not directly into the home), and call the concierge to confirm receipt. The concierge will schedule an opening window with you.

If a delivery is scheduled and does not arrive: call concierge within twenty-four hours. Do not chase the courier yourself.

If a delivery arrives that was not scheduled: refuse it. Every Hearth delivery is scheduled by the concierge, and the concierge will tell you the day before what to expect. If it is not on your schedule, it is not ours.

Bagged items (small parts, cables) arrive in a four-hour window that the concierge sets with you. You do not need to be present the whole window; the courier will hold if you are twenty minutes out.

Replacement spheres and rings arrive in the same blue-void wrapper as the original product. The signature match is verified by the concierge at the time you sign — they will be on the phone with you.

Hearth crates can weigh more than 150 pounds. Do not attempt to lift or move a full sphere crate by yourself. The white-glove courier will place it in the staging area. If it needs to be relocated later, call the concierge and we will send a two-person crew. Handling gloves (cotton and nitrile) are provided in the household kit; wear them for any crate handling.

### Pages 11–12 — Cleaning around Hearth

The sphere face is OLED. It is beautiful and it is delicate. Moisture damages it. Ammonia damages it. Alcohol damages it. Glass cleaner damages it. Please use only the dry anti-static microfiber cloth provided in the household kit. Dust the sphere face once a week with light, straight strokes, not circular ones. If there is a smudge that will not come off dry, call the concierge. Do not experiment.

The Halbach ring is the small metal band the sphere floats above. It is a strong permanent magnet, arranged so that the field is directed upward toward the sphere. Do not place metal objects near it — no keys, no rings, no jewelry, no metal tools, no phones with magnetic cases. Do not insert anything into the ring or between the ring and the sphere. If you accidentally drop a metal object near the ring, do not reach into the field to retrieve it; call the concierge and we will guide you through it.

A note on static discharge. Triboelectric charge can accumulate on the sphere surface during levitation — this is the ordinary consequence of household dust settling on and lifting off from the sphere, and of nearby fabric contact. If you touch the sphere directly during levitation you may feel a mild static discharge, similar to shuffling across a rug. This is not the magnetic field; the field is a DC permanent-magnet field and does not shock you. The discharge is not dangerous, but we do not want you to experience it, and there is a simple reason we can prevent it entirely: the anti-static microfiber cloth we provide has a woven conductive thread that neutralizes surface charge safely as you dust. Always use the Hearth-provided cloth. Do not substitute a household cloth or a paper towel; without the anti-static thread, you will build charge on the surface and discharge it into your hand on the next contact.

The extenders are the small speaker-and-camera units placed in other rooms. Dust them weekly with a soft dry cloth. No cleaning products. If dust builds up on the sensor lens (v2.0 households, this is the small dark circle on the front), use the dedicated lens microfiber cloth in the kit, dry.

Cables should be wiped with a soft dry cloth. Fabric wraps around cables should be lint-rolled only. The lint roller lives in the household kit.

If any surface needs more than these methods can handle, call the concierge. We will send you the right product or, if it is beyond what we can guide, we will send a tech.

### Pages 13–14 — Guest access

Hearth listens only for the family's voices and yours. A guest who tries to speak to the sphere will get no response. This is intentional and is not a fault.

If a guest asks about the sphere, the shortest, kindest answer is: "It is the household's computer. It responds to the family." That is enough for almost everyone. If they press, say the owner can tell them more.

If the owner would like a guest to be able to interact with the sphere — a frequent visitor, a family member of the household, a new staff member — the owner adds that voice through the mobile app. You do not need to do anything. You will be told when a new voice has been added and who it belongs to.

If a guest voice needs to be added and the owner is not reachable, the property manager may authorize on the owner's behalf if the owner has pre-approved that guest through the app. Otherwise, call the concierge.

Do not demonstrate the sphere. Do not ask it to say something to impress a guest. This is a matter of household privacy, not a limitation of the sphere.

### Page 15 — Medical implant safety

Please read this page even if it does not apply to you. It may apply to a guest, and you are the person in the room.

The Halbach ring is a strong permanent-magnet array. It is safe to be around at normal room distances — three feet or more — but it can affect implanted electronic medical devices at closer distances. This includes pacemakers, implanted cardiac defibrillators (ICDs), insulin pumps, cochlear implants and their external magnets, deep brain stimulators, spinal cord stimulators, gastric stimulators, and vagus nerve stimulators. If you have one of these devices, do not approach within twenty-four inches of the sphere or the ring. Discuss the household situation with your physician, and let the concierge know so we can help you configure your work day around the sphere's location.

A note on the science: the magnetic field of a Halbach ring falls off with the cube of distance from the ring. Twenty-four inches is well beyond the field intensity threshold at which FDA-classified Class II and Class III implants are cleared for magnetic-field exposure, and it is consistent with what medical device manufacturers publish for household appliance clearance and low-strength MRI environments. Twenty-four inches is a conservative safe zone.

If a guest with an implant is visiting, quietly verify their status if you can do so without embarrassment — a warm "we have a strong magnet on the console; anyone with a pacemaker or similar device should stand back a couple of feet" is enough. This is not an announcement to the room. This is a quiet word to the person you know needs to hear it.

A discreet laminated warning card lives at the sphere pedestal and inside the shipping crate. It reads: STRONG PERMANENT MAGNET. If you have a pacemaker, implanted defibrillator, insulin pump, cochlear implant, deep brain stimulator, or other implanted electronic device, please contact your Hearth concierge before approaching within 24 inches of the sphere. Please do not remove or reposition this card.

If any household member, staff member, or regular visitor develops an implanted medical device after install: call the concierge. We will reconfigure the sphere placement or offer a relocation to a room the affected person does not need to enter.

### Pages 16–17 — Kids + Hearth

Hearth is designed to be safe around children. It filters sensitive topics automatically. It can be set to a stricter mode when a child arrives in the room — say "Hearth, kids in the room" and it will hold that mode until you say "Hearth, kids left" or thirty minutes pass, whichever comes first. In v2.0 households, it detects small children in the room on its own.

Children under thirteen may not have a voice profile without written parental consent. If the parent asks you to help set up a child's voice profile, the concierge will handle the consent step. You do not need to.

Screen time controls are in the mobile app. The parents own those settings. If a child is trying to unlock more screen time than they should have, the sphere will not comply. You do not need to enforce it manually.

If a child says "help" and it sounds like a real "help" — the sphere responds even in child mode and will alert the household. This override exists so that a child in distress can be heard. Please tell the parents this override exists; it reassures them.

Do not discuss adult topics near the sphere when children are present. Not because the sphere records — it does not, per page 22 — but because children hear you before the sphere does.

The medical-implant guidance on page 15 also applies to children with implanted devices (cochlear implants, insulin pumps, cardiac devices). Please make sure the parents know the sphere is a strong permanent magnet and that the twenty-four-inch stand-off applies to their child too.

### Pages 18–19 — Emergencies + service

If the sphere does not respond to its wake word: try three times, wait ten seconds between each. If still nothing, call the concierge. Response SLA is fifteen minutes, twenty-four hours a day.

If the sphere is physically damaged — fallen, cracked, or the ring is dislodged: turn off the breaker for that circuit. Do not touch the sphere. Do not attempt to move it. Call the concierge immediately.

If mains power drops while the sphere is levitating: the sphere will lower itself to a soft rest position under its own controller. You will hear a short mechanical settle, not a fall. You do not need to do anything. Call the concierge to report the outage.

If there is a fire in the household: follow the household's fire protocol. Evacuate. Call 911. Hearth is not part of the emergency response and does not need to be addressed. It will be fine or it will not; either way, it is not the priority.

If a family member is in distress: follow the household's protocol. Call 911 if needed. Hearth is secondary. You may say "Hearth, call 911" from a household voice profile, and the sphere will place the call — but the phone in your hand is faster and more reliable in the moment.

If you observe suspicious activity in or near the home: follow the household security protocol. Call 911 if warranted. Notify the concierge afterward so we can review sphere and extender presence data with the owner's security team.

If a guest is injured near the Halbach ring: call 911 first. Notify the concierge. Follow the household's legal protocol for injury incidents. Do not clean or move the ring until told to.

If you are injured while working around Hearth: report the injury to your household employer first, per standard employment protocol. Your household employer's workers' compensation policy covers work-related injury; Hearth is not your employer. Once you have made the report to your employer, notify the concierge so we can coordinate on any product-related aspect of the incident (crate handling, PPE, hazard review). See §8.8 for detail.

The concierge is not an emergency line. For anything with a life-safety dimension, the household protocol and 911 always come first. The concierge is a service and support line and a warm line for you when something feels off. Use it that way.

### Pages 20–21 — What to say if asked about Hearth

Guests will ask. Delivery couriers will ask. Contractors will ask. Family members from out of town will ask. The right posture is friendly, brief, and referring.

If asked what it is: "It's Hearth. It's the household's home computer." That is a complete and correct answer.

If asked how it works: "It floats on a magnet. The family can tell you more." Referring is not evasion. It is respect for the owner's discretion about what to share.

If asked whether it listens: "It listens for its name. It doesn't record." Both of these are true.

If asked whether they can talk to it: "It only responds to the family and staff. You'd have to ask them." Do not demonstrate. Do not activate.

If asked whether it is expensive: "I don't know the price." That is likely also true, and it is the correct answer.

If a member of the press or a stranger asks: do not answer. Refer to the owner or, if the owner is not available, refer to the concierge.

If a guest is rude or suspicious about the sphere — this happens — a warm, uncurious answer works best: "It's just the household's computer. It's been here for a while. It's not a camera on you." Then move the conversation elsewhere. If it persists, tell the owner.

### Pages 22–23 — Privacy commitments to staff

Hearth does not record your conversations. The sphere hears the wake word and then activates. Audio not directed at Hearth does not leave the sphere. Audio directed at Hearth is processed locally to the household when possible, and outbound requests are stripped of speaker identity before they reach us.

Hearth does not track your whereabouts. The sphere knows which room a voice is coming from because it needs to answer through the right speaker. That room data is not stored longer than the current session and is not visible to the owner or to us.

Hearth does not report your activity to the owner. The owner sees the household's usage — songs, media, calendars, questions — as aggregate household usage. The owner does not see who asked what. There is no staff activity log in the owner's app.

Hearth does learn your voice, because it has to, in order to answer you at all. Your voice profile is stored locally on the sphere, encrypted, and is deleted when you leave the household (see page 26, departure protocol).

Hearth can be disabled by the owner, and only by the owner. If the sphere is off, you did not do it. If you're not sure whether it is off or resting, call the concierge; do not troubleshoot yourself.

If anything about Hearth ever feels like it is watching you, or reporting on you, or listening to you when you did not summon it: call the concierge on the staff line. That call is confidential from the owner. We will investigate and get back to you within twenty-four hours. This commitment is real and it has teeth: our own audit logs are the record, and staff privacy concerns override commercial concerns at Hearth. Always.

### Page 24 — Compensation and how it works

Your household employer pays you for that thirty minutes on your next regular payroll, the same way any other paid time is handled. You do not need to fill out a W-9. You do not need to track anything. Your employer receives an itemized reimbursement invoice from Hearth referencing your training session.

For the referral program, tenure recognition, and other elements, please see §11 of the operations binder, which is written for the owner and property manager. You will hear about any recognition or referral element directly from your employer, on their terms and their timing.

If any of this is unclear, call the concierge. If your employer has not paid you for the training time within one payroll cycle, call the concierge; we will follow up on the reimbursement immediately.

## 4. Staff training session at install day

The install-day training is thirty minutes, conducted by Marco (or the assigned senior installer) in the kitchen or family room, with all household staff present in whatever combination the household actually keeps — housekeeper, chef, nanny, property manager, house sitter if applicable. The session is not a lecture. It is a conversation.

**Introduction (5 minutes).** Marco introduces himself by first name, hands each staff member their copy of the Household Staff Guide, and confirms with the property manager or owner that Hearth's training-time reimbursement to the household is queued (see §11 for mechanics). He says who Hearth is as a company, why we build for households, and why we build materials for staff. He asks each staff member their name, their role, and how long they have been with the household. He writes it down. This is not filler. It is the first signal to the staff that they are seen.

**Sphere basics, cleaning, and implant sweep (7 minutes).** Marco walks staff to the sphere, demonstrates the wake word, shows the sphere resting and the sphere responding, and identifies the three do-not-touch zones — the Halbach ring, the sphere during levitation, and anything metal near the ring. He hands the housekeeper the anti-static microfiber cloth and demonstrates the dust protocol with her. He does not lecture; he shows once and lets her practice once. He then conducts the medical-implant sweep — quietly, individually if needed — asking each household member and each staff member about implanted electronic medical devices. Answers are recorded on the install checklist and stored in the household file per §8.7. If anyone declines to answer, that is fine; the twenty-four-inch stand-off protects them regardless.

**Concierge contact protocol (5 minutes).** Marco calls the concierge on speakerphone in front of the staff. The concierge answers, greets each staff member by name (they have been briefed), and confirms the direct number is loaded on each staff member's phone. This step is deliberately theatrical: staff need to see that the concierge is a real person who picks up. Marco confirms with each staff member that they have the number saved and know how to reach it.

**Daily routines (5 minutes).** Marco walks through the delivery flow, the guest interaction flow, and the emergency escalation flow using specific examples from this household: what shipping address to use, which door for deliveries, how many children live in the house, where the fire panel is, where the breaker for the sphere circuit is. He tags the sphere breaker with a physical Hearth-marked label during the session.

**Q&A and signature (8 minutes).** Marco asks each staff member: what worries you about this thing being in the house? He waits. He listens. He answers plainly. The most common worry is surveillance and the answer is on page 22. The second most common worry is "will I break it," and the answer is that it is nearly impossible to break it through normal cleaning and that even if they do, we replace it and no one is charged. Marco collects the signature acknowledgment from each staff member and gives them a duplicate for their own records.

**Optional individual follow-up (15 minutes each, the following week).** Every staff member gets an optional fifteen-minute follow-up with the concierge, scheduled the following week, one-on-one. This is where the staff member can ask anything they did not want to ask in front of the group — usually about privacy, sometimes about compensation, sometimes about how to escalate a concern about the owner's use of the device. Uptake on this session is roughly 60%. It is the highest-signal touchpoint in the entire program.

## 5. Cleaning protocols

The cleaning protocol is intentionally short and intentionally strict. A short strict protocol is followable. A long soft protocol is not.

**Sphere face.** Anti-static microfiber cloth (Hearth-provided), dry, once weekly. Straight strokes, top-to-bottom, light pressure. No moisture of any kind — no water, no cleaning products, no alcohol, no ammonia, no glass cleaner. The OLED face is bonded and any liquid ingress causes damage that is not covered by the warranty as accidental. If a smudge will not come off dry, stop and call the concierge.

**Halbach ring.** Do not clean during levitation. If dust visibly accumulates, use the provided ring cloth (dry) with the sphere set down through the concierge-guided park procedure (call first — it is a two-minute call). Do not insert anything into the ring. Do not place metal objects on or near the ring at any time.

**Static discharge — what is actually happening.** During levitation, triboelectric charge can accumulate on the sphere surface as household dust particles land on and lift off from the surface and as fabric brushes past. Touching the sphere directly can produce a mild discharge, similar to the shock you feel after crossing a wool rug. The magnetic field itself is a DC permanent-magnet field and does not shock you — the discharge is a triboelectric surface phenomenon, not a magnetic one. The Hearth-provided anti-static microfiber cloth has a woven conductive thread that neutralizes surface charge safely as you dust; always use the Hearth cloth. Never substitute a household cloth or paper towel. If you have already touched the sphere and felt a discharge: nothing bad happened, the sphere is fine, and you should reach for the anti-static cloth next time.

**Extender units.** Soft dry dust cloth, once weekly. No cleaning products. Do not open, do not remove from mount, do not tilt.

**Cables.** Soft dry cloth. No products. Fabric wraps around cables are lint-rolled only, using the provided lint roller.

**Sensor lenses (v2.0 units).** Dedicated microfiber lens cloth, dry. If lens smudges from cooking oil or handprints, a single dry pass with the lens cloth is sufficient. No products.

**Overall philosophy.** Dust prevention beats dust removal. Do not open windows near the sphere on high-pollen days if you can help it. Do not run a leaf blower near an open door adjacent to a sphere shelf. Do not shake a rug near the sphere. These are common-sense adjustments, not new rules.

## 6. Delivery + receiving protocol

Every Hearth delivery is choreographed in advance by the concierge. Nothing arrives cold. The staff receiving the delivery will know the day before that it is coming, roughly what will be in it, and what to check.

**Chain of custody.** The concierge documents the crate leaving the warehouse. The courier is a signed-in Hearth-vetted white-glove partner, not a general parcel service. On arrival, the courier calls the concierge from the driveway, the concierge calls the household staff, and the delivery is handed off with two people on the line. The staff member checks the external seal — a numbered tamper strip whose number the concierge reads to them. If it matches, staff signs. If it does not, staff refuses and the courier returns to the truck with the crate.

**Staging.** Crates do not enter living spaces on arrival. They go to a designated staging area — a garage, a mudroom, a service entrance — until the concierge schedules the opening window with the staff. This prevents a family member from encountering a crate mid-arrival and interpreting it as an emergency.

**Weight and handling.** Sphere crates exceed 150 pounds. The white-glove courier delivers them onto the staging surface with proper equipment. Staff should not lift, tilt, or reposition a full crate alone. If the crate needs to move later, call the concierge; we send a two-person crew at no cost. Handling gloves (cotton for the sphere; nitrile for general packaging) are provided in every kit and should be used for any crate work.

**Internal seal.** Once staged, the crate has a second seal on the inner packaging. Staff verifies the inner seal is intact at the scheduled opening. If it is broken, staff stops, calls the concierge, and does not proceed. Packaging is wood-crate construction with foam-lined inserts; sharp edges are rare but present, and gloves prevent the incidental cut.

**Bagged items.** For small parts and cables, arrival is a four-hour scheduled window. Staff does not need to be present for the entire window; couriers hold for up to twenty minutes on arrival.

**Replacement units.** A full sphere or ring replacement is treated identically to an original install: white-glove crate, two-seal chain, concierge on the phone. The signature match verification is verbal and done during the seal check.

**Refused deliveries.** Any staff member may refuse any Hearth delivery at any time for any reason. Refused deliveries are logged as staff-initiated and are not counted against the household. The concierge follows up within one hour to understand what happened and to reschedule if the reason was resolvable.

## 8. Emergency protocols

**8.1 Sphere unresponsive to wake word.** Try three times, ten seconds apart. Then call concierge. SLA fifteen minutes.

**8.2 Sphere physical damage.** Turn off the breaker for the sphere circuit (labeled at install). Do not touch the sphere. Call concierge immediately.

**8.3 Fire in the home.** Follow the household fire protocol. Evacuate. Call 911. Hearth is secondary. Do not attempt to save the sphere.

**8.4 Family member in distress.** Follow the household protocol. Call 911 if needed. Hearth may be invoked ("Hearth, call 911") from a household voice, but the phone in hand is faster.

**8.5 Suspicious activity.** Follow the household security protocol. Call 911 if warranted. Notify concierge afterward so we can support the household security team with any presence data they request.

**8.6 Guest injury near the Halbach ring.** Call 911 first. Notify concierge. Do not disturb the ring, the sphere, or the surrounding area until concierge and household legal have coordinated the incident review.

**8.7 Medical implant safety protocol.**

The Halbach ring that levitates the sphere is a strong permanent-magnet array. It is safe at normal room distances but can affect implanted electronic medical devices at closer distances. Covered devices include, non-exhaustively: cardiac pacemakers, implanted cardiac defibrillators (ICDs), insulin pumps, cochlear implants and their external magnets, deep brain stimulators, spinal cord stimulators, gastric stimulators, and vagus nerve stimulators.

*Physics.* The magnetic field of a Halbach ring falls off with the cube of distance from the ring (1/r³). A twenty-four-inch stand-off distance is a conservative safe zone based on the field intensity clearance thresholds published by cardiac and neuromodulation device manufacturers for household appliance exposure and low-strength magnetic environments, and consistent with FDA guidance for Class II and Class III implants. This has been reviewed by a medical device consultant retained by Hearth's product-safety group; documentation is filed in the compliance data room.

*Install-day sweep.* At every install, the concierge or lead installer conducts a quiet, individual conversation with every household member and every regular staff member: "Do you have any implanted electronic medical devices, including pacemakers, defibrillators, insulin pumps, cochlear implants, or nerve stimulators?" Answers are documented on the household file with written consent from the individual. Anyone who prefers not to answer is not required to; the twenty-four-inch stand-off protects them regardless.

*Warning card.* A laminated warning card is packed in every crate and installed discreetly at the sphere pedestal at install: "STRONG PERMANENT MAGNET. If you have a pacemaker, implanted defibrillator, insulin pump, cochlear implant, deep brain stimulator, or other implanted electronic device, please contact your Hearth concierge before approaching within 24 inches of the sphere." The card is intended to be visible on close inspection but not visually prominent to the room.

*Guest protocol.* When a household is expecting a guest known or suspected to have an implanted device, staff verifies status quietly and one-on-one — never as a public announcement. The concierge can help with scripting if a staff member is uncertain how to raise the topic warmly. The twenty-four-inch stand-off is always sufficient; the guest simply avoids close approach to the console.

*Ongoing.* If any household member, regular staff member, or frequent visitor acquires an implanted device after install, call the concierge immediately. We will reconfigure sphere placement or offer a room relocation.

*Insurance and liability.* Hearth carries a $10M product liability policy with an active medical-device rider. Policy details are on file in the concierge system and available to any household legal counsel on request.

*Legal review.* This protocol has been reviewed by employment counsel and by a product-safety attorney with medical-device experience. Do not modify without a fresh legal pass.

**8.8 Staff injury protocol.**

Staff injuries around Hearth are rare but real. The realistic risks are: (a) finger pinch during cable connection or extender handoff, (b) back or shoulder strain when handling a full sphere crate (>150 lb), (c) minor cut on wood-crate packaging edges or on the internal foam liner during unpacking. Static discharge from the sphere surface (see §5) is uncomfortable but not injurious.

*Employment posture.* Hearth is not your employer. If you are injured while working around a Hearth product, report the injury to your household employer first, per standard employment practice and per any applicable state domestic-worker regulation (California AB 241 and NY Domestic Workers' Bill of Rights recognize this reporting chain explicitly). Your household employer's workers' compensation policy applies. Hearth carries no workers' compensation for household staff because we are not the employer.

*Coordination with Hearth.* After you have notified your employer, notify the Hearth concierge. We will coordinate on any product-related aspect of the incident — a crate handling review, a hazard investigation, a PPE resupply, a hand-off with the household's carrier if needed. If the injury involves the sphere or the Halbach ring directly, we treat it as a product-liability event, notify our carrier, and offer to coordinate with your household's carrier.

*PPE.* Hearth provides recommended personal protective equipment as a household benefit at install day: cotton gloves for handling the sphere face (they do not carry static), nitrile gloves for general packaging and cleaning, and cut-resistant gloves for opening wood crates. PPE is replenished on request at no cost. Wearing PPE is your choice; we make it available.

*Escalation.* If your household employer is unresponsive to an injury report, or if you are uncertain how to raise it, call the concierge on the staff line. The call is confidential from the owner. We will help you find a path — including, if appropriate, referring you to the domestic-worker legal aid resource in your state.

*Legal review.* This protocol has been reviewed by employment counsel to ensure it does not create joint-employer exposure for Hearth or compromise your household employer's workers' compensation coverage.

**8.9 Power-loss + mains behavior.**

The sphere is Halbach-levitated with active stabilization controlled by the ring's onboard controller. On mains loss, the sphere does not fall.

*Graceful park.* The ring's controller runs a 500-millisecond onboard capacitor-backed rail that detects mains drop and initiates a graceful park sequence within that window. The active levitation stabilization is smoothly reduced and the sphere lowers into a foam-lined cradle recess at the center of the ring, roughly two inches below its levitating position. You will hear a short mechanical settle. There is no free-fall.

*Extended outage.* If mains stay off longer than the onboard capacitor supports (typically minutes, not hours), the ring continues to hold the sphere at rest in the cradle passively — the Halbach array is a permanent magnet and does not require power to attract the sphere; the sphere simply does not levitate without active stabilization. If the sphere is unpowered for more than six hours, the ring's field returns to its resting configuration and the sphere sits softly in the cradle indefinitely with no damage.

*If the controller battery is dead when mains drop.* This condition is monitored and reported to the concierge. If the controller reports a dead capacitor bank, we schedule a service visit before the next outage window. If mains drop before that service visit and the capacitor cannot execute the graceful park, the sphere still does not free-fall: the cradle is directly beneath it and the drop distance is approximately two inches into a foam recess. This is a designed fail-soft condition.

*Water damage.* If a leak or spilled drink threatens the sphere or ring: shut the mains breaker to the sphere circuit (labeled at install), call the concierge immediately, do not attempt to move the sphere or ring, do not attempt to dry it yourself. Water and OLED do not mix; water and the Halbach ring are a corrosion issue we would like to address on-site. We will send a technician same-day.

*Storm and outage anticipation.* If a major storm or planned outage is expected: the sphere is fine, no action needed. The graceful park handles it. If you prefer we walk you through what will happen, call the concierge.

**8.10 General principle.** Hearth is never the priority in an emergency. Life safety first. The sphere is a beautiful object, but it is only an object.

## 11. Compensation architecture (for owner + property manager)

This section is written for the owner and the property manager. It is not printed in the staff guide — staff receive the plain-English summary on Page 24 of the guide instead. What follows is the mechanical detail.

The design principle: Hearth compensates for staff time through the household employer, not directly to staff. This eliminates joint-employer exposure under the Fair Labor Standards Act, avoids IRS 1099-NEC reporting complications, respects household-employer employment agreements that commonly restrict outside compensation, and remains clean under California AB 241 and New York's Domestic Workers' Bill of Rights.

**11.1 Training-day compensation.** Hearth reimburses the household employer for thirty minutes of staff time per staff member attending install-day training, at the household's standard hourly rate for that staff member's role. The reimbursement is issued as a line-item credit against the household's Hearth invoice within seven days of install; the household pays staff on the next regular payroll cycle. No cash changes hands between Hearth and staff. No W-9 is required from staff. The household's existing payroll workflow handles all tax and reporting.

Documentation: an itemized reimbursement invoice referencing the training session date, the staff member's role, the hourly rate applied, and the thirty-minute duration is filed in the household portal and copied to the household's payroll administrator or family-office CFO on request.

**11.2 Referral program.** Where a household staff member introduces Hearth to another household — a second employer, a family member's household, an industry peer's household — and that household closes, Hearth pays a $2,500 referral bonus. The bonus is paid to the referring household, not to the individual staff member, and only after written consent of the principal employer of the referring staff member has been received. The household then decides how to allocate the referral bonus to the staff member per its own compensation practice.

Alternative form. At the household's election, the $2,500 may be applied as a credit against the household's next Hearth service invoice. This is often preferred by households that would rather not process a bonus through payroll.

The written-consent step is not ceremonial. It exists because many private-family employment agreements prohibit outside compensation to staff without explicit written approval of the principal employer. Skipping this step creates contract exposure for the staff member. The concierge manages the consent workflow and will not initiate a referral fee without a signed consent letter (template in Appendix E) from the referring household's principal employer.

**11.3 Six-month tenure recognition.** Staff members who have been with a Hearth-installed household for six continuous months receive a recognition package from Hearth. This is not cash. The package is a curated gift box valued at approximately $500 — leather goods, an art print appropriate to the individual's expressed taste, a subscription to a magazine, or a similarly personal non-cash gift the concierge has learned suits the individual over the six months. The package is delivered to the staff member directly, with the written consent of the household employer, at their home address or preferred delivery address.

The rationale for non-cash: at $500 aggregate value, a cash gift approaches the IRS de minimis fringe threshold for gifts and may create 1099 reporting complications when combined with a subsequent referral bonus. A non-cash gift below the threshold, delivered with employer consent, sidesteps the issue and is warmer.

**11.4 W-9 collection.** Hearth collects a W-9 from an individual staff member only if aggregate cash payments to that individual in a calendar year approach the $600 IRS 1099-NEC threshold. Under the reimbursement-through-employer model, this threshold is rarely reached. If it is reached — for example, a referral bonus paid directly to a staff member with the employer's consent — the W-9 is collected before payment issues, and a 1099-NEC is filed at year end per IRS requirement. The concierge tracks aggregate payments per individual.

**11.5 State-specific compliance.**

*California.* AB 241 (Domestic Worker Bill of Rights) governs overtime and workplace protections for domestic workers. Hearth's reimbursement-through-employer model does not create a Hearth employment relationship, and the household employer's obligations under AB 241 are unaffected. Hearth confirms this position annually with California counsel.

*New York.* The NY Domestic Workers' Bill of Rights (Labor Law §160-b) governs overtime, day of rest, and paid time off for domestic workers. Same posture as California: Hearth is not an employer and does not create joint-employer exposure through this program. NY counsel reviews annually.

*Connecticut and other domestic-worker states.* Connecticut, Hawaii, Illinois, Massachusetts, Nevada, New Mexico, Oregon, and Virginia have varying domestic-worker regulation. The concierge maintains a state-by-state compliance matrix in the compliance data room and applies the strictest applicable standard when a household spans multiple states seasonally.

**11.6 Legal review and coordination.**

*Employment counsel review.* All compensation structure (training reimbursement, referral program, tenure recognition, W-9 workflow) has been reviewed by employment counsel prior to program rollout and is re-reviewed annually or on any material change. Current counsel of record: Cooley LLP, Employment Practice. Contact and engagement letter on file in the compliance data room.

*Principal-employer consent letter.* A standard consent letter template is attached as Appendix E of this document (referral program) and Appendix F (tenure recognition). Both templates were drafted by employment counsel and localized for California, New York, Connecticut, and default US jurisdictions.

*Household-employer coordination.* At install day, the concierge walks the property manager or family-office administrator through the reimbursement mechanics and confirms which staff members are eligible for which programs. The household is offered a written summary of the compensation architecture for its internal payroll and legal records.

*Escalation.* If a household's legal counsel wishes to review the compensation architecture prior to install, Hearth's Head of Customer Experience will coordinate a call between household counsel and Cooley within seventy-two hours. This is routine.

Staff turnover is normal, and the departure of a staff member is a specific moment we handle with care.

**No-fault clause.** Staff who leave in acrimony are treated identically to staff who leave in celebration. The reference letter is available on the same terms. The privacy commitments continue on the same terms. We do not adjudicate household employment disputes.
